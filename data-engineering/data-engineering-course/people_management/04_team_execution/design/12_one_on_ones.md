# 12 — Running Effective 1:1s

> **Lesson 12 of 21 — Managing Team Execution** · ~20 min

The 1:1 doc, frequency, what to talk about, and the 5-section
structure for every 1:1. The 1:1 is the substrate for the
coaching, career, and performance work in Modules 02 and 03.
If your 1:1s are good, the rest of the job is easier than it
looks. If your 1:1s are bad, nothing else compensates.

---

## 1. The 1:1 is the engineer's meeting, not yours

The single most important thing about 1:1s: **they are the
direct report's meeting, not the manager's**. The agenda is
theirs. The tone is theirs. The outcomes are theirs.

Most new EMs treat the 1:1 as a status update — the engineer
reports on what they shipped, the EM reacts, the meeting ends.
That's a status meeting, not a 1:1. The senior move is to
make the 1:1 a space where the engineer can bring *anything*
they need to bring — work problems, career questions, personal
stress, ideas they haven't shared publicly — and get a
generous, patient, focused response.

The 1:1 is also the **highest-leverage coaching surface in
the EM job**. Every lesson in Modules 02 and 03 (coaching,
career, goals, performance) happens in 1:1s. The 1:1 is
where you do the work.

---

## 2. The 5-section 1:1 doc

The 1:1 doc is a shared document (Google Doc, Notion, etc.)
that both the EM and the direct contribute to. The format I
recommend:

```
1:1 — [Name] — [Date]

UPDATES FROM [DIRECT]:
- [What's on their mind this week — work, life, ideas]
- [What's blocking them]
- [What's going well]

UPDATES FROM [EM]:
- [What the EM is working on that affects the direct]
- [Org / company context the direct should know]
- [Feedback or signal to share]

COACHING TOPIC:
- [The thing we're working on together — see Lesson 03]

CAREER:
- [The longer-arc question — see Lesson 04]

ACTION ITEMS:
- [What we'll each do by next 1:1]
```

The "coaching topic" and "career" sections are the load-
bearing parts. They make the 1:1 a *coaching* meeting, not
a status meeting. The senior move is to **keep the coaching
topic active across multiple 1:1s** — if the topic is
"cross-functional communication," it stays on the doc for 4-6
weeks until the engineer has demonstrably improved.

The "action items" section is the second most load-bearing
part. Without action items, the 1:1 is venting. With them,
the 1:1 produces outcomes. The senior move is to **start the
next 1:1 by reading the action items from the last 1:1**.

## 3. Frequency and length

| Engineer level | 1:1 frequency | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| New joiner (first 3 months) | Weekly | 30-45 min | Coaching-heavy. Lots of context. |
| E3/E4 | Weekly or biweekly | 30 min | Coaching + career |
| E5/E6 | Biweekly | 30 min | Career + projects. Coaching is more peer-like. |
| E7+ | Biweekly or monthly | 30-45 min | More peer-like. Career + strategic context. |
| Engineer on a PIP | Weekly | 45 min | Performance-focused, with the action items from the PIP doc. |
| Skip-level (you are the skip) | Monthly | 30-45 min | Career-focused, not project-focused. |

The mistake new EMs make: defaulting to 30 minutes weekly
for everyone. The senior move is to **match the cadence to
the need**, not the calendar.

For new joiners, weekly is the right cadence — they're
learning the team, the codebase, the rhythm, and they need
more access. For senior engineers, biweekly is usually
right — they're more independent, and the 1:1 is for
peer-level discussion. For engineers on a PIP, weekly is
non-negotiable — the action items need to be tracked
closely.

The other mistake: skipping 1:1s when things are busy. The
busier the team, the more important the 1:1s. Skipping them
because "we're in launch mode" is the move that loses
engineers in launch mode.

---

## 4. What to talk about

The 1:1 has 5 buckets of content, and most EMs cover 2-3
of them per meeting. The senior move is to **cover all 5
across a month's worth of 1:1s** so nothing falls off.

1. **Work** — projects, blockers, dependencies. The
   operational stuff.
2. **Career** — promotion trajectory, growth areas, what
   the engineer wants to be doing in 12-24 months.
3. **Coaching** — the specific growth area we're working on
   (e.g., "design-doc reviews," "executive communication,"
   "running effective meetings").
4. **Team / org** — context the engineer needs that they
   won't get from public channels.
5. **Personal** — what's going on in their life that affects
   their work. The senior move is to leave 5-10 minutes of
   the 1:1 for this, even if you never go there. The
   engineer who needs it will use it.

The mistake new EMs make: living in bucket 1 (work) and
skipping buckets 2-5. The 1:1 becomes a status meeting,
the engineer feels managed instead of grown, and they
stop bringing the personal / career stuff because "Sam
only wants to talk about work."

---

## 5. The 5 questions that make every 1:1 better

When the 1:1 starts to feel stale or transactional, these 5
questions reset it:

1. **"What's on your mind this week that you haven't had
   the chance to talk about?"** — surfaces the thing the
   direct hasn't been able to raise in public channels.
2. **"What would make next week better?"** — focuses the
   conversation on the future, not the past.
3. **"What am I doing that's getting in your way?"** — the
   senior move. The engineer almost never volunteers this,
   and the answer is usually specific and actionable.
4. **"What's a question you have for me that you haven't
   asked?"** — the engineer often has questions about the
   org, the strategy, the manager's job, that they don't
   know how to raise.
5. **"What should we be doing as a team that we
   aren't?"** — the senior move for surfacing ideas the
   direct is too junior or too cautious to raise in the
   staff meeting.

The 5 questions together take 10-15 minutes. They make the
1:1 a space the engineer looks forward to instead of
tolerates.

## 6. A worked example: the 1:1 doc for Priya

```
1:1 — Priya — 2026-08-12

UPDATES FROM PRIYA:
- Migration v1 design review went well on Friday, 2 senior
  engineers signed off, 1 raised concerns about the
  on-call handoff — she's following up.
- Blocked on the new Kafka cluster from infra; it's 4 days
  late.
- Personal: her partner is starting a new job next month
  and they'll be moving — she's anxious about the
  transition and asked for flexibility in late September.

UPDATES FROM SAM:
- The director approved the Q4 platform planning doc; the
  themes are "reliability" and "ML features."
- I'll be out next Wednesday for a skip-level offsite.
- Org context: there's a rumor about a reorg in the data
  org. It's premature, I don't have details, but I want
  you to hear it from me first.

COACHING TOPIC:
- Continuing the cross-functional communication work from
  the past 4 weeks. This week: the schema design review
  with the data science team on Friday. Specifically: how
  she handled the "I'd like to see the data before we
  commit" pushback. (She handled it well — used a
  question, not a statement. We'll celebrate that.)

CAREER:
- The E6 trajectory doc. She wants to write the first
  draft by end of month. We'll co-write the first 2
  sections in next week's 1:1.

ACTION ITEMS:
- [Sam] Loop in the infra EM about the Kafka cluster
  delay.
- [Sam] Send Priya the reorg context in writing.
- [Priya] Send the on-call handoff follow-up to the
  senior engineer by Friday.
- [Priya] E6 trajectory doc first draft by Aug 31.
```

**What makes this land:** The 5 buckets are all represented
(work, career, coaching, org, personal). The action items
have owners and dates. The coaching topic is a continuation
of a multi-week thread. The personal bucket is acknowledged
without being intrusive.

## Try it

For each of your directs, look at your last 3 1:1 docs.
Check: did you cover all 5 buckets (work, career, coaching,
team/org, personal)? Did you have action items that you
followed up on? If not, the next 3 1:1s are an opportunity
to restart the rhythm.

## Action item

This week, try the "what am I doing that's getting in your
way?" question in one 1:1. The answer is almost always
specific, often surprising, and almost always useful. The
worst-case outcome is the engineer says "nothing" and you've
signaled that the question is on the table.
