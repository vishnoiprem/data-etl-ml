# Capstone Exercise — Write 3 Retrospectives

> **Capstone for the project_retrospective module**
> · ~6-8 hours of focused work

Produce 3 fully-written, 5-minute retrospectives on
real projects in your career — and rehearse each
in the 5/3/90-second versions. This is the
artifact. Everything else in the module is in
service of producing these 3 retrospectives.

---

## What you're shipping

By the end of this exercise you should have:

1. **A candidate list** — 10-15 PR topics from
   your career, tagged by category and quality
2. **3 written retrospectives** — each in the
   5-minute, 5-beat format from Lesson 03
3. **A pitfall audit** — each retrospective passes
   the 8-pitfall checklist from Lesson 06
4. **3 rehearsed versions** — each retrospective
   has 5/3/90-second spoken versions, recorded

That's the deliverable. The work happens in 4
phases.

---

## Phase 1: Mine (2-3 hours)

Use the framework from Lesson 02.

Open a blank document. Set a 60-minute timer. Do
Pass 1 (the brain dump). List every project you
can remember, with a number if you have one.

When you have 30-50 items, take a 10-minute break.

Do Pass 2 (categorize and tag) on the same day if
you can. Tag each candidate by category
(re-platform / launch / incident / cross-team /
strategy), senior level, number (yes/no), and
quality (1-5).

Then do Pass 3 (pick the top 3). Pick 3 candidates
that, between them, cover 3 different categories,
have numbers, and have honest failure beats.

**Output:** 3 chosen projects, each with a 1-line
description, a decision, an outcome, a lesson, and
a "what I'd do differently" beat.

---

## Phase 2: Write (2-3 hours)

For each of the 3 chosen projects, write a
5-minute retrospective in the 5-beat structure from
Lesson 03.

**The structure (recap):**

- **Context** (30-45s) — situation, constraint,
  baseline number
- **Approach** (60-90s) — what you did, why,
  judgment call, team
- **Outcome** (60-90s) — result, multiple numbers,
  the surprise
- **Lessons** (45-60s) — 2-3 transferable
  insights with behavior changes
- **What I'd Do Differently** (30-45s) — 2-3
  specific changes with cost estimates

Aim for 4.5-5.5 minutes per retrospective when
read aloud at 150 wpm.

**Score each one against the pitfall checklist
from Lesson 06:**

- [ ] "I" appears at least 5-7 times
- [ ] At least 4 specific numbers
- [ ] No "they" without "we" reframing
- [ ] At least one negative surprise
- [ ] "What I'd Do Differently" has 2-3 specific
      changes
- [ ] Specific tools, schemas, failure modes named
- [ ] Every lesson has a behavior change
- [ ] Total time is 4.5-5.5 minutes

If any box is unchecked, rewrite that beat. The
checklist is the bar.

---

## Phase 3: Rehearse (1-2 hours)

For each retrospective, rehearse 3 versions:
5-minute, 3-minute, and 90-second.

**The 5-minute version is your default.** Time
yourself. Aim for 4.5-5.5 minutes.

**The 3-minute version is your tight version.**
Compress the Approach and Outcome beats. Keep the
Context, Lessons, and "What I'd Do Differently"
beats intact. Time yourself. Aim for 2.5-3.5
minutes.

**The 90-second version is your elevator pitch.**
Compress all 5 beats to 5 sentences. Aim for
80-100 seconds.

**Record each version on your phone.** Listen back.
Note every "um", "uh", "like", "you know", and
"kind of" you hear. Note every long pause. Note
every trail-off. Rewrite from the transcription if
the spoken version is meaningfully different
from the written version.

Do this 2-3 times for each retrospective. By the
third take, the filler is gone and the structure
is automatic.

---

## Phase 4: Pitfall audit (30 min)

Take your final 3 retrospectives and run each one
through the 8-pitfall checklist from Lesson 06
one more time. For each pitfall, do the sample
rewrite exercise on the specific beat that trips.

Specifically check for:

- **Pitfall 1 (over-using "we"):** count the "I"s
  in the 5-minute version. Should be 5-7.
- **Pitfall 2 (skipping metrics):** count the
  numbers in the Outcome beat. Should be 4+.
- **Pitfall 3 (blaming others):** search for
  "they", "the team didn't", "X was blocking".
  Replace with reframes.
- **Pitfall 4 (everything went well):** is there
  at least one negative surprise named?
- **Pitfall 5 (skipping "what I'd do differently"):**
  is the beat present with 2+ specific changes?
- **Pitfall 6 (vague technical depth):** are
  specific tools, schemas, failure modes named?
- **Pitfall 7 (generic lessons):** does each
  lesson have a behavior change attached?
- **Pitfall 8 (rambling):** does the 5-minute
  version land in 4.5-5.5 minutes?

If all 8 pitfalls are clean, you're done.

---

## Suggested schedule

- **Day 1 (90 min):** Phase 1 — Mine. Brain dump,
  categorize, pick 3.
- **Day 2 (2-3 hours):** Phase 2 — Write the
  first retrospective. Score it against the
  pitfall checklist. Iterate.
- **Day 3 (2-3 hours):** Phase 2 continued —
  Write the 2nd and 3rd retrospectives. Same
  process.
- **Day 4 (1-2 hours):** Phase 3 — Rehearse all 3
  at 5/3/90 seconds. Record. Listen. Cut.
- **Day 5 (30 min):** Phase 4 — Final pitfall
  audit. Fix any remaining issues.

Total: 6-8 hours over 5 days.

---

## Output: what to share (if working with a friend)

If you have a friend who'll listen, send them your
3 written retrospectives and ask them to:

1. **Read each one and score it** against the
   8-pitfall checklist. Any pitfall they catch is
   a pitfall the interviewer will catch.
2. **Listen to each one** at the 5-minute, 3-minute,
   and 90-second versions. Anything that drags,
   repeats, or trails off is something to fix.
3. **Ask you 2-3 follow-up questions** after each
   retrospective. The questions that surprise you
   are the questions you need to pre-rehearse.

If you don't have a friend, use the recording. It
catches most of the same things.

---

## What "done" looks like

You're done when you can:

- [ ] Tell any of the 3 retrospectives in any of
      the 3 versions (5/3/90 seconds) on demand,
      without notes
- [ ] Pass the 8-pitfall audit on all 3
      retrospectives
- [ ] Identify the senior signal in each
      retrospective (judgment, learning, technical
      depth, self-awareness)
- [ ] Name 2-3 follow-up questions the interviewer
      might ask, and have a rehearsed answer for
      each

That's the deliverable. The retrospective that
passes all 4 is the one you take into the
interview.
