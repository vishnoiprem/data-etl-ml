# 02 — Brainstorming Project Retrospective Ideas

> **Lesson 2 of 6** · ~25 min

The 3-pass mining process for finding 10-15 candidate
PR topics from your career, adapted from the
behavioral track's story-mining workshop. By the end
of this lesson you'll have a candidate list tagged by
category, with at least 3 ready to write up in full.

---

## 1. The setup

You need:

- A blank document (or a spreadsheet)
- 2-3 hours of uninterrupted time
- Your resume (for jogging your memory)
- A timer

The output:

- 30-50 raw project candidates
- 10-15 polished candidates, tagged by category
- 3 final picks, ready to write up in the 5-minute
  PR format from Lesson 03

This is a *project* mining exercise, not a story
mining exercise. The unit of work is a project, not
a moment. A 6-month re-platform counts as one
candidate; a 90-minute debugging session doesn't.

---

## 2. Pass 1: The brain dump (60-90 min)

Set a 60-minute timer. Open a blank document titled
"PROJECT RETROSPECTIVES." Write without stopping
for the full 60 minutes. For every project, role,
or effort that comes to mind, write 1 line:

- 1-line description
- Year(s) and company
- The specific decision I made
- The outcome (with a number if possible)

Don't filter. Don't edit. Don't worry about whether
the project is "big enough" or "senior enough."
The point is raw material. You can curate later.

**If you get stuck after 20 minutes** (which is
common), re-read your resume and use it as a
prompt. For each role listed, ask: *"What was the
biggest project I shipped in this role?"* Write the
first thing that comes to mind, even if it feels
small.

**If you get stuck at the 30-minute mark**, switch
to a *category prompt* approach. For each of the
5 PR categories (re-platforms, launches, incident
response, cross-team migrations, technical strategy
shifts), brainstorm 2-3 projects:

- A re-platform I led
- A launch I shipped
- A production incident I handled
- A cross-team migration I drove
- A technical strategy I changed

If you can't fill one of the categories, that's
information. Most senior engineers have 2-3
projects in 3-4 of the 5 categories, and 0-1 in
the rare one. The PR question is flexible enough
that you can answer most prompts with a project
from any category.

By minute 60, you should have 30-50 items. Most
will be small. Some will be obvious retrospectives.
A few will be hidden gems you hadn't thought about
in years.

---

## 3. Pass 2: Categorize and tag (60-90 min)

Take the brain dump. For each item, tag it with:

| Tag | Values |
|---|---|
| **Category** | re-platform / launch / incident / cross-team / strategy |
| **Senior level** | M3 / M4 / M5 — your gut for what level the project demonstrates |
| **Number** | yes / no — does it have a quantifiable outcome? |
| **Quality** | 1-5 — your gut feel for the project's strength as a PR |

The output is a 2-axis matrix. A worked example for
a hypothetical candidate:

```
                M3     M4     M5
Re-platform     [2]    [2]    [1]
Launch          [3]    [1]    [0]
Incident        [2]    [1]    [0]
Cross-team      [1]    [2]    [1]
Strategy        [1]    [1]    [0]
```

Read the matrix. The cells with 0 or 1 are your
**coverage gaps**. The cells with the most items
are where you'll likely find your strongest 3
retrospectives. You want at least one project from
3 different categories in your final 3 — it gives
you flexibility when the interviewer asks a
specific variant of the question.

The "Number" tag is critical. **A PR without a
number is a 2/4 at best.** Every one of your 3
final picks must have at least one anchoring number
(cost, runtime, latency, error rate, count of
users, count of teams, etc.). If a candidate
project has no number, you have two options: (1)
estimate it (e.g. "we saved roughly 20 hours of
manual work per week"), or (2) drop it.

The "Quality" tag is gut feel. A 5/5 is a project
that has all four PR signals (judgment, learning,
technical depth, self-awareness) AND a number AND
a story arc. A 1/5 is a project that was important
but doesn't tell well.

---

## 4. Pass 3: Pick the top 3 (30-60 min)

Pick the 3 candidates that have:

- The highest quality score
- The best category coverage (ideally from 3
  different categories)
- At least one number each
- The clearest decision (the moment you committed
  to a direction)
- The most honest failure beat (a PR without
  something that went wrong is suspicious)

The format I recommend for your final 3:

```
TITLE: [One-line name]
CATEGORY: [Re-platform / Launch / Incident / Cross-team / Strategy]
THE DECISION: [1 sentence — the call you made]
THE OUTCOME: [1-2 sentences with the number]
THE LESSON: [1 sentence — the takeaway]
THE "WHAT I'D DO DIFFERENTLY": [1 sentence — the
honest beat]
```

**The "What I'd Do Differently" line is the
differentiation.** Most candidates skip it. If you
have it pre-written for all 3 retrospectives,
you're ahead of 80% of the candidate pool.

---

## 5. When to pick a "swing for the fences" project vs a "safe and solid" one

You'll have 3 retrospectives, but the interviewer
will only ask 1 PR question. So you need to
**sequence** your 3 picks: which one is your
"primary," which is your "secondary," which is
your "tertiary."

### Primary: "Swing for the fences"

This is the project that, if it lands, puts you
in the top 10% of the candidate pool. It's the
project that has:

- A senior-signal scope (cross-team, org-wide, or
  with a real cost/revenue impact)
- A genuine judgment call (you had to choose
  between two real options)
- A real failure beat (something went wrong and
  you recovered)
- A real "what I'd do differently" (you have
  specific changes you'd make)

This is the project you lead with when the
interviewer says "tell me about a project you're
proud of" or "walk me through a complex technical
effort." It's your best story.

### Secondary: "Safe and solid"

This is the project that's clearly senior but
doesn't have the dramatic arc of your primary.
It's the project you use when the interviewer
asks a more specific prompt — "tell me about a
migration you led" or "tell me about a launch you
shipped." It should be a real, well-told story,
but it doesn't have to be the magnum opus.

### Tertiary: "Specialist"

This is the project that has a specific signal you
want to demonstrate — a deep technical decision, a
specific kind of judgment call, or a particular
domain expertise. Use it when the interviewer asks
a targeted prompt: "tell me about a project where
you had to debug a complex issue" or "tell me
about a time you changed the technical direction
of your team."

The third project is also your "spare" — the one
you'd use if the interviewer's first PR question
doesn't fit your primary. Always have a spare.

## 6. 15 candidate PR topics from the canonical EM list

Across the 131 community-reported behavioral
questions in
`docs/reference/em_interview_canonical_questions.md`,
the PR questions cluster heavily. Here are 15
candidate PR topics — pick the ones that match
your career:

### Re-platforms (5)

1. **Re-platformed a 4-year-old nightly ETL pipeline
   from a custom Airflow-on-EC2 setup to dbt +
   Snowflake, reducing cost by 60% and runtime
   from 8hr to 45min.** *(the worked example in
   Lesson 05)*
2. Migrated a customer-facing analytics dashboard
   from a legacy BI tool to Looker.
3. Re-platformed a data warehouse from Redshift to
   Snowflake.
4. Migrated a batch ML training pipeline from
   cron-on-VM to Airflow + Kubernetes.
5. Replaced a homegrown feature store with Feast.

### Launches (4)

6. Launched a new self-serve analytics product from
   0 to 1.
7. Shipped a new data ingestion API for partner
   integrations.
8. Launched a real-time alerting product for the
   on-call team.
9. Built and launched a new ML feature store from
   scratch.

### Incidents (3)

10. Diagnosed a 6-hour production outage caused by
    a silent schema-evolution bug.
11. Recovered from a corrupted state in the
    primary OLTP database during a routine
    migration.
12. Handled a 3-day data-quality incident where
    downstream ML models were silently using stale
    data.

### Cross-team (2)

13. Consolidated 4 team-specific data pipelines
    into a single shared platform used by 12 teams.
14. Drove the org-wide migration from per-team
    Airflow instances to a centralized managed
    Airflow service.

### Strategy (1)

15. Led the technical strategy shift from
    monolithic nightly batches to a streaming-first
    architecture across the data org.

For each candidate topic, ask: *"What's the
specific decision I made, and what's the number?"*
If you have an answer, it's a viable PR. If you
don't, drop it.

---

## Try it

Set a 60-minute timer. Do Pass 1 right now. Don't
overthink it — just write for the full 60 minutes.
List every project you can remember, with a number
if you have one.

When you're done, take a 10-minute break, then do
Pass 2 (30 min) and Pass 3 (30 min) on the same
day if you have the energy, or split them across
2 days if you don't.

By the end you'll have 3 retrospective candidates
ready to write up in the 5-minute format from
Lesson 03.
