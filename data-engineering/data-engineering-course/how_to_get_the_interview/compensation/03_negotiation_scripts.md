# 03 — Negotiation Scripts

> **Lesson 3 of 4** · ~30 min

The 3 levers, the 5 reframes, and 5 worked negotiation
transcripts. The closing round, scripted.

---

## 1. The 3 levers

Every tech offer has 3 negotiable levers. They are not equal.
The recruiter will try to negotiate on the smallest one. Your
job is to negotiate on the largest one.

| Lever | Form | When to use | How much room |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Base** | Cash, bi-weekly | When the band has room. When you have competing offers. When the RSU grant is fixed. | 5-15% of band width. |
| **RSU** | Equity, vests 4yr | When you want long-term upside. When the stock is likely to appreciate. When base is locked. | 10-25% of grant. **This is the biggest lever.** |
| **Signing** | Cash, one-time | When the recruiter says "base and RSU are locked." When you have a competing offer. To bridge to year-1 cash. | 0-50% — most negotiable. |

**The mistake:** Most candidates negotiate base. The recruiter
moves base by 5%, the candidate feels good, the actual TC change
is 1-2%. The same conversation, redirected to RSU, would have
moved TC by 5-7%.

**The reframe:** When the recruiter offers $185k base, ask
about the **total compensation** and then ask about the **RSU**.
Almost all RSU movement is hidden from the recruiter's opening
number; the band has more room than they're showing.

---

## 2. The anchor-high principle

Recruiters anchor on a number within the band. They will offer
the **floor** of the band. You anchor on the **ceiling**.

**The mechanics:**
- The recruiter says "the base band for this role is $170-200k,
  and we're offering $185k." That's a midpoint offer. They have
  7.5% more room on base.
- You say: "Based on my research, the band for a Senior DE with
  my experience and your comp benchmarks is closer to $200-215k
  for the base, and $325-350k for the RSU over 4 years. I'd
  like to talk about getting closer to that range."

**What you've done:** Replaced their midpoint anchor with the
*ceiling* anchor. Their next number will be at the high end of
the band, not the low end. You haven't asked for something
unreasonable — you've asked for the top of the band, which is a
real number inside their structure.

**The numbers you anchor on:**
- Use levels.fyi (Lesson 04) for the 75th percentile at the
  level, in the same metro, at the same company.
- If the company is in the Bay Area and you're remote in
  Austin, anchor on Bay Area numbers but expect the recruiter
  to push back to geo-adjusted numbers.
- Always anchor on the dollar amount, not the percentage. They
  understand "$210k base" better than "10% above midpoint."

## 3. The 5 reframes

These are the sentences that move the negotiation when it's
stuck. They're not magic — they're just the right words at the
right time.

### 3.1 "I was expecting $X based on Y"

The most-used reframe. The structure is **number + justification**.

> *"Thanks for the offer. I was expecting the all-in TC to be
> closer to $X, based on [levels.fyi 75th percentile for this
> level / the competing offer I have / the market data for this
> role]. Can we talk about what it would take to get there?"*

The justification is critical. Without it, the recruiter says
"this is the best we can do." With it, they have something to
escalate with.

### 3.2 "I need a number to decide"

The deflection reframe. Use this when the recruiter asks for
your number.

> *"I'd rather hear the range you have approved for this role
> first. I don't want to anchor low by guessing — and I don't
> want to anchor high by guessing either. What's the band for
> the level and the role?"*

This puts the burden of disclosure on them. Most recruiters
will give you the band if you ask. If they refuse, say: *"Then
help me understand — what's the comp philosophy for this team?
Is it 50th percentile? 75th?"*

### 3.3 "I'd walk for $Y"

The "walk-away" reframe. The most underused. Use this *only*
when you actually would walk, and *only* once.

> *"I appreciate the offer, and I want to be honest with you —
> $X is below the threshold I need to make this work. I'd love
> to be at [Company], and I think I can add a lot to the team,
> but the gap between the offer and what I need to move is real.
> If we can close the gap to $Y, I'm in. If not, I understand
> and I wish the team well."*

The "if not, I understand" is what makes this credible. Don't
bluff. If you wouldn't actually walk, don't say you would.

### 3.4 The competing offer

The most powerful reframe. The structure is **compliment + reveal
+ ask**.

> *"I really enjoyed the loop and I'm excited about the team.
> To be transparent, I'm in process with [Company B] and they
> came in at $Z all-in. I want to be at [Company A] — the role
> is a better fit and the team is stronger — but the gap is
> real. Is there any way to revisit the [RSU / base / signing]
> to get closer to $Z?"*

The three rules:

- **Be honest about the competing offer.** Don't fabricate.
  Recruiters talk. A fake competing offer is a small-world
  hazard.
- **Don't trash the competing company.** Say "they came in at
  $Z" — not "they gave me way more."
- **Don't name the competing company unless asked.** "A
  peer-tier company" is enough. The recruiter will ask if they
  need to know.

### 3.5 "This is the best we can do" — the response

The recruiter says: "I'm sorry, this is the top of the band, we
can't move." Your response:

> *"I hear you. I want to make this work. Two questions: is
> there any flexibility on the [signing / RSU / relocation]? And
> if not, can we revisit the RSU at the 12-month mark if I'm
> hitting the bar in the first 6 months?"*

Two things just happened:

- You pushed on a different lever (the one they can move).
- You opened the door to a 12-month refresher negotiation,
  which is *more* valuable than the current offer in some cases
  (because you'll have a competing offer or promotion lever by
  then).

If they say no to both, the offer is what it is. Decide.

## 4. Email vs call: which channel for which part

Most candidates over-email and under-call. Email is for the
formal back-and-forth on the number. Call is for everything else.

| Step | Channel | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter asks for your number | **Email** | You want the ask in writing, with thinking time. |
| They come back with the band | **Call** | The reframe ("I was expecting $X") lands better spoken. |
| You push back on the number | **Call** | Tone matters. Written "I was expecting more" reads as a complaint; spoken it reads as a discussion. |
| You reveal a competing offer | **Call** | Sensitive. Tone matters more than ever. |
| They say "best we can do" | **Call** | Same — tone. You want to push back live. |
| They confirm the final number | **Email** | You want the final number in writing before you say yes. |
| You accept | **Email** | Always. With a thank-you line. |

**The rule of thumb:** If it's a *new* ask or a *sensitive*
reveal, call. If it's a *formal* number exchange or a
*confirmation*, email.

## 5. Five worked transcripts

Anonymized and condensed from real negotiations. Names and
numbers changed. The cadence and the lines are the point.

### 5.1 Meta E5 Senior DE

> **Recruiter (call):** "We'd like to extend an offer for the
> Senior DE role on the [X] team at E5. Base is $185k, RSU is
> $300k over 4 years, $20k signing, 10% target bonus. All-in
> year 1 is about $330k. What are your thoughts?"
>
> **Candidate:** "Thanks — I'm excited about the team and the
> role. Before I respond, I want to be honest. Based on
> levels.fyi and the Senior DE comp data, the 75th percentile
> for E5 at Meta is closer to $215k base and $375-400k RSU. The
> year-1 all-in I was expecting is closer to $380-400k. The
> gap between the offer and that range is about $50-70k. Can
> we talk about what it would take to close that gap?"
>
> **Recruiter:** "Let me check with the team. I'll come back
> tomorrow."
>
> **[Next day, call]**
>
> **Recruiter:** "We can move base to $200k, RSU to $340k
> ($85k/yr), and bump signing to $30k. New year-1 all-in is
> $358k."
>
> **Candidate:** "I appreciate that. The base movement helps.
> The RSU is still below the band. Can we push RSU to $360k? I
> have a competing offer that came in at $370k all-in, and I'd
> rather be at Meta — the role and team are a better fit — but
> the gap is still real."
>
> **[Final, email]**
>
> **Recruiter:** "We can do RSU at $360k and a one-time equity
> refresh at month 6 if you're hitting the bar. New all-in year
> 1 is $373k. Let me send the updated offer letter."
>
> **Outcome:** $330k → $373k year 1 (+$43k, +13%). 4-year TC
> improvement: ~$170k. One phone call and one email.

### 5.2 Google L5 Senior DE

> **Recruiter (call):** "We're extending an offer at L5 for the
> Senior DE role. Base $190k, RSU $320k over 4 years, 15%
> target bonus, $25k signing. Year-1 all-in $358k."
>
> **Candidate:** "Thanks. The offer is closer to where I need
> to be. Two specific asks: can we revisit the RSU grant? $340k
> would put me at the midpoint of the L5 band on RSU. And
> given the cost of living in the Bay Area, is there flexibility
> on the signing bonus to bridge year 1?"
>
> **Recruiter:** "Let me see what I can do."
>
> **[Next day]**
>
> **Recruiter:** "We can move RSU to $340k and signing to $35k.
> New year-1 all-in is $378k."
>
> **Outcome:** $358k → $378k (+$20k, +5.6%). 4-year TC
> improvement: ~$80k. The RSU was the lever — Google has more
> room on RSU than on base for L5.

### 5.3 Stripe Senior (IC3) DE

Stripe's bands are tighter on base but more flexible on RSU.
The negotiation moved the RSU more than the base.

> **Recruiter:** "Stripe is excited to extend an offer for the
> Senior DE role at IC3. Base $175k, RSU $400k over 4 years,
> $15k signing. All-in year 1 $320k."
>
> **Candidate:** "Thanks — Stripe has been my top choice. The
> base is below what I was expecting; the RSU is closer. For
> context, I have a competing offer at $340k all-in, and I'm
> choosing between them. Can we look at the RSU grant? I think
> $450k over 4 years is closer to the IC3 band for someone with
> my experience."
>
> **Recruiter:** "Let me check the RSU band. We can't move
> base — it's at the top of the band for IC3. We have some room
> on RSU."
>
> **[Two days later]**
>
> **Recruiter:** "We can do $440k RSU and a $25k signing.
> New year-1 all-in is $345k."
>
> **Outcome:** $320k → $345k (+$25k, +7.8%). The RSU moved
> $40k, which compounds over 4 years. Total 4-year improvement
> ~$100k.

### 5.4 Databricks Senior (L5) DE

Databricks is interesting because the equity is private
discounted RSUs that vest into public stock on liquidity
events. The negotiation moved both base and signing.

> **Recruiter:** "Databricks would like to extend an offer at
> L5 Senior DE. Base $165k, RSU $380k over 4 years, $10k
> signing, 10% target bonus. Year-1 all-in $310k."
>
> **Candidate:** "Thanks. Two things. First, on the RSU — given
> the illiquidity, I'd want to value these closer to a 50%
> discount, which puts the effective equity at $190k, not
> $380k. Can we look at either a larger grant or a different
> mix? Second, can we revisit the base? $165k is below the
> L5 band I've seen for Bay Area seniors."
>
> **Recruiter:** "Fair. Let me check."
>
> **[Next day]**
>
> **Recruiter:** "We can do $180k base, $420k RSU, $20k
> signing. Year-1 all-in is $345k."
>
> **Outcome:** $310k → $345k (+$35k, +11%). The candidate got
> the base to the top of the L5 band and the RSU up by $40k
> despite the illiquidity discount.

### 5.5 Snowflake Senior (IC3) DE

Snowflake is in the same boat as Databricks — public but
volatile stock. The negotiation was about *stock price risk*,
not just the number.

> **Recruiter:** "Snowflake is extending an offer at IC3 for
> the Senior DE role. Base $180k, RSU $350k over 4 years, 10%
> bonus, $15k signing. Year-1 all-in $335k."
>
> **Candidate:** "Thanks. I want to be transparent: given the
> recent volatility in the stock, I'd want to value the RSU
> more conservatively — closer to 30-40% off the headline. Can
> we either bump the RSU grant or add a signing bonus to
> compensate? My target is $370k year-1 all-in."
>
> **Recruiter:** "Let me see what I can do."
>
> **[Next day]**
>
> **Recruiter:** "We can do $190k base, $380k RSU, $25k
> signing. Year-1 all-in is $362k."
>
> **Outcome:** $335k → $362k (+$27k, +8%). The volatility
> argument moved both base and signing in addition to the RSU.

## 6. The "I need to think about it" move

When the recruiter says "the offer expires Friday" or "we need
a decision this week":

> *"I appreciate the timeline. I want to be honest — making a
> decision this week isn't realistic for me. I'm in process
> with another company, and I owe them a courtesy to complete
> their loop. Can we revisit on [date 7-10 days out]? If we
> can close the gap on [RSU / base] in the meantime, I'm
> confident we can wrap up then."*

The rules:

- **Don't accept a 24-hour deadline.** Most "exploding" offers
  are recruiter pressure, not company pressure.
- **Don't accept a 5-business-day deadline if you have another
  loop in flight.** A week is the minimum. Two is better.
- **If you have a competing offer in hand, name it.** "I have
  an offer from [Company B] that expires next Friday. I need
  to be fair to them."

---

## 7. What to do when they say "this is the best we can do"

The hardest moment. The recruiter is telling you the offer is
final. Three paths:

1. **Push on a different lever.** You may have already moved
   base and RSU. Ask about signing, relocation, ESPP, 401k
   match, vacation, start date, level, title, or the 6-month
   refresher. There's almost always a lever you haven't
   pushed.
2. **Walk.** If the gap is real and you have a competing
   offer (or are willing to interview for one), the strongest
   move is to say: *"I hear you, and I appreciate the
   transparency. The gap is real, and I'm going to continue
   my search. If anything changes, I'd love to revisit."*
   Most recruiters will come back with one more move in 2-3
   days.
3. **Accept.** If the offer is at-market for the level, and
   you have no competing offer, and the team/role is a
   good fit, accept. Not every negotiation is a 20% bump.

The wrong move: negotiating past the point of diminishing
returns. If the recruiter has moved 3 times and the gap is
$5k, take the offer. The cost of *not* taking it (continued
search, opportunity cost) is higher than $5k.

---

## 8. The closing script

When you accept, the script is short.

> **Email:**
>
> *"[Recruiter name], thank you for the time and the
> transparency through the process. I'm excited to accept the
> offer for the [role] at [level] and to join the [team]. The
> final offer — [base / RSU / signing / bonus] — is great and
> I'm looking forward to [start date]. Please let me know what
> the next steps are. Thanks again, [name]"*

The thank-you is real. The recruiter spent 4-6 weeks on you.
A gracious close is a small thing that pays off forever (you
will run into this recruiter again).

## Try it

Pick one of the 5 transcripts. Re-read it out loud. Mark the
lines you'd actually say. Then write your own 5-line script for
the 3 most likely scenarios in your negotiation:

1. The recruiter asks for your number.
2. The recruiter comes in below the band.
3. The recruiter says "this is the best we can do."

Practice them out loud. The words that work in real
negotiations are the words that don't surprise you when you say
them.
