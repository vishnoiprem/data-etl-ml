# 02 — Offer Anatomy

> **Lesson 2 of 4** · ~45 min

The 5 components of a tech offer, how each is taxed and vested,
and 3 worked all-in TC breakdowns (Meta E5, Google L5, Stripe
Senior).

---

## 1. The 5 components

Every tech offer is a bundle of 5 components. Most candidates
focus on the **base** because it's the only one on the pay stub.
This is a 4-7 figure mistake over a 4-year vest.

| # | Component | Typical % of TC (senior) | Form | When paid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Base salary** | 25-35% | Cash, bi-weekly | Every pay period |
| 2 | **RSU** (Restricted Stock Units) | 50-65% | Equity, vests over 4 yrs | On vest dates (cliff + monthly) |
| 3 | **Signing bonus** | 5-15% | Cash, one-time | First paycheck (or split) |
| 4 | **Performance bonus** | 0-10% | Cash, annual | Annually (or quarterly) |
| 5 | **Benefits / perks** | 5-10% | Insurance, 401k match, ESPP, commuter, etc. | Throughout the year |

A "500K offer" at Meta E5 might look like:

- Base: $185k
- RSU: $300k / 4yr ($75k/yr)
- Bonus: 10% of base ($18.5k target, paid quarterly)
- Signing: $20k
- Benefits: $30k equivalent (401k match + ESPP + perks)

**All-in TC in year 1:** $185k base + $75k RSU + $20k signing +
$18.5k bonus + $30k benefits = **$328.5k**.

Most candidates see "185 base" and anchor on that. The 4-year TC
is closer to $1.3M. The **base is the smallest lever** and the
**RSU is the largest**.

## 2. Component-by-component

### 2.1 Base salary

Cash. Paid bi-weekly (or semi-monthly at some companies). It's
the only component that's negotiable on a year-over-year basis
(raises) and the only one that compounds via 401k match.

**Tax:** Ordinary income. Federal + state + FICA. In California
at $200k, you're paying ~38% effective (federal 24%, state 9.3%,
FICA 1.45%, Medicare 2.35%, plus SDI). Take-home is ~62% of base.

**What's negotiable:** The band has a floor and a ceiling. The
recruiter will offer the floor; you negotiate up. Most companies
have ~10-20% band width within a level.

**What it's not:** It's not your "salary." It can be re-banded at
any time, and your next offer at the same level elsewhere will
have a different number.

### 2.2 RSU (Restricted Stock Units)

The biggest lever and the most mis-valued. An RSU is **not** a
stock option. It's a promise to give you N shares of company
stock on a vest date, taxed as ordinary income at vest.

**Grant vs vest:**
- The **grant** is the total number of shares awarded at offer
  time, usually expressed as a dollar value at grant (e.g., "$300k
  RSU over 4 years").
- The **vest** is the schedule — typically 25% at the 1-year
  cliff, then monthly or quarterly for the remaining 3 years.
- The **value** depends on the stock price at vest, not at
  grant. If the stock doubles, your RSU doubles. If it halves,
  it halves.

**Tax:** Ordinary income at vest. The company withholds at a
supplemental rate (often 22% federal + state, which is *not*
enough for most senior+ candidates — you'll owe more in April).
Some companies allow you to sell-to-cover at vest; others
auto-sell.

**Refreshers:** Most public companies grant refreshers annually
(typically 1x of base or 0.5-1x of original grant, depending on
performance). Refreshers usually vest over 4 years from the
refresh date. A "$300k RSU" offer is really a "$300k RSU + ~$75k
annual refresher" if you stay 4+ years.

**Acceleration on termination:** This is the "golden handcuffs"
question. If you're laid off, do your unvested shares vest? Most
public companies (Meta, Google) have **no acceleration** —
unvested shares are forfeited. Some (Netflix, in the past) had
full acceleration. Some have **double-trigger** acceleration
(unvested + change of control). Read the offer letter for the
specific clause.

**How to value it:**
- If the company is public: use the current stock price, but
  discount by 15-30% for the 4-year risk. (Forces you to assume
  the stock won't grow, which is conservative.)
- If the company is private: discount by 50% or more. Private
  shares are illiquid; the realized value is the next liquidity
  event (IPO, acquisition, secondary). Some never liquidate.

### 2.3 Signing bonus

Cash, one-time, paid in the first paycheck (or split over 2
paychecks). Used to bridge the gap between your current TC and
the new offer, *or* to compensate for a forfeited bonus at your
current employer.

**Tax:** Ordinary income, but the supplemental rate is steep.
A $50k signing bonus is closer to $32k take-home.

**Payback clause:** Most signing bonuses have a **1-year
clawback**. If you leave within 12 months, you owe the unearned
portion back. Some companies prorate monthly; some require the
full amount back. Read the clause.

**When to ask for it:**
- You're leaving a sign-on bonus at your current employer.
- You're below band on base and the recruiter won't budge.
- You have a competing offer and the recruiter needs a
  short-term lever to close.

The signing bonus is the **shortest-term lever** a recruiter has.
If the recruiter says "we can't move base or RSU," they can
usually move signing. Use it.

### 2.4 Performance bonus

Cash, paid annually (or quarterly at some companies). Most big
tech pays 10-20% of base as a target bonus, with multipliers
(0.5x to 2x) based on performance. P50 is typically 1.0x.

**Tax:** Ordinary income. Withheld at the supplemental rate
(22% federal at offer time, often more in April).

**When to count it:** Only count the **target** bonus (1.0x) in
your all-in TC, not the max. Companies rarely pay max. If the
target is 10% of $185k = $18.5k, that's what you count. Don't
count the 2x number; you won't hit it.

### 2.5 Benefits / perks

The most under-counted component. Includes:

- **Health insurance:** Premium paid by employer. Family coverage
  can be worth $15-25k/yr to you (the company's cost). Your
  payroll deduction is often $0-3k/yr for employee-only; family
  is more.
- **401k match:** Typical 4-6% of base. Free money. If base is
  $200k, the match is $8-12k/yr.
- **ESPP (Employee Stock Purchase Plan):** Allows you to buy
  company stock at a 15% discount. If the stock is flat, you make
  15% in 6 months. Worth ~$2-5k/yr for senior+.
- **Commuter:** Pre-tax transit/parking. Worth $1-3k/yr.
- **Food, gym, internet:** Variable. Worth $2-5k/yr if you use
  it.
- **Parental leave, fertility, childcare:** Hard to value but
  real. Worth checking.

**How to value:** Add up the dollar amounts where you can.
For perks you don't use, value at $0 — they're not real comp
to you. The full benefits number for a senior at a public big-tech
company is $30-50k/yr. The number for a 50-person startup is
$5-15k/yr.

## 3. The "all-in" number

The **all-in TC** is what you should compare offers on. The
recruiter computes it. You should compute it. They should match.
They often don't.

### 3.1 The recruiter's calculation

Recruiters typically quote all-in TC in **year 1**. This
includes:

- Base
- Target bonus
- First-year RSU (1/4 of the 4-year grant, including the cliff)
- Signing bonus
- Benefits (sometimes)

### 3.2 Your calculation

You should also compute **4-year TC**, which is the right
comparison if you're going to stay 4 years. The 4-year TC is:

- 4 × base
- 4 × target bonus
- 4-year RSU grant (the full number, discounted by 15-30% for
  public stock risk or 50% for private)
- Signing bonus (one-time)
- 4 × benefits

Most candidates don't do the 4-year number. It's the single
biggest comp mistake. An offer that looks higher in year 1 can
be lower over 4 years because of a smaller RSU grant.

### 3.3 The discount

Public company RSU is **not** guaranteed. Discount the grant by
15-30% to be conservative. Private company RSU is much riskier;
discount by 50% or more. If the company is pre-IPO, the RSU
might be worth $0 if the company fails or never goes public.

A "$300k RSU over 4 years" at a public company is closer to
$210-255k in expected value.

## 4. Worked example: Meta E5 Senior Data Engineer

**Headline:** "We're excited to extend an offer for the Senior
Data Engineer role on the [X] team at the E5 level."

**The offer:**

- Base: $185,000 / yr
- RSU: 1,200 shares at $250 grant price = $300,000 over 4 years
  ($75,000 / yr, 25% cliff at 12 months, then monthly vest)
- Bonus: 10% target = $18,500 / yr (paid quarterly)
- Signing: $20,000 (one-time, 12-month clawback)
- Benefits: $32,000 / yr (health + 401k match + ESPP + commuter)

**Year 1 all-in TC (recruiter math):**

- Base: $185k
- RSU (1/4 grant): $75k
- Bonus: $18.5k
- Signing: $20k
- Benefits: $32k
- **Total: $330,500**

**4-year TC (your math, with 20% RSU discount):**

- Base (4 yr): $740k
- RSU (4 yr, discounted 20%): $240k
- Bonus (4 yr): $74k
- Signing: $20k
- Benefits (4 yr): $128k
- **Total: $1,202,000**

**What this means:** The offer is "~$330k year 1 / ~$1.2M over
4 years." Most candidates anchor on the $185k base and leave the
$75k/yr RSU on the table during negotiation (see Lesson 03).

## 5. Worked example: Google L5 Senior Data Engineer

**The offer:**

- Base: $190,000 / yr
- RSU: $320,000 over 4 years ($80,000 / yr, 25% cliff then
  monthly)
- Bonus: 15% target = $28,500 / yr (paid quarterly)
- Signing: $25,000
- Benefits: $35,000 / yr

**Year 1 all-in TC:**

- Base: $190k
- RSU: $80k
- Bonus: $28.5k
- Signing: $25k
- Benefits: $35k
- **Total: $358,500**

**4-year TC (your math):**

- Base: $760k
- RSU (discounted 20%): $256k
- Bonus: $114k
- Signing: $25k
- Benefits: $140k
- **Total: $1,295,000**

**Compare to Meta E5 above:** Google L5 is **+$28k year 1** and
**+$93k over 4 years**. The delta is mostly in the RSU grant
($300k vs $320k) and the slightly higher base.

## 6. Worked example: Stripe Senior (IC3) Data Engineer

Stripe is the most negotiable of the three because their bands
have more room. The base is *lower* than Meta/Google, but the
RSU is *higher* in expected value because Stripe equity has
historically appreciated faster.

**The offer:**

- Base: $175,000 / yr
- RSU: $400,000 over 4 years ($100,000 / yr, 1-year cliff then
  monthly)
- Bonus: 0% (Stripe doesn't have a cash bonus; everything is in
  RSU)
- Signing: $15,000
- Benefits: $30,000 / yr

**Year 1 all-in TC:**

- Base: $175k
- RSU: $100k
- Signing: $15k
- Benefits: $30k
- **Total: $320,000**

**4-year TC (your math, with 20% RSU discount):**

- Base: $700k
- RSU (discounted 20%): $320k
- Signing: $15k
- Benefits: $120k
- **Total: $1,155,000**

**Note:** The headline year-1 number is *lower* than Meta E5
and Google L5. But the 4-year number is also lower — Stripe
isn't always the highest TC offer at senior, especially if
you're comparing to Meta/Google at the same calendar time.

What Stripe *is* good at: negotiable base, negotiable RSU, and
historically strong equity appreciation. A 2020 Stripe offer
appreciated 4-5x by 2024. A 2024 Stripe offer is more of an
unknown.

## 7. The 6-question checklist before you respond

Before you reply to any offer, do this:

1. **Do I know the level?** (Lesson 01)
2. **Do I have the offer in writing?** Verbal offers are not
   offers. Ask for the written offer letter.
3. **Have I computed the year-1 and 4-year all-in TC?** If not,
   do it now. The recruiter's number and yours should match.
4. **What's the RSU stock price at grant vs current?** If the
   stock has moved 20% since the grant, the value is 20% off.
5. **What's the cliff and vest schedule?** 25% cliff + monthly
   is standard. Anything different (e.g., 50% cliff) is a
   negative signal.
6. **What's the signing bonus clawback period?** 12 months is
   standard. Anything longer is a yellow flag.

If all 6 are clear, you have the information you need to
negotiate. Go to Lesson 03.
