# How to Get the Interview — A Practical Guide for Data Engineers

> **13 lessons (1 main module + 1 compensation sub-module) · 9 videos · ~5 hours of focused prep**
>
> **Author:** [Prem Vishnoi](https://medium.com/@premvishnoi) · <pvishnoi@avilx.com>
>
> **Companion articles:** [medium.com/@premvishnoi](https://medium.com/@premvishnoi)

The most under-prepped part of the data engineering interview loop is
the part that happens **before** the loop: getting the interview in
the first place.

You can be the best data engineer in the world. If your resume gets
filtered out by the ATS, your cover letter reads like a template, and
you have no referral, you will never get to demonstrate it. This
track fixes that.

This is not a "how to write a resume" guide in the generic sense.
This is a **data-engineering-specific, senior+ calibrated** guide
to the mechanics of getting your application in front of a human
who is hiring, and convincing them to spend 45 minutes with you.

---

## What's in this track

**Prose-only, with one artifact.** Lessons are Markdown files you
read and act on. The capstone is a **targeted resume artifact** you
produce (a 1-page resume customized to a specific job description)
that you can ship the same day.

**Pattern-based, not generic.** Every lesson is built around the
realities of data engineering hiring: how "data engineer" and
"analytics engineer" and "ML platform engineer" labels get routed
inside an ATS, how the 6-second recruiter scan works, what a good
targeted resume looks like for *this* JD vs. *that* JD.

**Calibrated for senior+.** The audience is data engineers targeting
**Senior (L5/E5) → Staff (L6/E6) → Principal (L7/E7)** data
platform, ML platform, and analytics engineering roles. Junior
candidates will get value from the resume structure, but the
internal-transfer and staff-targeting lessons assume 3+ years of
experience.

## How to use this track

**Week 1 — Read in order (3-4 hours).** Read Lessons 01-04 in one
sitting. You'll have the mental model and the mechanics by Sunday.
Then do Lesson 05 (the sample resume) as a reference — return to it
when writing your own.

**Week 2 — Produce your artifacts (4-6 hours).** Do Lesson 06
(cover letter) and the capstone in `exercise.md`: pick 2 real JDs,
write 2 targeted resumes, write 2 cover letters, set up your
tracking spreadsheet (Lesson 08), and send 3 cold-outreach messages
(Lesson 09). **The work happens this week, not last week.**

**Week 3 — Refine and ship (2-3 hours).** Iterate on the artifacts
based on feedback from 2 friends. Send the referrals. Apply. Track.

**Module 02 — Compensation (the closing round).** When an offer
comes in, drop into `compensation/`. Read Lesson 10 (leveling)
to confirm you're being offered the right level, then Lessons
11-13 in order before responding to the recruiter. The closing
round is the round most candidates under-prep — and where 5-15%
of TC is left on the table.

By the end of week 3 you should have:
- 1 master resume (1 page, untargeted)
- 2 targeted resumes (each customized to a specific JD)
- 2 cover letters (each <250 words)
- 1 tracking spreadsheet (with 10+ open applications)
- 5+ referral / cold outreach messages sent

That's a full pipeline. The actual interview-prep work happens
*after* this track — in the system design and behavioral tracks.
The closing round (negotiating the offer) happens *with* this
track — in the `compensation/` sub-module.

## A note on what this track is *not*

It is **not** a job search motivational guide. There is no "manifest
your next role" content here. It is also **not** a guide to lying on
your resume, faking credentials, or gaming ATS systems. Every tactic
in this track is one a senior hiring manager at a top company would
endorse. If a tactic feels manipulative, skip it.

The premise: **the work of getting an interview is the same work as
doing the interview well — specific, honest, quantified, and tailored
to the audience.** If you do this track right, you will literally
do less work in your job search while getting more responses.
