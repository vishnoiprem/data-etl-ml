# Capstone — Produce Your Own Targeted Resume

> **End-of-track exercise** · ~3-4 hours

The deliverable that proves you finished the track: **a real
targeted resume + cover letter, for a real JD, ready to send
this week.**

---

## Goal

By the end of this exercise, you will have:

- [ ] A **master resume** (1 page, untargeted, the same structure
  as the Jordan Park sample in `artifacts/sample_de_resume.md`)
- [ ] **2 targeted resumes** (each 1 page, each for a specific
  real JD)
- [ ] **2 cover letters** (each < 300 words, each for the same
  2 JDs)
- [ ] A **tracking spreadsheet** (template in Lesson 08)
- [ ] **3 outreach messages** sent (cold DM, peer referral,
  alumni outreach — pick 3 from Lesson 09)
- [ ] **2 real applications** submitted through company career
  pages, with the targeted resumes + cover letters attached

This is the deliverable. If you ship it, you have a job-search
pipeline. If you don't, you don't — yet.

---

## Step 1: Pick 2 real JDs (30 min)

**Do not skip this step or substitute fake JDs.** The exercise
only works with real ones.

Pick 2 JDs that meet all 3 of these criteria:

1. **You would actually take the role** if offered. No "this is
   good practice" picks. The work has to be real.
2. **The role is a credible match** for your experience (50%+
   match on the required skills). Stretch roles are fine; fantasy
   roles are not.
3. **The role is currently open** (not closed, not "we expect
   to open this in Q3"). You need a real application path.

**Where to find them:**

- LinkedIn (search "senior data engineer" + filter to "Past
  Week")
- Levels.fyi (browse companies, find data roles)
- HN Who's Hiring (this month's thread)
- Company career pages directly (Stripe, Databricks, Airbnb,
  Notion, the FAANG data platform teams)

**Save the JDs.** You'll reference them in every step below.
Add them to your tracking spreadsheet as the first 2 rows.

---

## Step 2: Do the 4-pass read on each JD (30 min)

For each of the 2 JDs, do the 4-pass read from Lesson 04:

1. **Title and level** (30 sec)
2. **Required vs. preferred** (2 min)
3. **The 3-5 mirror keywords** (5 min)
4. **The "pain" and the "win"** (5 min)

Write the outputs in a notes file (`jd_analysis_<company>.md`)
or in a column in your spreadsheet. The 4-pass output is what
you'll reference when writing the targeted resume and cover
letter.

---

## Step 3: Build your master resume (60-90 min)

Using the Jordan Park sample in `artifacts/sample_de_resume.md`
as a template:

1. **Header** — name, location, email, LinkedIn, GitHub (if
   relevant)
2. **Positioning statement** — 1 line, title + stack + seniority
   signal + target role
3. **Skills** — 4 categories, 4-6 tools each, JD-relevant
4. **Experience** — 3-4 jobs, 4-6 bullets each, all in XYZ
   format
5. **Selected projects** — 2-3, with tech stack + outcome
6. **Education** — 1-2 lines

Save as `resume_master.md`. Convert to PDF (1 page, single
column, 9-11pt font, 0.5-0.7" margins).

**Self-check before moving on:**

- [ ] 1 page (not 2, not "almost 1")
- [ ] Single column (no tables, no multi-column layout)
- [ ] Every bullet has a number
- [ ] Every bullet starts with a strong action verb
- [ ] No "responsible for," no "worked on," no "helped with"
- [ ] Positioning statement is 1 line, names title + stack +
  target role

If any of these fail, fix it before proceeding. The master
resume is the substrate for the 2 targeted resumes.

---

## Step 4: Build the 2 targeted resumes (60-90 min each)

For each of the 2 JDs:

1. **Save the master as a new file** (`resume_<company>.md`).
2. **Rewrite the positioning statement** using the JD's title
   and stack. (10 min)
3. **Reorder the skills section** to put the JD's top 3
   keywords first. (5 min)
4. **Tighten 2-3 bullets** in your experience section to
   mirror the JD's keywords and pain. (30-45 min)
5. **Proofread, convert to PDF, save as
   `resume_<company>.pdf`.** (10 min)

**Self-check for each targeted resume:**

- [ ] Positioning statement references the JD's title and stack
- [ ] Top 3 skills in the skills section match the JD's top
  3 keywords
- [ ] At least 1 bullet mentions a tool/tech from the JD that
  wasn't prominent in the master
- [ ] Still 1 page
- [ ] File name is `resume_<company>_<role>.pdf`

---

## Step 5: Write the 2 cover letters (30 min each)

For each of the 2 JDs, using the 4-paragraph structure from
Lesson 06:

1. **Paragraph 1 (Hook):** the role title + a specific
   reference to a company blog post, product, or recent
   announcement
2. **Paragraph 2 (Why them):** 2-3 sentences on what the
   company is doing that you find interesting
3. **Paragraph 3 (Why you):** 1-2 of your best accomplishments,
   in XYZ format, mapped to the JD's pain
4. **Paragraph 4 (The ask):** direct, with a specific
   conversation topic

Save as `cover_letter_<company>.md`. Convert to PDF.

**Self-check for each cover letter:**

- [ ] 200-300 words total
- [ ] Names the role title in paragraph 1
- [ ] Has a specific company reference (not "I love your
  mission")
- [ ] Has 1-2 XYZ bullets in paragraph 3
- [ ] Has a direct ask in paragraph 4
- [ ] No "I am a perfect fit" / "I am writing to apply"
- [ ] File name is `cover_letter_<company>.pdf`

---

## Step 6: Set up your tracking spreadsheet (30 min)

Use the template from Lesson 08, Section 5. Add 2 rows for the
2 applications you're about to submit, plus 3-5 more rows for
3-5 *other* roles you find this week. The other roles are the
"supplemental" applications — lower-effort, but you have to
have them in the pipeline to get to 5-10 applications per week.

Columns: Company, Role, URL, Date Applied, Source, Referrer,
Status, Last Action, Next Action, Next Action Date, Notes.

**Self-check:**

- [ ] Every row has a status (Applied / Planned / Withdrawn)
- [ ] Every "Applied" row has a "Next Action Date"
- [ ] At least 5 rows in the spreadsheet (your 2 + 3-5
  supplemental)

---

## Step 7: Send 3 outreach messages (30 min)

Pick 3 from the templates in Lesson 09:

1. **One cold DM to a hiring manager** at one of the 2 target
   companies (use Lesson 08, Section 3 template)
2. **One peer / friend-of-friend referral ask** (use Lesson 09,
   Section 3, Message 2)
3. **One alumni outreach** (use Lesson 09, Section 3, Message 3)

Send them this week. Log each in the tracking spreadsheet
(under the "Notes" column of the relevant row, or as a
separate "Outreach" tab).

**Self-check:**

- [ ] 3 messages actually sent (not drafted, *sent*)
- [ ] Each message references specific work / a specific role
- [ ] Each message is bounded (15 min, 5 min, etc.)
- [ ] Each message has a graceful out

---

## Step 8: Submit the 2 real applications (30 min)

For each of the 2 JDs:

1. **Find the company career page** (not the LinkedIn Easy
   Apply path)
2. **Submit the application** with:
   - `resume_<company>.pdf`
   - `cover_letter_<company>.pdf`
   - The positioning-statement-adapted version of your
     "About me" / "Why are you interested in this role"
     free-text fields
3. **Log in the spreadsheet** (Date Applied, Source, Next
   Action Date = 7 days from today for the first follow-up)

**Self-check:**

- [ ] Both applications actually submitted (not just the PDFs
  ready)
- [ ] Both rows in the spreadsheet have Date Applied, Source,
  Next Action Date
- [ ] Calendar reminder set for the 7-day follow-up for each

When you're done, you should have these 7 files in a single

Plus:
- 3 outreach messages sent (logged in the spreadsheet)
- 2 applications submitted (logged in the spreadsheet)
- 5-7 follow-up reminders on your calendar for the next 7 days

That's the deliverable. That's the end of the track.

## What to do if you get stuck

The 3 most common stuck points, in order of frequency:

1. **"I can't write a 1-page master resume."** You're not
   prioritizing. Cut the 3 least-relevant jobs / projects.
   Cut bullets that don't have numbers. Cut the "Skills"
   section down to 4 categories of 4-6 tools. Iterate. If
   you can't cut to 1 page, your master isn't done.

2. **"I don't have numbers for my bullets."** You have numbers.
   You just haven't extracted them. Spend 30 minutes going
   through your calendar / PRs / Slack messages for the last
   6 months. Every project has a number — TB/day, latency,
   users, dollars, % improvement, time saved. If you genuinely
   cannot find a number for a bullet, the bullet isn't important
   enough to keep.

3. **"I can't find a JD I actually want."** You're being too
   picky. The exercise works with any JD where you'd take the
   call if it came. Lower the bar for the *exercise* (not for
   the search itself). You can always do another round later.

If you're stuck for >2 hours, ping the course author or
re-read the relevant lesson. Don't grind on a stuck point.

---

## After the capstone

You have a job-search pipeline. The next step is to **maintain
it** — 1 hour/day, 5 days/week, for the next 4-8 weeks. The
work is:

- **Daily:** 1-2 cold DMs, 1-2 follow-ups on old applications
- **Weekly:** 2-3 new applications, 1-2 new targeted resumes
- **Monthly:** review the spreadsheet, retire dead applications,
  refresh the master resume with new accomplishments

After 4-8 weeks, you'll have 20-30 applications in flight,
8-15 screen calls, 3-6 onsites, and (with any luck) 1-3
offers. That's the outcome the track is designed to produce.

Now go ship it.
