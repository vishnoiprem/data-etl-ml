# 01 — How to Get The Interview

> **Lesson 1 of 9** · ~10 min

The mental model for the entire job-search funnel — and where your
effort actually goes.

---

## 1. The funnel

A data engineering job application does not go from "you click apply"
to "you're sitting in front of a hiring manager." It passes through
**four gates**, and each gate has a different reader, a different
budget, and a different reason to reject you.

```
  1,000 applications
        │
        ▼   (Gate 1: ATS keyword + filter pass)
       250   ← 75% filtered out before any human sees them
        │
        ▼   (Gate 2: Recruiter 6-second scan)
        40   ← 6-8 seconds per resume; ruthlessly optimized for fit
        │
        ▼   (Gate 3: Hiring manager 2-3 minute read)
        10   ← now the bar is "would I work with this person?"
        │
        ▼   (Gate 4: Recruiter screen call)
         4   ← 30 min, behavioral + logistics
        │
        ▼
    Interview loop
```

The numbers vary by company and seniority, but the **shape** is
the same. The funnel is *not* linear — your effort should be
weighted heavily toward the top of the funnel (the gates that
filter out the most candidates) and toward the *inputs* to those
gates (your resume, your referrals, your positioning).

A candidate who optimizes only for the bottom of the funnel (the
interview itself) while ignoring the top (the resume, the title,
the keywords) will send 200 applications and get 4 screens. A
candidate who optimizes both gets the same 4 screens out of 30
applications, with 1/7th the work.

---

## 2. Who reads what

Different readers at different gates. Different readers want
different things. This is the most-skipped step in most candidates'
mental model — they write *one* resume and hope it speaks to all
four readers. It doesn't.

| Gate | Reader | Time spent | What they look for | What they filter on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | ATS (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever) | <1 sec | Keyword match, job title, years of experience | Missing keywords, wrong title, missing degree, weird formatting |
| **2** | Recruiter / sourcer | 6-8 sec | Title + 1st company + 1 number | Bad title, no quantification, "responsible for" bullets, typos |
| **3** | Hiring manager | 2-3 min | Does this person have *the* skill we need? | Generic bullets, no DE-specific skills, mismatch with JD |
| **4** | Recruiter (screen call) | 30 min | Communication, salary, logistics | Rambling, ego, no specific stories |

The trick: **your resume is read by all four readers**, and the
reader who spends the *least* time on it (the recruiter) has the
most veto power. Optimize for them first, then for the others.

---

## 3. The three levers

You have exactly three levers to get more interviews. There is no
fourth.

1. **Volume** — apply to more roles. Cheap, exhausting, low-yield.
   Diminishing returns past 30-50 applications.
2. **Conversion** — make each application better. Expensive per
   application, but compounds. Targeted resumes, good cover letters,
   clean positioning. This is where this track focuses.
3. **Sourcing** — get the role to come to you, or get a warm intro
   that bypasses Gate 1 entirely. Highest yield, hardest to do at
   scale. This is referrals, internal mobility, and cold outbound
   to hiring managers.

Most candidates default to lever 1. They send 200 applications, get
3 screens, and wonder why they're not getting offers. The answer
is: they're playing a 1-lever game in a 3-lever market.

A more senior+ strategy is **2 and 3, with 1 as backup**: 20-30
highly targeted applications with referrals or cold outbound, plus
a small volume tail for completeness.

## 5. A worked example: one candidate, two strategies

**Maya, 4 years DE, targeting senior roles at FAANG.**

**Strategy A (volume-only):**
- 150 applications over 3 months
- 1 targeted resume (the same for all 150)
- No referrals, no cold outreach
- Outcome: 6 screens, 2 onsites, 0 offers
- Time spent: ~40 hours on applications

**Strategy B (conversion + sourcing):**
- 35 applications over 6 weeks
- 3 targeted resumes (one per role family)
- 1 cover letter template, customized per role
- 12 cold-outreach DMs to hiring managers
- 4 referrals from ex-coworkers
- Outcome: 9 screens, 4 onsites, 2 offers
- Time spent: ~25 hours on applications + outreach

The math is not subtle. Strategy B is fewer applications, fewer
hours, and 2x the offers. The only reason more candidates don't
do Strategy B is that **the work happens up front**: you have to
write the targeted resume, send the DMs, and ask for the referrals
*before* you have anything to show for it. That's the entire
game.

## Try it

Before Lesson 02, write down your current answers to these 4
questions. Be honest.

1. How many applications did I send in the last 90 days? ____
2. How many of those were *targeted* (customized to the JD)? ____
3. How many referrals did I get in the last 90 days? ____
4. How many cold-outreach messages did I send to hiring managers? ____

If your answers to (1) are >30 and your answers to (2), (3), and
(4) are all <5, you're playing Strategy A. That's fine — but
this track is designed to move you to Strategy B in 2-3 weeks.
