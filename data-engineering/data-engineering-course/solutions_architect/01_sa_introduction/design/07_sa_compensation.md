# 07 — Compensation and Career Trajectory for SAs

> **Lesson 7 of 8 — SA Interview Introduction** · ~10 min

The SA career arc, the comp at each level, the IC-vs-SA
fork, and the question you should ask your recruiter before
you take the offer. Numbers are rough; the *shape* of the
trajectory is the durable part.

---

## 1. The SA career arc

The SA role has a clear ladder at every hyperscaler, with
some variation in titles. The shape is consistent:

| Level | AWS | GCP | Azure | Salesforce | Snowflake |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Entry** | SA I (SAA) | L5 Solutions | IC5 | Solutions Engineer | SA I |
| **Senior** | SA II / Sr. SA | L6 / L7 | IC6 / IC7 | Senior SA | Senior SA |
| **Principal** | Principal SA | L8 | IC8 | Principal SA | Principal SA |
| **Distinguished** | Distinguished SA | L9 | IC9 | — | — |

At most companies, the "entry" SA (SA I / L5) is the
post-program role (e.g., post-AWS-SA-Associate or post-
Snowflake-onboarding). The "senior" SA (SA II / L6) is the
*first level you can come in from outside* with sufficient
experience. The "principal" SA (L7-L8) is the senior+
level — strategic accounts, public content, and peer
reputation are the metrics.

The bar to move from SA II to Sr. SA is **typically 2-3
years and a "principal-track" performance rating.** The bar
to move from Sr. SA to Principal SA is **typically 4-6
years, peer reputation, and a strategic-account win.**

## 2. Comp ranges (FAANG-scale, 2024-2026, rough)

These are rough ranges for US-based, hyperscaler-scale SA
roles. Treat as order-of-magnitude, not exact.

| Level | Title | Base | Variable / RSU | Total (year 1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AWS SA I / GCP L5 / Azure IC5** | Solutions Architect | $130-170k | $80-150k | **$210-320k** |
| **AWS SA II / GCP L6 / Azure IC6** | Senior SA | $160-200k | $150-300k | **$310-500k** |
| **AWS Sr. SA / GCP L7 / Azure IC7** | Principal SA | $190-240k | $300-600k | **$490-840k** |
| **AWS Principal SA / GCP L8 / Azure IC8** | Sr. Principal SA | $230-300k | $500-1M+ | **$730k-1.3M+** |

The variable / RSU column breaks down differently by
company:

- **AWS / GCP / Azure:** Variable is 10-30% of base, paid
  against deal-attribution and customer-outcome metrics. RSU
  is the bulk of the upside, vesting over 4 years.
- **Salesforce:** Higher base, lower variable. Variable is
  tied to territory performance and customer NPS.
- **Snowflake / Databricks / Confluent:** Higher RSU
  weighting, often 50%+ of total comp is RSU. Variable is
  tied to deal outcomes.

For comparison, the IC equivalent at the same companies:

| Level | Title | Total (year 1) |
|---|---|---|
| **L5 (Google) / E5 (Meta) / IC5** | Senior Engineer | $400-700k |
| **L6 / E6 / IC6** | Staff Engineer | $700k-1.1M |
| **L7 / E7 / IC7** | Sr. Staff | $1.2-2M |
| **L8 / E8 / IC8** | Principal | $2-4M+ |

Two patterns worth noticing:

1. **At SA I/II (L5/L6 equivalent), SA comp is noticeably
   *below* the IC ladder.** The trade-off at this level is
   real — you're trading dollars for customer contact and
   varied work. If you don't love the customer-facing work,
   this is a bad trade.
2. **At Sr. SA / Principal SA (L7/L8 equivalent), the comp
   converges with the IC ladder.** At this level, the dollar
   trade-off is gone; the question becomes "what kind of
   work do I want to do for the next 5-10 years."

The comp for **post-sales SA** is typically 10-15% *below*
pre-sales SA at the same level (less variable comp, more
stable base). The comp for **technical SA** (hyperscaler
senior roles) is *above* the standard SA ladder at the same
level, often 10-20% higher than the SA II equivalent.

## 3. The IC-vs-SA fork

The decision most senior engineers face at L5/L6 is "do I
go IC Staff or do I go SA Senior?" The comp trade-off
flips at different companies and different levels. The
honest version of the trade-off:

| Dimension | IC Staff | Senior SA |
|---|---|---|
| **What you ship** | Systems, code, design docs | Customer wins, architectures, relationships |
| **Leverage model** | Your own output (deep) | Your deals' outcomes (wide) |
| **Skill set** | Technical depth, judgment, craft | Customer focus, communication, technical depth |
| **Reversibility** | High — you can switch teams, switch stacks, stay IC | Medium — going back to IC is a re-pitch, not a re-entry |
| **Feedback loop** | Fast — code compiles, system scales | Slow — a deal takes 3-12 months; a customer renewal takes 24 |
| **Failure mode** | Technical debt, missed deadlines | Deals you couldn't close, customer escalations |
| **Best signal of success** | "You ship things that work" | "Customers ask for you by name" |
| **What you give up** | Customer contact, deal variety, the win moment | Hands-on building, deep technical ownership |

The last row is the one nobody talks about. The "joy of
shipping" is real, and you give it up. The "joy of closing
a deal" is also real, and you trade for it. The decision is
which joy you want.

## 4. The 3 questions to ask before you take the offer

When you're evaluating an SA offer, ask the recruiter (or
the hiring manager) these 3 questions:

1. **What's the variable comp tied to?** If it's tied to
   deal-attribution (closed-won revenue, ACV, etc.), the
   comp is partly out of your control — the deal might slip
   and your variable is reduced. If it's tied to
   *customer-outcome* metrics (NPS, customer renewal, etc.),
   the comp is more under your control.
2. **What's the deal allocation model?** Some companies
   assign deals to SAs by territory; others by skill; others
   by relationship. The model determines your "luck" factor
   — i.e., whether the deals you're assigned are the ones
   you're most likely to win.
3. **What's the path from SA II to Sr. SA, concretely?** Is
   it tenure-based? Performance-based? A specific
   certification? The answer tells you whether the next
   promotion is realistic in 2-3 years (which is the norm)
   or 4-5 (which is a flag).

The 3 answers together tell you whether the comp trajectory
is real or theoretical. If the recruiter is vague on all 3,
assume the trajectory is theoretical and negotiate your
year-1 offer accordingly.

## 5. The "comp target" exercise

Before you interview, set your comp target. The exercise:

1. **What's your current total comp?** (base + RSU/bonus,
   year-1 actual.)
2. **What's your target total comp in year 1 as an SA?**
   (The rule of thumb: -10 to +30% for lateral moves, +30
   to +50% for moves from smaller vendors.)
3. **What's your target in year 3?** (RSU vesting + variable
   comp typically catches up or exceeds the IC track by
   year 3.)
4. **What's your "walk-away" number?** (The comp below which
   the trade-off isn't worth it.)

Write these 4 numbers down. Negotiate from #2 against #4.
If the company can't get to #4, the deal isn't worth it.
