# 08 — Common Misconceptions About SA Roles

> **Lesson 8 of 8 — SA Interview Introduction** · ~10 min

The 8 misconceptions about the SA role that send good
candidates home with a "thanks but no thanks." Each one is
also something experienced interviewers watch for. Knowing
what they're watching for is half the battle.

---

## 1. The 8 misconceptions

### Misconception 1: "SAs don't code"

**The misconception:** SAs spend their day in meetings and
slide decks. They never write code.

**The reality:** All three SA flavors (pre-sales, post-sales,
technical SA) write code. Pre-sales SAs build PoCs, demos,
and reference architectures. Post-sales SAs pair-debug and
review customer code. Technical SAs write deep analyses and
demo code. The amount varies, but "no code" is a myth.

**What the interviewer watches for:** Does the candidate
expect to stop writing code? If yes, that's a flag. The SA
role is *technical*, just customer-facing. Candidates who
want to stop writing code are usually misaligned and will
churn within 18 months.

### Misconception 2: "SAs are senior engineers who got tired of coding"

**The misconception:** The SA role is the "retirement
track" for engineers who don't want to keep up with the
technical ladder.

**The reality:** The SA role is its own ladder, with its own
*technical* progression. Sr. SAs and Principal SAs are
expected to be technically deep — often deeper than
the Staff IC equivalent — because they're defending
architectures against CTOs and principal engineers, not
just building them.

**What the interviewer watches for:** Does the candidate
frame the SA role as a step *down* in technical depth, or
as a step *across* (different kind of technical work)?
Step-down framings get downleveled; step-across framings
get offers.

### Misconception 3: "SAs are salespeople"

**The misconception:** SAs are quota-carrying sales reps who
happen to be technical.

**The reality:** SAs are *technical* roles that *support*
sales. They don't carry quota (in most companies). The AE
carries the deal; the SA provides the technical credibility
that unblocks the deal. AEs at AWS, GCP, Azure, and
Snowflake are paid on closed-won revenue; SAs are paid on
customer-outcome metrics, deal-attribution, and peer
reputation.

**What the interviewer watches for:** Does the candidate
say "I want to be in sales" or "I want to be in technical
work with customers"? The first framing gets routed to
the AE track. The second is the SA framing.

### Misconception 4: "SAs can't influence product"

**The misconception:** SAs are downstream of the product
team. They take what engineering ships and figure out how
to position it.

**The reality:** Sr. SAs and Principal SAs *heavily*
influence the product roadmap. They file product feedback
(constructively), participate in roadmap reviews, and
sometimes drive specific feature launches. The most
senior SAs are *better* at this than the AEs, because
they have deeper technical credibility with the product
team.

**What the interviewer watches for:** Does the candidate
talk about the SA role as "I take what engineering ships
and pitch it," or as "I help engineering understand what
customers need"? The first framing is reactive; the
second is the senior SA framing.

### Misconception 5: "SAs work alone with customers"

**The misconception:** SAs have a 1:1 relationship with
the customer. They own the deal solo.

**The reality:** SAs work in *deal teams* with AEs,
specialist SEs, customer success managers, and sometimes
proposal managers. The deal team is the unit. The SA is
the technical voice in the team, not the sole owner.

**What the interviewer watches for:** Does the candidate
talk about "I work with customers" (singular) or "I work
with *deal teams* on customer engagements" (team-based)?
The team-based framing is the senior SA framing. It also
signals the candidate will collaborate well with AEs,
which is the bar-raiser signal at most companies.

### Misconception 6: "SAs have low prestige"

**The misconception:** SAs are below the technical ladder
in prestige. The "real" engineers are on the product team.

**The reality:** SAs at hyperscalers are *highly* respected
internally. The Sr. SAs in particular are often the
best-known technical voices in the company — they speak at
conferences, they file the most-cited product feedback,
they're trusted by the most important customers. The
prestige is *different* from the IC ladder (it's
customer-facing, not internal), but it's real.

**What the interviewer watches for:** Does the candidate
frame the SA role as "less prestigious than engineering"
or as "a different kind of prestige"? The framing tells
you whether the candidate will be motivated in the role.

### Misconception 7: "SAs are a dead-end career"

**The misconception:** Once you're an SA, you can only be
an SA. There's no path back to engineering, to management,
or to anything else.

**The reality:** The SA role has 3 onward paths:
1. **Stay in the SA ladder** — Sr. SA → Principal SA →
   Distinguished SA. This is the most common path.
2. **Move to a customer-facing director role** — SA manager,
   director of SAs, VP of customer engineering. This is the
   path if you want to manage.
3. **Move to product management** — especially PM roles on
   the data/analytics/infrastructure side, where SA
   experience is highly valued. This is the path if you
   want to drive product direction.

The "back to engineering" path is harder but possible; it
usually requires a deliberate move (e.g., 1-2 years of
internal IC work to re-establish technical credibility).

**What the interviewer watches for:** Does the candidate
have a 5-year career plan? Or are they "just trying SA"?
Candidates with a plan are more likely to stay, perform,
and become senior SAs. Candidates without a plan churn
faster.

### Misconception 8: "SAs are interchangeable across companies"

**The misconception:** The SA role is the same at AWS,
GCP, Azure, Salesforce, Snowflake, Databricks, etc. A
senior SA can hop between them.

**The reality:** The SA role is *structurally* similar
across companies (the loop, the deal-team, the customer
contact), but the *specialty* differs. An AWS data-
analytics SA is not a Salesforce Sales-Cloud SA. The
product depth is in the product. A senior SA who has
spent 5 years on AWS data-and-analytics will need 6-12
months to ramp on, e.g., Databricks' lakehouse platform.

**What the interviewer watches for:** Does the candidate
have product-specific depth, or generic "SA" experience?
The product-specific depth is what gets hired.

## 2. The 3 things the misconceptions hide

Underneath the 8 misconceptions, there are 3 deeper
patterns that interviewers watch for:

1. **"What's the candidate's *self-concept*?"** The
   candidate who sees themselves as "an engineer who
   happens to work with customers" reads differently from
   the candidate who sees themselves as "a customer-
   focused technical advisor." The second is the SA
   self-concept.
2. **"Does the candidate have a *reason* to be an SA, or
   are they *settling* for an SA role?"** The candidate
   with a clear reason (e.g., "I love the customer
   contact; I'm tired of working on internal systems
   nobody sees") reads differently from the candidate who
   is "trying SA because the IC track stalled."
3. **"Would the candidate *thrive* in 5 years in the
   role?"** Most SAs who fail in the first 18 months
   failed because they were mismatched on the role's
   actual content, not because they lacked skills. The
   interview is partly a fit assessment.

The 3 patterns are what you should be signaling against,
not just the 8 individual misconceptions. If your
narrative is "I want to be a customer-facing technical
advisor for the next 5+ years, and the SA role is the
right vehicle for that," the 8 misconceptions fade into
the background.

---

## 3. The "anti-misconception" reframings

For each misconception, the senior candidate's reframe:

| Misconception | Junior reframe | Senior reframe |
|---|---|---|
| "SAs don't code" | "I'm OK with less coding" | "I want to write code in the *context* of customer outcomes — PoCs, demos, reference architectures" |
| "SAs are tired senior engineers" | "I want to try something different" | "I want to apply my technical depth to customer outcomes, where the feedback loop is business results" |
| "SAs are salespeople" | "I'm comfortable with customers" | "I want to be the technical voice in a deal team — the person the AE trusts" |
| "SAs can't influence product" | "I'm OK not influencing product" | "I want to bring customer signal back to the product team, where it shapes the roadmap" |
| "SAs work alone" | "I like working with customers" | "I want to be a force multiplier in a deal team — the technical credibility the team leans on" |
| "SAs have low prestige" | "I'm OK with the trade-off" | "I want the kind of prestige that comes from being trusted by customers, not the kind that comes from being the smartest person in the room" |
| "SAs are a dead-end" | "I'll see where it goes" | "I see the Sr. SA → Principal SA path as my 5-10 year plan" |
| "SAs are interchangeable" | "I have SA experience" | "I have deep specialty in [product area X], and I want to go deeper" |

The senior reframes are not what you *say* in the
interview — they're the *mental model* that shows up in
everything you say. You don't need to memorize the
reframes. You need to *internalize* the mental model.

---

## Try it

For each of the 8 misconceptions, write down your honest
current belief. Then write down the senior reframe. The
goal is to identify the 1-2 misconceptions where your
current belief is closest to the misconception itself.
Those are the ones to fix *before* the interview.

For example, if your honest answer to Misconception 1 ("SAs
don't code") is "yeah, I'd be OK with not coding," you
have a misalignment. The senior reframe is the one you
need to internalize before the interview, or the
interview will surface the misalignment.

Pick the 1-2 weakest refrains, write them down, and
practice saying the senior version out loud 10 times.
By the 10th repetition, the senior version will start
to feel natural instead of rehearsed.
