# Solutions Architect Track — Capstone Exercise

> Cross-module exercise · ~3-4 hours

The capstone pulls together every module of the
SA Interview Prep track. It's the *one* exercise
that, if you do it well, signals senior SA
readiness. Treat it as a mock loop, not as a
homework assignment.

The capstone has 4 parts, each tied to a module
of the track. The whole exercise should take
3-4 hours of focused work, ideally split across
2 days.

---

## The scenario

You are interviewing for a **Senior Solutions
Architect** role at a hyperscaler (AWS, GCP,
Azure, Snowflake, Databricks, or Confluent).
The loop is a 5-round interview, 60-90 minutes
per round, 4 interviewers plus a hiring manager.

Your task is to *prepare* for the loop by
producing the 4 artifacts below. The artifacts
are what you'd bring to the loop; the interview
itself is the performance.

---

## Part 1: Your 90-second self-introduction
*(Modules 01, 05)*

Write a 90-second self-introduction that you
would deliver at the start of the loop. The
introduction should include:

- **Who you are** (1 sentence: name, current role,
  years of experience).
- **What you do** (1 sentence: what you actually
  do, day-to-day).
- **Your SA narrative** (3-4 sentences: the
  arc of your SA career, the customer impact,
  the technical depth).
- **Why this role** (1-2 sentences: why this
  company, why this team, why now).
- **Close** (1 sentence: the *one* thing you want
  them to remember).

Constraints:

- 90 seconds when spoken at 130 wpm = ~195 words.
- Anchor in 1-2 specific customer outcomes
  (e.g., "saved $2M/year in overstock costs").
- Avoid generic claims ("I'm a hard worker,"
  "I love technology").
- Use the PREP framework (Module 06 Lesson 09)
  to structure the closing point.

**Deliverable:** A 195-word self-introduction,
written out in full, with a marker at every
30-second boundary.

---

## Part 2: Your 4 C's architecture answer
*(Modules 02, 03, 06)*

Pick *one* of the 3 worked examples from Module
06 Lesson 08 (real-time fraud detection,
e-commerce checkout, or self-serve insurance).
Re-tell it using the 4 C's framework (Module 06
Lesson 08) and produce the *full* architecture
diagram for it.

The architecture answer should include:

- **Customer** (2-3 sentences).
- **Context** (3-4 sentences).
- **Constraints** (4-5 specific, measurable
  constraints).
- **Choice** (5-6 sentences describing the
  architecture).
- **Diagram** — a clean, labeled architecture
  diagram with at least 6-8 services, hot-path
  annotations, and 1-2 failure-mode annotations.
  Use a tool like Excalidraw, draw.io, or
  mermaid in markdown.
- **Tradeoffs** (3-4 sentences describing the
  alternatives considered and why the Choice
  won).

Constraints:

- 90-120 seconds when spoken at 130 wpm.
- Use the whiteboard rules (Module 06 Lesson 07)
  for the diagram.
- The Choice must be anchored in the Constraints,
  not in a generic best-practice.

**Deliverable:** A 4 C's script + a labeled
architecture diagram + a 3-4 sentence tradeoff
section.

## Part 3: Your 5-story behavioral bank
*(Module 05)*

Using the story-bank worksheet from Module 05
Lesson 02, write *5* anchor stories that cover
the 5 categories:

1. **Complex problem for a customer** (technical
   depth + customer impact).
2. **Challenge working with sales** (cross-
   functional collaboration).
3. **Lost a deal** (reflection + learning).
4. **Influenced a technical direction** (leadership
   + technical judgment).
5. **Biggest pre-sales win** (customer outcome +
   commercial impact).

Each story should be written in PREP format
(Module 06 Lesson 09), with:

- **Point** (1 sentence, 15-25 words).
- **Reason** (2-3 sentences, 40-60 words).
- **Example** (4-6 sentences, 100-150 words).
- **Point** (1 sentence, 15-25 words).

Each story should also have:

- A 1-line *headline* (the title of the story).
- A 1-line *customer* (who the customer was,
  anonymized if needed).
- A 1-line *metric* (the specific outcome —
  dollar amount, latency, scale, time saved).

Constraints:

- Total length of all 5 stories: ~1,500 words.
- Each story must include at least one *specific
  number* (latency, scale, dollar amount, time
  saved). Generic claims don't count.
- Each story must anchor on a *customer*, not on
  your internal team.
- Each story must end with a *lesson* (what you
  learned, what you'd do differently).

**Deliverable:** 5 stories, each in PREP format,
with headline, customer, and metric.

## Part 4: Your 24-hour pre-interview checklist
*(Module 06)*

Using Module 06 Lesson 06 as the template,
produce a *customized* 24-hour, 12-hour, 1-hour,
and 5-minute checklist for the specific
interview you're preparing for.

The checklist should include:

- **24 hours** (12 items, customized to your
  specific loop time, format, and interviewers).
- **12 hours** (6 items, customized to your
  sleep schedule and the loop start time).
- **1 hour** (7 items, customized to your
  workspace and setup).
- **5 minutes** (5 items, customized to your
  calming routine).
- **30 minutes post** (5 items, customized to
  your post-mortem habit).

Constraints:

- Each item should be *actionable* (not "be
  calm" but "take 3 deep breaths").
- Each item should be *time-bounded* (not
  "review stories" but "review stories for 5
  minutes").
- The checklist should be *printable* — single
  page, scannable in 30 seconds.

**Deliverable:** A single-page printable
checklist, customized to a specific loop.

---

## The "interview day" simulation

Once you have the 4 artifacts, simulate the
interview day:

1. **Run the 24-hour checklist** for 1 day, even
   if you don't have a real interview. The point
   is to *practice* the routine.
2. **Deliver the 90-second self-introduction** to
   a friend or to a mirror. Time it. Get feedback.
3. **Deliver the 4 C's architecture answer** to a
   friend. Show the diagram. Get feedback.
4. **Deliver 2 of the 5 behavioral stories** in
   PREP format. Get feedback.
5. **Run a 30-minute post-mortem** on the day.
   Note 3 things that went well, 3 things to
   improve.

The simulation is the *practice*. The artifacts
are the *output*. The senior SA move is to do
both, not just one.

## How this maps to the modules

| Part | Module | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| **Part 1: Self-intro** | M01, M05 | Your SA narrative, your story bank |
| **Part 2: Architecture** | M02, M03, M06 | 4 C's, technical depth, tradeoffs, whiteboard |
| **Part 3: Stories** | M05 | PREP, customer-flavored STAR, behavioral |
| **Part 4: Checklist** | M06 | 24-hour prep, recovery moves, post-mortem |

The 4 parts cover 4 of the 6 modules. Modules 01
(introduction), 02 (customer interaction), 03
(technical), 05 (behavioral), and 06 (tips and
frameworks) all show up. Module 04 (system
design) is intentionally absent — that module
is a cross-link to the system_design track, and
its lessons are referenced from Parts 2 and 3.

---

## Try it

Set a 4-hour timer. Produce the 4 artifacts in
order:

- **Part 1** (30 min)
- **Part 2** (90 min)
- **Part 3** (90 min)
- **Part 4** (30 min)

Then run the simulation (Part 5) over 1 day.

The investment is 4 hours of focused work + 1 day
of practice. The return is the *confidence* of
having the 4 artifacts ready for any SA loop.
