# 02 — Building Your SA Story Bank

> **Lesson 2 of 11 — Behavioral for SAs** · ~20 min

The 5 story categories, the worksheet, and the 8-story
minimum. This is the highest-leverage exercise in the
module — the deliverable that turns 6 lessons of reading
into a winning behavioral round.

---

## 1. The 5 story categories

Every behavioral story falls into one of 5 categories.
A balanced story bank has 1-2 stories in each category.

| # | Category | What the story is about |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer-facing technical work** | You solved a customer problem with a technical solution. |
| 2 | **Customer-facing collaboration** | You worked across stakeholders (customer + sales + product + engineering) to land a deal or a project. |
| 3 | **Customer-facing conflict** | You disagreed with a customer (or with sales, or with product) and how you resolved it. |
| 4 | **Customer-facing failure** | You lost a deal, made a mistake with a customer, or failed to land the outcome — and what you learned. |
| 5 | **Personal growth in customer-facing work** | A specific trait, skill, or perspective that you've developed for customer-facing work. |

The 5 categories are not rigid — a single story can
span 2-3. But every story has a *primary* category, and
the story bank should be balanced across all 5.

---

## 2. The worksheet

For each of the 5 categories, write down 1-2 stories.
Use this template:

```
Story [N]: [2-5 word title]

Category: [1 of the 5]

The customer / stakeholder:
  - Name: [or "NDA, but..."]
  - Role: [their title]
  - Their company: [or anonymize: "a Fortune 500 financial-services company"]

The situation:
  - What was the context? (1-2 sentences)
  - What was the customer trying to achieve?

Your task:
  - What was your specific responsibility?
  - What outcome were you accountable for?

Your actions:
  - What did you do specifically? (Tools, decisions,
    communications)
  - Who else was involved? (Names or roles)

The result:
  - Quantified outcome: [numbers: deal size, NPS change,
    cost savings, time savings]
  - Customer outcome: [what changed for the customer]
  - Your growth: [what you learned or changed]

The transferable lesson:
  - [1-2 sentences on what's true across contexts]
```

The worksheet is the *raw material*. Once you have 8-
10 stories in the worksheet, refine each into a 90-
second version using the STAR framework (Lesson 03).

---

## 3. The story-mining exercise

If you don't have 8-10 customer-facing stories, you
need to *mine* for more. The 4-step mining exercise:

### Step 1: List every customer-facing project in the last 5 years

Go through your resume, your calendar, your Jira /
GitHub history. For each project, write:

- Was the customer named or unnamed?
- What was the customer's role (decision-maker, end-
  user, influencer)?
- What was the outcome?

If you've worked on 20+ projects in 5 years, you
should have 10-15 with *any* customer involvement.

### Step 2: Pick the 3 projects with the most material outcomes

Of the 10-15 customer-involved projects, pick the 3
with the most material outcomes. These are your
*anchor stories* — the ones you'll tell most often.

### Step 3: Reframe each as a STAR story

For each anchor project, use the worksheet template
to write a STAR story. The customer is the protagonist.

### Step 4: Branch each anchor story into 2-3 variations

Each anchor story can answer 2-3 questions. For
example:

- "Tell me about a time you solved a complex problem
  for a customer" → the original story.
- "Tell me about a time you had to learn a new
  technology quickly" → the *technical learning* part
  of the same story, focused and reframed.
- "Tell me about a time you worked across multiple
  stakeholders" → the *cross-functional* part of the
  same story, focused and reframed.

The branching is what gives you 8-10 stories from 3-4
anchor projects.

---

## 4. The "anchor story" model

The 3 anchor stories that every SA candidate should
have:

| # | Anchor story | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Big technical win.** You solved a complex technical problem for a customer that had a measurable business outcome. | "Complex problem for a customer," "biggest pre-sales win," "learned a new technology quickly," "influenced technical direction" |
| 2 | **Cross-functional collaboration.** You worked across stakeholders (sales, product, customer) to land a deal or project. | "Multiple stakeholders," "challenge working with sales," "aligned product and customer" |
| 3 | **Setback and recovery.** You lost a deal, made a mistake, or had a customer escalation — and how you recovered. | "Lost a customer," "failed with a customer," "made a technical mistake with a customer" |

The 3 anchors are the *spine*. From each, you can
derive 2-3 variations, giving you 6-9 stories total.
Add 1-2 more for personal growth and you're at 8-10.

---

## 5. The "what makes a strong story" checklist

For each story, score against this checklist:

- [ ] **Customer named (or anonymized with specificity)** —
  "a Fortune 500 retailer" not "a customer"
- [ ] **Situation is 1-2 sentences, with stakes** — why did
  it matter to the customer?
- [ ] **Task is your specific responsibility** — not
  "we were responsible for..." but "I was responsible
  for..."
- [ ] **Actions are specific** — tools, decisions,
  named collaborators
- [ ] **Result is quantified** — dollars, NPS points,
  latency reduction, time saved
- [ ] **Customer outcome is named** — what changed for
  the customer, not just for you
- [ ] **Reflection is included** — what you learned, what
  you changed, the transferable lesson
- [ ] **Story is 90-120 seconds when told out loud** —
  not 30 seconds (too thin) or 5 minutes (too long)

A story that scores 7-8 is "passing." 5-6 is "borderline."
4 or below is "failing."

---

## 6. The story-bank assembly

Once you have the stories, assemble the bank as a
single document. The format:

```markdown
# My SA Story Bank

## Anchor 1: [Title]
- **Customer:** [name or anonymized]
- **Situation:** [1-2 sentences]
- **Task:** [1 sentence]
- **Actions:** [3-5 bullets]
- **Result:** [1-2 sentences, quantified]
- **Reflection:** [1-2 sentences]
- **90-second version:** [the full 90-second answer]
- **Variations:**
  - "Tell me about a complex problem..." → [focused reframing]
  - "Tell me about a time you learned..." → [focused reframing]

## Anchor 2: [Title]
...
```

Keep this document. Before every interview loop,
re-read the story bank, time each 90-second version,
and re-tell each story out loud once.

---

## 7. The "shadow a current SA" technique

If you're a senior engineer transitioning to SA and
don't have 8-10 customer-facing stories, *shadow a
current SA*. The shadow gives you:

- Access to customer calls (where you take notes).
- Access to deal-team meetings (where you observe the
  pattern of customer-facing work).
- A potential mentor who can help you turn your
  engineering experience into customer-facing stories.

Even 2-4 weeks of shadowing can give you 2-3
*additional* customer-facing stories and enough context
to reframe your engineering stories as customer-
facing.

---

## 8. The "tell it out loud" practice

Writing the stories is necessary but not sufficient.
The stories need to be *told out loud*. The telling
process surfaces:

- Filler words you didn't know you used ("um," "so,"
  "actually").
- Moments where the story is too vague.
- Moments where you're rushed (the action needs more
  detail) or slowed (the situation needs more
  compression).
- Moments where you lose eye contact (and need to
  practice the visual delivery).

For each story, set a 90-second timer. Tell the
story out loud, alone. Listen back to a recording.
Note 3 things that went well, 3 things to improve.
Re-tell. Repeat until the story is 90-120 seconds and
lands clean.

The investment: 5-10 minutes per story × 8-10 stories
= 60-90 minutes total. The return: a passing
behavioral round.

---

## Try it

Do the worksheet for the 5 categories. Write down 1-2
stories per category. Don't filter — write everything
you remember, even rough edges.

Once you have 8-10 stories in raw form, run the "what
makes a strong story" checklist on each. Score each
story. Pick the 3 highest-scoring as your anchor
stories.

Refine each anchor story into a 90-second version.
Time yourself out loud. Adjust until each story is 90-
120 seconds.

Now you have a story bank. Save it. Re-tell each
story once a week for the 4 weeks before your
interview. By the interview, the stories will be in
muscle memory.

This is the deliverable for Module 05. By the end of
the module, the story bank should have 8-10 stories,
each with a 90-second version, ready to deploy.
