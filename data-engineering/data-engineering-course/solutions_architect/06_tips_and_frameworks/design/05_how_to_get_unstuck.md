# 05 — How To Get Unstuck

> **Lesson 5 of 9 — Tips & Frameworks** · ~8 min

When your mind goes blank in an interview, the 3
recovery moves. Why getting stuck is *normal* and the
recovery is what gets tested.

---

## 1. Why getting stuck is normal

Every candidate gets stuck in an interview. Mind
goes blank. The architecture question is harder
than expected. The behavioral story falls apart.
The objection hits a nerve.

The interviewer is *not* surprised. They expect
candidates to get stuck. What they're testing is
*how you recover*.

The 3 recovery moves below are the senior SA
patterns. The candidate who uses them calmly
signals "I can handle pressure," which is the
senior SA bar.

---

## 2. The 3 recovery moves

### Move 1: The explicit "let me think"

The most-used recovery move. When your mind goes
blank, *say so explicitly* and take a deliberate
pause.

What to say:

> "That's a great question. Let me think about
> this for a moment."

> "I want to give this the time it deserves. Can
> I take 30 seconds to think?"

> "I'm going to pause and think this through
> before I answer."

The explicit "let me think" is a senior move. It
signals:

- "I take this seriously."
- "I want to give you a thoughtful answer, not a
  fast one."
- "I'm not afraid of silence."

The pause can be 5-10 seconds. The silence is your
friend. The interviewer uses the time to *want* you
to succeed; you use the time to recover your
thoughts.

### Move 2: The structured "let me work through it"

When you know the *shape* of the answer but not the
specifics, use the structured thinking move.

What to say:

> "Let me work through this out loud. There are 3
> parts to consider: [X, Y, Z]. Let me start with
> [X]."

> "I'm going to think about this in 3 stages: the
> constraint, the candidate choices, and the
> tradeoff. The constraint is..."

The structured thinking move is the *verbal
whiteboard*. You're not asking the interviewer to
wait in silence; you're inviting them into your
thinking process. Most interviewers respond to
this with engagement, not impatience.

### Move 3: The "I don't know, but here's how I'd
find out"

When you genuinely don't know the answer, the
senior SA move is honesty. Don't fake it; don't
guess. Say so, and name how you'd find the answer.

What to say:

> "I don't know the specific answer off the top of
> my head. If I had to find out, I'd [look at the
> docs, ask the product team, run a benchmark]. I
> can come back to you on this within 24 hours."

> "I'm not sure about that specific case. My
> instinct is [X], but I'd want to validate before
> committing. What I'd do is [specific validation
> step]."

The "I don't know" move is the *highest-trust*
move. The interviewer reads it as "this person
won't bullshit me, and they have a model of how to
find answers." Most candidates *try* to fake
answers; the senior SA move is to admit the gap
and propose the recovery.

---

## 3. The 3 anti-patterns for getting unstuck

### Anti-pattern 1: Waffling

You don't know the answer, so you talk for 60
seconds without saying anything specific. The
interviewer reads this as "I'm making it up."

**Fix:** Use the explicit "let me think" move
first. If 30 seconds of thinking doesn't help,
use the "I don't know" move. Don't waffle.

### Anti-pattern 2: Defensive

You get stuck, and the interviewer pushes for an
answer. You get defensive, raise your voice, or
become combative.

**Fix:** Use the "let me work through it" move.
The structured thinking de-escalates because it
shows you're engaged, not defensive.

### Anti-pattern 3: Surrender

You get stuck, and you just say "I don't know"
and stop. The interviewer reads this as "I give
up."

**Fix:** The "I don't know" move must include
*how you'd find out*. The recovery is part of the
move, not a separate thing.

---

## 4. The "pre-recovery" routine

In the 24 hours before an interview, practice the
3 recovery moves. The practice is what makes them
muscle memory.

The pre-recovery routine:

1. **Have a recovery script ready.** Write down the
   3 moves verbatim. Have the script visible during
   the interview.
2. **Practice each move out loud, alone.** Say the
   lines; feel the pause. The practice makes the
   moves feel natural.
3. **Use one in your next mock.** Pick a moment in
   a mock where you get stuck, and use one of the
   3 moves. The in-mock practice is the highest-
   leverage.

The pre-recovery routine is the *insurance policy*
for the interview. The recovery moves are *normal*;
practicing them is what signals senior SA.

---

## 5. The 3 places you get stuck most

The 3 places candidates get stuck most in SA
interviews:

1. **Architecture design.** "I'm not sure which
   service to choose for this." Use the
   "let me work through it" move — name the
   constraint, the candidate choices, the tradeoff.
2. **Behavioral story detail.** "I can't remember
   the specific numbers." Use the "let me think"
   move — take 5-10 seconds, then give the
   *approximate* number with appropriate hedging.
3. **Customer objection.** "The customer just pushed
   back hard and I don't have a good answer." Use
   the "I don't know, but here's how I'd find out"
   move — admit the gap, propose the recovery.

The 3 places are the predictable stuck-points. The
recovery move for each is also predictable. The
candidate who has practiced the recovery moves for
each place is the candidate who passes the round.

---

## 6. The "interviewer as recovery partner" reframe

The senior SA reframe: the interviewer is your
*recovery partner*, not your judge. They *want*
you to succeed (most of them). The recovery move
is a way of *inviting* them into your thinking.

When you use the "let me work through it" move,
the interviewer often responds with:

- "Yeah, that's the right framing..."
- "I'd add the constraint of X..."
- "Let me know if you want me to clarify..."

The interviewer's response is *help*, not
judgment. The recovery move is what unlocks the
help.

---

## Try it

For each of the 3 places (architecture, behavioral,
objection), write down the recovery move you'd use.
Have the script visible during your next interview.

Then, in your next 2 mocks, deliberately get stuck
on one question and use the recovery move. The
in-mock practice is the highest-leverage. By the
2nd mock, the recovery move will feel natural.

The investment is 30 minutes of prep. The return
is the comfort of knowing you have a recovery
script for the moments when your mind goes blank.
