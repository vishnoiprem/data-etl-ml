# 06 — What To Do Before an Interview

> **Lesson 6 of 9 — Tips & Frameworks** · ~10 min

The 24-hour checklist. The 12 hours before, the 1
hour before, and the 5 minutes before. The most-
underutilized prep you can do.

---

## 1. The 24 hours before

The 24 hours before an interview are *the* prep
window. Most candidates waste them on social media
or general anxiety. The senior SA move is to use
the 24 hours for *targeted* prep.

### The 24-hour checklist

- [ ] **Re-read your story bank.** Skim the 8-10
  STAR stories from Module 05. Don't re-write;
  just re-read. The re-reading primes the
  stories.
- [ ] **Re-read the 4 C's and PREP frameworks.**
  (Lessons 08 and 09 of this module.) The
  frameworks are the *structure* of your answers;
  re-read them to keep them top of mind.
- [ ] **Re-read the 4 customer-interaction mocks.**
  (Module 02, lessons 06-09.) The 4 mocks
  internalize the *patterns* of customer-
  facing conversations.
- [ ] **Practice 2 stories out loud.** Pick 2
  stories from your bank and tell them out loud
  in front of a mirror or to a friend. The
  out-loud practice is the highest-leverage 5
  minutes you can spend.
- [ ] **Re-read the company-specific research.**
  (Recent blog posts, product launches, customer
  case studies.) The 30-60 minutes of company
  research is the *signal* that differentiates
  you from other candidates.
- [ ] **Prepare 3 reverse-interview questions.**
  The 3 questions you'll ask at the end of the
  loop. (See Lesson 02's framework.)
- [ ] **Confirm logistics.** Interview time, dial-in
  info, video link, recruiter contact, backup
  contact. The logistics matter more than you
  think.
- [ ] **Pick your outfit.** If it's a video call,
  pick the *full* outfit (in case you need to
  stand up). If it's in-person, pick the
  *comfortable* one.
- [ ] **Confirm your reference list.** 3-5 former
  managers / peers who can speak to your
  customer-facing work. Have their contact
  info ready.
- [ ] **Set up your workspace.** Quiet room, water,
  notes, time visible. See the phone interview
  checklist in Lesson 04.
- [ ] **Set a wake-up alarm.** With buffer. The
  worst interview day starts with a rushed
  morning.
- [ ] **Plan your meals.** Don't skip meals; don't
  eat a heavy meal right before. Both are
  avoidable energy problems.

The 12-item checklist is the *minimum*. The
investment is 60-90 minutes. The return is the
*calm* of having nothing left to do at the
interview itself.

## 2. The 12 hours before

The 12 hours before the interview, focus on
*recovery*, not more prep.

### The 12-hour checklist

- [ ] **Stop prep by 6 hours before bed.** The
  last 6 hours before sleep are for *decompress*,
  not study. Studying right before sleep means
  restless sleep and tired interview.
- [ ] **No caffeine after 4pm.** If your interview
  is in the morning, the 4pm cutoff prevents
  restless sleep. If your interview is in the
  afternoon, the 12pm cutoff is even better.
- [ ] **No screens in the last 30 minutes before
  bed.** Blue light disrupts sleep. Read a
  physical book instead.
- [ ] **Set 2 alarms.** Backup alarm in case the
  first one fails.
- [ ] **Lay out your outfit, your notes, your
  water, your snacks.** The morning should be
  *easy*, not *decision-laden*.
- [ ] **Plan your commute.** If in-person, do a
  dry-run. If video, test your connection.

The 6 items in the 12-hour checklist are about
*recovery*. The senior SA move is to recognize
that your best prep the night before is *sleep*,
not more study.

---

## 3. The 1 hour before

The 1 hour before the interview is for *settling*,
not more prep.

### The 1-hour checklist

- [ ] **Light breakfast / lunch.** Protein, complex
  carbs, water. Avoid heavy / sugary foods.
- [ ] **Skim your story bank one more time.** 5
  minutes, max. Don't re-read; just skim.
- [ ] **Re-read the company-specific research.**
  5 minutes, max. Just the most recent 1-2 things.
- [ ] **Test your setup.** Video link works, audio
  works, slides / whiteboard work. 5-10 minutes.
- [ ] **Take a 5-minute walk.** Outside if
  possible. The walk clears the head.
- [ ] **Take 3 deep breaths.** Slow, deliberate.
  Signals to your body that you're calm.
- [ ] **Put away all distractions.** Phone on
  silent, no email, no Slack. The next 60
  minutes are for the interview.

The 7 items in the 1-hour checklist are about
*settling*. The interview is the performance;
the 1 hour before is the warmup.

---

## 4. The 5 minutes before

The 5 minutes before the interview is for
*centering*, not more prep.

### The 5-minute checklist

- [ ] **Re-read the 4 C's framework.** (Lesson 08.)
  Just the headline. The 4 C's are the structure
  of any architecture answer.
- [ ] **Re-read the PREP framework.** (Lesson 09.)
  Just the headline. PREP is the structure of any
  behavioral answer.
- [ ] **Re-read your 3 reverse-interview questions.**
  1 minute, max.
- [ ] **Take 3 more deep breaths.**
- [ ] **Smile.** It sounds silly; it works. The
  smile signals to your face that you're calm,
  which signals to your body, which signals to
  your voice.

The 5 items in the 5-minute checklist are the
*last touch* before the interview starts. They're
not for learning new things; they're for
re-confirming the things you already know.

## 5. The "interview day" schedule (template)

A worked template for a 9am video interview:

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| **6:30pm (night before)** | Stop prep. Review checklist. Lay out outfit. |
| **9:00pm** | No screens. Read a book. |
| **10:00pm** | Sleep. |
| **6:30am** | Wake. 30 min buffer. |
| **7:00am** | Light breakfast (oatmeal, fruit, water). |
| **7:30am** | 5 min walk outside. |
| **7:45am** | Test video / audio / slides. |
| **8:00am** | Skim story bank (5 min). |
| **8:15am** | Skim company research (5 min). |
| **8:30am** | 3 deep breaths. |
| **8:45am** | Re-read 4 C's + PREP (2 min). Re-read reverse-interview questions (1 min). |
| **8:50am** | Final deep breaths. Smile. |
| **8:55am** | Join the video link 5 min early. |
| **9:00am** | Interview starts. |

The schedule is 24 hours of *targeted* prep. The
senior SA move is to use the 24 hours for *recovery*
and *centering*, not for last-minute study.

## 6. The "post-interview" routine

The 30 minutes after the interview is for
*post-mortem*, not for the next interview (yet).

### The 30-minute checklist

- [ ] **Write down 3 things that went well.**
  Be specific.
- [ ] **Write down 3 things to improve.** Be
  specific.
- [ ] **Write down 1 thing to practice before the
  next round.**
- [ ] **Send the per-round follow-up email.**
  (See Module 02 Lesson 12.)
- [ ] **Take a 10-minute break.** Don't jump
  straight to the next thing.

The 5 items in the 30-minute checklist are about
*closing the loop*. The post-mortem converts the
interview into a learning; the follow-up email
converts the interview into a relationship.

---

## Try it

For your next interview, build the 24-hour, 12-
hour, 1-hour, 5-minute, and 30-minute checklists
*in advance*. Customize the templates above to your
specific interview time and format.

The investment is 30-60 minutes of *pre-prep*. The
return is the calm of having nothing left to do
during the interview itself.

The senior SA move is to recognize that the
interview is *only 5%* of your prep. The 95% is the
story bank, the framework, the practice. The
checklist is what closes the loop.
