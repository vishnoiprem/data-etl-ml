# Engineering Management Introduction — A Practical Guide for Engineers

> **6 lessons · 2 videos · ~3 hours of focused reading**
>
> **Author:** [Prem Vishnoi](https://medium.com/@premvishnoi) · <prem.vishnoi@example.com>
>
> **Companion articles:** [medium.com/@premvishnoi](https://medium.com/@premvishnoi)

A short, prose-only primer for engineers who are **thinking about
becoming an engineering manager** — or who have been told they
*should* become one and want to know what they're actually signing
up for.

The engineering-management interview is the interview candidates
prep for the least. Most engineers prepare for coding, then system
design, then maybe a behavioral — and treat "the EM loop" as
"behavioral but with manager questions." It isn't. The EM loop
tests a different set of muscles: people judgment, scope, and
*the trade-off you made by leaving the IC ladder*.

This track is the on-ramp. It's six lessons and a capstone. By
the end of it, you'll know whether management is for you, what
the interview actually looks like, and how to start the internal
pitch if you decide to make the move.

---

## What's in this track

**Prose-only.** No code, no service to run, no system to design.
Every lesson is a Markdown file you read, think about, and — most
importantly — *discuss with someone*. The capstone is a writing
exercise. The deliverables are stories, not artifacts.

**Decision-focused.** The whole point of the track is to help you
make a single decision: *"Should I become an engineering
manager?"* The lessons are organized so that, by Lesson 6, you
have a clear answer — yes, no, or "I need 6 more months of
evidence."

**Calibrated for the senior+ IC who's considering the fork.** The
audience is engineers at **E4/E5 (Meta), L5/L6 (Google),
Senior/Staff (most), Staff/Principal (Netflix)** who are getting
pulled toward management by their managers, their skip-levels, or
their own restlessness. Junior candidates are welcome, but the
framing is "you're a strong IC being asked to consider the
manager ladder."

## How to use this track

**Weekend 1 — Orientation (2-3 hours).** Read the track
front-to-back in one sitting. Don't take notes; let the
mental model build. By Sunday night you should know whether
you want to keep reading deeper resources (the books in
Lesson 05's glossary) or whether you've already decided
this isn't for you.

**Week 1 — Self-assessment (3-4 hours).** Go back to Lesson 03
with a pen. Score yourself honestly on the 4 "ready" signs and
the 3 "not ready" signs. Talk to your manager about the
"test drive" idea. If you don't currently have a manager
who'd let you test-drive, Lesson 06 has the 4 alternative
archetypes worth considering.

**Week 2 — Decision and pitch (2-3 hours).** Write your
"why EM" story (the capstone in `exercise.md`). Tell it to
a friend, a peer, your manager. The story is the deliverable
— it's what you bring to the conversation when you say
"I want to make the move."

**Week 3 — Interview prep (only if you've decided).** If you
land on "yes, I want to interview for EM roles," go to
`behavioral_interviews/04_mock_interviews_and_analyses/04_mock_em.md`
for the worked mock interview, and back to this track's
Lesson 04 for the loop structure.

## A note on the prose-only format

There is intentionally **no coding exercise, no system design,
no mock rubric** in this track. Engineering management is a
*practice* — like medicine, like teaching, like sales. You can't
unit-test whether you should become a manager. You can only
*decide*, then *try*, then *iterate*.

So: **read the lesson. Do the "Try it" out loud, ideally with
another person. Write down what you learned.** That's the whole
workflow.
