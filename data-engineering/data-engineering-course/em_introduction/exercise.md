# Capstone Exercise — Your "Why EM" Story

> **End-of-track capstone** · ~2 hours

The single deliverable for the EM Introduction track: a
90-second "why I want to be an engineering manager" story
that you can tell in 90 seconds, that sounds like you, and
that you'd be willing to say to your current manager.

This is the *only* artifact in the track. There's no code,
no system, no rubric. There's a story.

---

## Why this is the deliverable

The decision to become an engineering manager is, in
practice, made in a 90-second conversation. Either:

- **Your manager asks "would you consider the EM track?"**
  and you answer in 90 seconds.
- **You pitch the move** (using Lesson 03's script) in a
  5-minute conversation, but the *core* of the pitch is
  the 90-second "why."
- **You interview for an EM role** and the hiring manager
  asks "why management, why now" — your 90-second answer
  is the one that lands.

The story is the deliverable. Everything else in the track
exists to make sure that when you tell it, you mean it.

---

## The 4-part structure

Your "why EM" story has 4 parts, in order. Total length:
**90 seconds, 200-250 words.**

| Part | Length | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The hook | 15 sec | The work, not the title. "I want to manage because [specific work] is what I find most energizing." |
| 2. The evidence | 30 sec | 2-3 specific things you've done in the last 12 months that were management-shaped. |
| 3. The "why now" | 20 sec | What you want to do as a manager that you can't do as an IC. |
| 4. The ask | 25 sec | What you want from the listener: a conversation, a nomination, an interview, or a 6-month development plan. |

The story is *not* a justification. It's a clean
explanation of a decision you've already made (or are
making). The listener should leave the 90 seconds thinking
"this person has thought about it."

---

## Worked example — Priya, 7 years DE, L6 at a fintech

> *"I want to be an engineering manager, and the reason is
> the work, not the title.*
>
> *Over the last 12 months, I've been doing more
> management-shaped work than IC work. I mentored Maya
> through her promotion to Senior. I owned the data-quality
> initiative that touched 4 teams and 14 services. I ran
> last quarter's hiring loop for the staff DE req. The
> work I find most energizing is the 1:1s with my mentees,
> the cross-team coordination, and the writing — the perf
> packets, the strategy docs, the design-review feedback
> that helps someone level up.*
>
> *What I want to do as a manager that I can't do as an
> IC is multiply. Right now, my leverage is my own output.
> As a manager, my leverage would be 5-8 people — and
> based on what I've seen with my mentees, that's 3-5x
> more than I could ship alone.*
>
> *I'd like your help in two ways: first, your nomination
> when an EM req opens on the team. Second, a 6-month
> development plan — more 1:1 practice, a perf-cycle
> observation, and ideally a hiring loop this quarter.*
>
> *If the timing doesn't work, I want to stay on the IC
> ladder and aim for Staff. But I wanted to put it on your
> radar now so we can plan for it."*

**Word count:** 226. **Time at speaking pace:** ~95 seconds.
**Tone:** specific, not aspirational. **Evidence:**
3 specific examples. **Ask:** 2 specific things.

---

## Your turn

Set a 30-minute timer. Write your own story, using the
4-part structure above. Don't revise. First draft.

When the timer goes off, time yourself out loud. The story
should be 80-100 seconds. If it's under 60, you cut too
much. If it's over 120, you cut too little.

Then read it back to yourself and ask:

1. **Does the hook name the work, not the title?** If
   you said "I want to be a manager because it's a
   promotion," rewrite it.
2. **Are the 2-3 examples specific?** If you said
   "I mentored a junior engineer," rewrite it. Names,
   dates, what they did after.
3. **Does the "why now" make the trade-off explicit?**
   "I want to multiply my output" is fine. "I want
   more impact" is not.
4. **Is the ask specific?** "I want to explore the
   path" is not an ask. "I want your nomination for
   the next EM req" is.

If any of the 4 are weak, revise that part. Keep the
others.

---

## The "tell it to 3 people" rule

Once your draft is at 90 seconds, tell it to 3 people
in this order:

1. **A peer engineer** (not your manager). Get their
   reaction. Does it sound like you? Does it sound like
   LinkedIn?
2. **A manager you respect** (not your current one). Get
   their reaction. Does the evidence land? Is the ask
   specific enough?
3. **Your current manager.** This is the actual pitch.
   Use the script in Lesson 03 as a backup if the
   conversation goes off-script.

After each telling, write down one specific thing to
improve. By the third telling, the story should feel
*easy* — not rehearsed, but clean.

---

## The "what if I've decided *not* to" version

If after Lesson 03's self-check you've decided management
isn't for you, write the *opposite* story: "Why I want to
stay on the IC ladder." The 4-part structure is the same,
but the ask is "I'd like to be considered for the next
Staff req" instead of an EM req.

This is just as important. The track is decision-focused,
not "go become a manager"-focused. The honest "no" is a
great outcome.

---

## Submission

There's no formal submission — this exercise is for you.
But if you want feedback:

- **Tell it to a friend** and have them score you on the
  4 rubric dimensions in Lesson 04 (people judgment,
  conflict, growth, scope, communication). The story is
  a behavioral answer; the rubric applies.
- **Record yourself** and listen back. The 90-second mark
  should be at the end of your ask, not the middle.
- **Save the story** as `why_em_story.md` in your personal
  notes. You'll re-use it in 12 months when you re-evaluate.

## What to do after

- **If you decided yes** → Read Lesson 04 again, then go to
  `behavioral_interviews/04_mock_interviews_and_analyses/04_mock_em.md`
  for a full worked mock. Mine 10-15 people stories using
  `behavioral_interviews/03_tactics/01_story_bank.md`.
- **If you decided no** → Read Lesson 06's "4 archetypes"
  section again. Pick the archetype that fits you and
  build a 12-month plan to be ready for the next Staff
  req.
- **If you decided "not yet"** → Pick *one* of the test
  drives in Lesson 03, ask your manager, and revisit this
  exercise in 90 days.

All three paths are good outcomes. The exercise is the
deliverable. The decision is yours.
