## What this lecture covers

- The 3 concepts every IAM user must understand: **authentication** (who are you?), **authorization** (what can you do?), and **identities** (the entities that act on your behalf).

## Narration

> "IAM stands for Identity and Access Management. It's the AWS service that answers two questions: who are you, and what are you allowed to do? Authentication answers the first: you prove your identity with a username + password, or an access key + secret, or an MFA token. Authorization answers the second: once we know who you are, IAM checks your policies to see what you can do. The 3 identity types are: IAM users (a person or service that has long-term credentials), IAM roles (an identity that can be *assumed* by a trusted principal, like an AWS service), and IAM groups (a collection of users that share policies). For Glue, you'll mostly deal with *roles* — every Glue Job runs as a role that Glue assumes on your behalf."

## Key concepts

- **Authentication** = proving you are who you say you are. Methods: password, access key, MFA.
- **Authorization** = what you can do once authenticated. Defined by policies.
- **Identities** = users, groups, roles. The "actors" in IAM.
- **Policies** = JSON documents that grant or deny permissions. Attached to identities or resources.
- **Trust policy** (roles only) = the policy that says *who* can assume the role. Roles have 2 policies: trust + identity.
- **Resource-based policy** = a policy attached to a resource (e.g., an S3 bucket policy) that says who can access *that* resource.
