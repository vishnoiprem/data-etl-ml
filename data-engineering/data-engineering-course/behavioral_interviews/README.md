# Behavioral Interviews — A Practical Guide for Engineers

> **31 lessons · 19 videos · ~12 hours of focused practice**
>
> **Author:** [Prem Vishnoi](https://medium.com/@premvishnoi) · <prem.vishnoi@example.com>
>
> **Companion articles:** [medium.com/@premvishnoi](https://medium.com/@premvishnoi)

A practical, prose-only guide to the part of the engineering interview
loop that candidates underestimate the most: **the behavioral round**.

System design tests how you think about systems. Coding tests how you
think about code. **Behavioral tests how you think about people,
priorities, and yourself under pressure.** It's the round where
candidates at the senior+ bar get downleveled — not because they can't
design a key-value store, but because they can't tell a clean 2-minute
story about a hard decision they made two years ago.

This track fixes that.

---

## What's in this track

**Prose-only.** There is no code, no service to run, no test to pass.
Every lesson is a Markdown file you read, think about, and — most
importantly — *do the exercises in*. The exercises are the part that
moves the needle. Reading alone doesn't.

**Pattern-based.** Every lesson is built around patterns that repeat
across companies: Meta, Google, Amazon, Netflix, Stripe, Airbnb, the
big banks, the AI labs. Once you see the pattern, you stop being
surprised by the questions.

**Calibrated for senior+.** The audience is engineers interviewing for
**E4/E5 (Meta), L5/L6 (Google), Senior/Staff (most), Staff/Principal
(Netflix), and EM (M5)**. Junior candidates will still get value, but
the framing is "be seen as a senior engineer" — see Module 01.

## How to use this track

**Week 1 — Fast Track (4-6 hours).** Read Module 01 in one sitting. Do
the self-check at the end of each lesson. After Lesson 7, take a real
mock interview with a friend and compare to the analysis in Lesson 8.
You'll know exactly where you stand by Sunday.

**Week 2 — Theory + Story Bank (8-10 hours).** Work through Module 02
in order, then start Module 03 by mining your career for 10-15 real
stories (use the Workshop in `05_workshops/01_story_mining.md` as your
framework). Write each one down. Tell each one out loud.

**Week 3 — Practice (4-6 hours).** Read the intros in Module 04, then
*do* the mock interviews. Out loud. Recorded. With a friend. Compare
your delivery to the analysis. Iterate.

**Week 4 — Refinement (2-3 hours).** Module 05. Quantify your impact.
Tighten delivery. Build your reverse-interview question list. Read two
of the books.

By week 4, you should be able to answer any of the 50 most-asked
behavioral questions with a clean 90-120 second story that has
specific impact numbers, no blame, and a clear "so what."

## A note on the prose-only format

There is intentionally **no code, no `app.py`, no `pytest`** in this
track. Behavioral interviewing is a *craft* — like writing, like
public-speaking, like sales. You can't run a unit test on whether your
story lands. You can only practice, get feedback, iterate.

So: **read the lesson. Do the "Try it" section out loud. Get a
friend to listen. Repeat.** That's the entire workflow.
