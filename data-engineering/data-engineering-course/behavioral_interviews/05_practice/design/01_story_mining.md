# 01 — Workshop: Story Mining

> **Lesson 1 of 5 — Workshops** · ~20 min

A 3-pass process for mining 10-15 STAR stories from your career,
with worked examples. Block out 2-3 hours. This workshop
produces the artifact (your story bank) that the rest of the
course assumes you have.

---

## 1. The setup

You need:
- A blank document (or a spreadsheet)
- 2-3 hours of uninterrupted time
- Your resume (for jogging your memory)
- A timer

The output:
- 30-50 raw story candidates
- 10-15 polished stories, written out, in your bank

---

## 2. Pass 1: The brain dump (60-90 min)

Set a 60-minute timer. Open a blank document titled "CAREER
STORIES." Write without stopping for the full 60 minutes.

For every project, role, decision, conflict, success, or failure
that comes to mind, write 1 line:

- 1-line description
- Year(s) and company
- The specific decision I made
- The outcome (with a number if possible)

Don't filter. Don't edit. Don't worry about whether the story
is "good" or "senior enough" or "impressive enough." The point
is raw material. You can curate later.

**If you get stuck after 20 minutes** (which is common),
re-read your resume and use it as a prompt. For each role
listed, ask: *"What was the most interesting thing that
happened in this role?"* Write the first thing that comes to
mind, even if it's small.

**If you get stuck at the 30-minute mark**, switch to a
*category prompt* approach. For each of these categories,
brainstorm 2-3 stories:

- A time I led a project
- A time I disagreed with someone
- A time I failed
- A time I made a hard call with incomplete info
- A time I learned a new technology fast
- A time I mentored or was mentored
- A time I went above and beyond
- A time I had to push back on my manager
- A time I had to deliver bad news
- A time I had to deal with a low performer
- A time I shipped something I'm proud of
- A time I had to learn a hard lesson
- A time I had to give hard feedback
- A time I had to receive hard feedback
- A time I had to negotiate scope

By minute 60, you should have 30-50 items.

---

## 3. Pass 2: Categorize and tag (60-90 min)

Take the brain dump. For each item, tag it with:

| Tag | Values |
|---|---|
| **Category** | leadership / conflict / failure / ambiguity / technical / ownership / helping |
| **Senior level** | E4 / E5 / E6 / E7 — your gut for what level it demonstrates |
| **Number** | yes / no |
| **Quality** | 1-5 |

The output is a 2-axis matrix. A worked example for a
hypothetical candidate:

```
              E5     E6    E7
Leadership   [3]    [1]   [0]
Conflict     [2]    [1]   [0]
Failure      [2]    [0]   [0]
Ambiguity    [3]    [1]   [0]
Tech depth   [4]    [2]   [1]
Ownership    [2]    [0]   [0]
Helping      [2]    [0]   [0]
```

Read the matrix. The cells with 0 or 1 are your **prep
gaps**. Spend the rest of this pass brainstorming for the
gaps specifically.

---

## 4. Pass 3: Write the top 10-15 (90-120 min)

Pick the 10-15 stories that have:
- The highest quality score
- The best category coverage (fills gaps)
- At least one number
- The clearest decision

For each, write the **spoken** version — 90-120 seconds, in
first person, in present tense where possible. Use the STAR /
CAR / PAR / SOAR template from `02_theory/01_star_method.md` as
the structure.

The format:

```
TITLE: [One-line name]
CATEGORY: [Leadership / Conflict / etc]
QUESTION(S) IT ANSWERS: [2-3 specific questions]
RUBRIC ROW: [Ownership / Judgment / etc]
THE STORY (90-120 sec spoken):
[Write it out as you'd say it]
THE NUMBER: [1-2 anchoring numbers]
THE TAKEAWAY: [One sentence, the beat you end on]
FOLLOW-UP I'LL PROBE FIRST: [The follow-up I want them to ask]
```

---

## 5. Worked example: a real brain-dump item

**Raw item (from Pass 1):**

> "Q3 2024 — had to migrate analytics pipeline to streaming.
> Pm was hard. Schema stuff with data sci team. Took 4
> months. Sub-minute data."

**Categorized (Pass 2):**

- Category: leadership
- Senior level: E5
- Number: yes (4 months, sub-minute)
- Quality: 4

**Polished (Pass 3):**

```
TITLE: Streaming migration with data-science schema
CATEGORY: Leadership
QUESTION(S) IT ANSWERS: "Influence without authority,"
  "Tell me about a hard project," "Tell me about a
  cross-team alignment"
RUBRIC ROW: Influence, Collaboration, Ownership
THE STORY (90-120 sec spoken):
  "Last year I led the migration of our analytics
  pipeline from nightly batches to streaming. The hard
  part wasn't technical — it was that the data science
  team was blocking the migration because their
  downstream models would break under the new schema.
  
  I spent 2 weeks just listening to them — what they
  needed, what would break, what was non-negotiable.
  I came back with a proposal: a backward-compatible
  schema with a 6-month deprecation window, plus me as
  the point of contact for any breakage.
  
  We shipped 4 months later. Data freshness went from
  24 hours to sub-minute. The data science team's
  models didn't break. The pattern is now the default
  for the next 2 schema changes on the team."
THE NUMBER: 24h → sub-minute; 0 downstream breakage
THE TAKEAWAY: "The pattern is now the default for the
  next 2 schema changes on the team."
FOLLOW-UP I'LL PROBE FIRST: "Tell me more about the
  backward-compat schema — what was the design?"
```

---

## 6. What to do with the gaps

If you have a category with 0 stories (e.g. no conflict
story), you have 3 options:

1. **Brainstorm harder.** Most candidates have 2-3 conflict
   stories buried in their career that they don't remember
   until prompted. Use the category prompts in Pass 1.

2. **Use a smaller story.** A "small" conflict (a 1:1
   disagreement with a peer) is fine. The story doesn't have
   to be dramatic; it has to be *specific* and *honest*.

3. **Use a different question category.** If you have no
   conflict stories, you can sometimes answer a conflict
   question with a *feedback* story or a *disagreement-with-
   manager* story, which are softer variants.

If you genuinely cannot find a story for a category after
2 hours of mining, that's information. It may mean your
career hasn't had that kind of moment yet, or it may mean
you need to broaden your definition of "story." Most
candidates, after 2 hours of mining, find they have more
material than they thought.

---

## 7. The maintenance pass

The story bank isn't a static artifact. After every
interview, spend 15 minutes updating it:

- Stories that landed → note what worked
- Stories that didn't land → rewrite or retire
- Questions you didn't expect → brainstorm stories that
  would answer them
- New stories you remembered → add them

After 5-6 interviews, your bank will be substantially
better than it was at the start. The compounding is real.

---

## Try it

Set a 60-minute timer. Do Pass 1 right now. Don't
overthink it — just write for the full 60 minutes.

When you're done, take a 10-minute break, then do Pass 2
(30 min) and Pass 3 (60 min) on the same day if you have
the energy, or split them across 2 days if you don't.

By the end you'll have a story bank you can take into
any interview.
