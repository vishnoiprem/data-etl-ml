The book list, the practice communities, the mock-interview

## 1. The book list

There are 8 books worth reading on behavioral interviewing
and the surrounding craft. Read in this order. Skip any
that don't resonate.

### Tier 1: The must-reads (3 books)

These three are the most-leveraged. Read all three.

1. **"Cracking the PM Interview" by Gayle McDowell & Jackie
   Bavaro** — despite the title, ~40% of this book is
   directly applicable to senior IC behavioral interviews.
   The "leadership principles" chapter and the "tell me
   about yourself" chapter are the best in the genre. The
   PM-specific parts can be skimmed.

2. **"The Manager's Path" by Camille Fournier** — even if
   you're an IC, this book teaches the *vocabulary* of
   engineering leadership, which is the vocabulary of
   behavioral interviews. Read it for the language of
   alignment, scope, and growth.

3. **"An Elegant Puzzle: Systems of Engineering
   Management" by Will Larson** — this is the single best
   book on the *practice* of being a senior engineer.
   The essays on "pull" vs "push" and on hiring are
   directly applicable to behavioral interviews because
   they teach the senior mindset the interviews are
   testing for.

### Tier 2: The should-reads (3 books)

Read these if you have time. All three deepen your
understanding of specific dimensions.

4. **"Designing Data-Intensive Applications" by Martin
   Kleppmann** — not a behavioral book, but the *single
   best* book for technical-depth stories in data
   engineering. Read it to be able to talk fluently about
   the tradeoffs in any data system. (If you've read it
   already, you know.)

5. **"Thinking in Systems" by Donella Meadows** — the
   single best book on second-order consequences, which
   is the staff/principal differentiator. Read this and
   your "unintended consequences" stories will become
   much sharper.

6. **"The Fearless Organization" by Amy Edmondson** —
   the definitive book on psychological safety in
   engineering teams. The language in this book
   ("speak up", "failure-preventing", "failure-
   facilitating") is the language of the best
   behavioral answers about team dynamics.

### Tier 3: The deep cuts (2 books)

Read these if you want to go really deep.

7. **"The Leadership Challenge" by Kouzes & Posner** —
   the canonical book on leadership *behaviors* (not
   traits). Useful for Staff+ candidates who need a
   framework for the leadership stories.

8. **"Range" by David Epstein** — a contrarian book on
   why generalists outperform specialists in complex
   domains. Useful for the *career* narrative, not the
   specific stories. Helps with the "tell me about
   yourself" answer at the senior+ level.

## 2. The practice communities

Behavioral interviewing is a *motor skill*. You need to
practice. These are the communities where senior engineers
practice together.

### Online communities

- **interviewing.io** — anonymous mock interviews with
  senior engineers from top companies. The most
  effective paid service for behavioral practice.
  Roughly $200-400 per session. Worth it for 2-3
  sessions before your real loop.

- **Pramp** — free peer mock interviews. The quality
  varies because the partners are random, but the
  practice itself is valuable. Best for early-stage
  prep, less useful right before the real loop.

- **IGotAnOffer** — paid service with structured prep
  and mock interviews. More expensive than
  interviewing.io but more structured.

### Local communities

- **Local meetups** — most cities have a "tech
  interview prep" meetup or a "women in tech"
  meetup that runs mock interview groups. Search
  Meetup.com for your city. The in-person practice
  is significantly better than online.

- **Friends** — the cheapest and most effective
  practice. 3 friends × 2 mock interviews each is
  6 practice rounds. Pick friends who are *more
  senior* than you, ideally at the level you're
  targeting.

### Internal communities

- **Your company's interview prep groups** — many
  larger companies have internal Slack channels or
  ERGs (employee resource groups) that run mock
  interviews. If your company has one, use it.

## 3. The mock-interview services

If you want professional coaching, these are the
options, roughly in order of cost:

| Service | Cost | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Friends** | Free | General practice, low-pressure |
| **interviewing.io** | ~$300/session | Real senior interviewers, anonymous |
| **IGotAnOffer** | ~$500-1500 | Structured programs with coaching |
| **Pathrise** | % of salary | Full-job-search support, more than just interviews |
| **Private coaches** | ~$300-500/hr | Personalized 1:1 coaching, often ex-interviewers from top companies |

A 4-session package with interviewing.io or a private
coach is usually enough for behavioral rounds. Save
the rest of your prep budget for system design and
coding.

## 4. The long-term plan

Behavioral interviewing is a skill you'll use for the
*next 20 years*. The investment compounds. Here's the
long-term plan:

### Year 1: Get good

- Build a 10-15 story story bank (this course)
- Do 3-5 mock interviews with friends
- Do 2-3 real interview loops (whether or not you
  want the job)
- Read at least 3 of the books in the Tier 1/2 list

### Year 2: Get calibrated

- Run mock interviews for *other* people
- Watch how senior interviewers score answers
- Notice the patterns in what gets a 4/4 and what
  gets a 2/4
- Add 3-5 more stories to your bank as you do more
  work

### Year 3+: Get sharp

- Mentor other candidates
- Write about behavioral interviewing (blog,
  conference talk, internal doc)
- Re-read this course once a year
- Re-read at least one book from the list per year

The senior move is to make behavioral interviewing a
*practice*, not a *cram*. The engineers who ace the
behavioral round across their careers are the ones
who treat it like any other skill — continuously
maintained, never assumed.

## 6. The final word

Behavioral interviewing is not a test you cram for. It's
a *practice* you build. The engineers who are good at it
have been practicing for years — not because they were
preparing for an interview, but because the *underlying
skills* (clear communication, structured thinking,
self-awareness, empathy under disagreement) are the same
skills that make them good engineers.

If you do this course, build the story bank, do the mock
interviews, and read the books, you will pass the
behavioral round. Not because you "gamed" it, but because
you became a better engineer in the process. The two are
the same thing.

Good luck.
