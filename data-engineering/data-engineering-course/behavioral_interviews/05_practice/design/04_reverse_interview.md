# 04 — The Reverse Interview: Questions to Ask Your Interviewer

> **Lesson 4 of 5 — Workshops** · ~15 min

30+ reverse-interview questions organized by what they
signal. The reverse interview is the last 5-10 minutes of
every round. Most candidates waste it. The senior move is
to use it.

---

## 1. Why the reverse interview matters

The reverse interview is your only chance in the round to
*evaluate the company* and to *signal senior thinking*. The
interviewer is forming an impression of you for the full 45
minutes — the last 5 minutes are the most recent data point
in their memory. A strong reverse interview can rescue a
weak middle; a weak reverse interview can sink a strong
middle.

There are 3 things the reverse interview signals:

- **Seniority of thinking** — generic questions ("what's the
  team like?") signal junior. Specific questions ("where
  does the team disagree with the broader org?") signal
  senior.
- **Genuine interest in the company** — interviewers can tell
  when you've done your homework and when you haven't.
- **Calibration of your own fit** — the questions you ask
  reveal what you care about, and the answers reveal whether
  this is the right place for you.

---

## 2. The questions, organized by what they signal

### Questions that signal "I think at the senior level"

These questions engage with judgment, strategy, and
cross-team dynamics. They work at E5+.

- *"Where does the team disagree with the broader org
  about how things should be done?"*
- *"What's a recent decision the team made that you're
  still not sure was the right call?"*
- *"What's the biggest open question on the team's
  roadmap right now?"*
- *"What would you want a new senior engineer to be
  doing differently in their first 90 days?"*
- *"What does 'good' look like for a senior engineer
  on this team in the first 6 months?"*
- *"What's the most under-appreciated risk the team is
  facing?"*
- *"What does the team disagree about internally?"*

### Questions that signal "I think about people and
teams"

These questions engage with hiring, mentorship, and
team dynamics. Especially strong for Staff+ and EM.

- *"How does the team handle underperformance?"*
- *"What's the team's approach to mentorship and career
  development?"*
- *"What's the biggest people-leverage thing you've
  done as a manager?"* (for hiring-manager rounds)
- *"What does a healthy team look like to you, and how
  do you know when a team is unhealthy?"*
- *"What's the most common management mistake you see
  on this team?"*
- *"How does the team handle disagreements about
  technical direction?"*

### Questions that signal "I've done my homework"

These questions reference specific public information
about the company — engineering blogs, conference talks,
open-source projects, public tech reports. They work at
every level but are especially strong for Staff+.

- *"I read your engineering blog post on [X]. How has
  the approach evolved since then?"*
- *"I saw the conference talk on [Y]. What was the
  pushback in the room?"*
- *"I noticed [company] open-sourced [project]. What's
  the team's involvement in maintaining it?"*
- *"In the [annual report / 10-K / blog post], you
  mentioned [challenge]. How is the team working on
  that?"*
- *"What's the technical decision you're most uncertain
  about right now, and what would change your mind?"*

### Questions that signal "I want to do meaningful work"

These questions engage with the *substance* of the work,
not the meta-process. Strong at every level.

- *"What's the most interesting technical problem the
  team is working on right now?"*
- *"What would I be working on in my first 6 months?"*
- *"What's the hardest thing about this work that
  doesn't show up in the job description?"*
- *"What does the team do that you think is
  underrated?"*
- *"What's a recent win the team has had that you're
  particularly proud of?"*

### Questions that signal "I want to be a good colleague"

These questions engage with collaboration and culture.
Strong for every level.

- *"How does the team handle on-call? Is it sustainable?"*
- *"What's the team's relationship with product
  management like?"*
- *"How do you balance building new things with
  maintaining existing ones?"*
- *"What does the team do for fun?"* (yes, really —
  this signals you're a human, not a robot)

### Questions to avoid

Some questions signal the opposite of what you intend:

- *"What does the team look like?"* — recruiter question,
  not hiring-manager question.
- *"What's the salary range?"* — wait for the recruiter
  conversation.
- *"How often do you do performance reviews?"* — feels
  transactional.
- *"What's your policy on remote work?"* — important
  question, but ask the recruiter, not the interviewer.
- *"What's your policy on [perk]?"* — the reverse
  interview is not the place for this.
- *"Do you have any concerns about me as a candidate?"*
  — never ask this. It invites the interviewer to
  manufacture concerns they didn't have.

## 3. The pre-loaded 5

Every senior candidate should have 5 reverse-interview
questions pre-loaded and ready to ask. Pick 5 from the
list above, memorize them, and have them ready for any
round.

**My personal pre-loaded 5** (use as a starting point, but
pick your own):

1. *"Where does the team disagree with the broader org
   about how things should be done?"*
2. *"What's the biggest open question on the team's
   roadmap right now?"*
3. *"What's a recent decision the team made that you're
   still not sure was the right call?"*
4. *"I read [specific company content]. How has that
   evolved?"*
5. *"What would you want a new senior engineer to be
   doing differently in their first 90 days?"*

These work for any senior IC round. Adjust for EM
(more people questions), for Staff+ (more strategy
questions), for early-career (more learning questions).

---

## 4. The "do not ask" list

A few questions are actively harmful. They signal
something you don't intend:

- *"What is the company culture like?"* — generic, gives
  the interviewer nothing to engage with.
- *"Why do you like working here?"* — invites a generic
  answer; you're not going to make a decision based on
  it.
- *"What are the next big projects the company is
  working on?"* — confidential, the interviewer will
  deflect.
- *"Will I be working on [specific thing I read about]?"*
  — too transactional; signals you have a pre-formed
  idea of the role.
- *"Do you have any concerns about my candidacy?"* — see
  above. Never ask this.
- *"How soon can I expect a promotion?"* — wait until
  you're in the role.

---

## 5. The follow-up

The best reverse-interview questions are the ones that
*invite a follow-up*. After the interviewer answers,
you should be able to ask 1-2 more questions that dig
deeper. This signals genuine curiosity and engagement.

The pattern:

- Q1: "Where does the team disagree with the broader
  org?"
- A1: "We have a different philosophy on..."
- Q2: "Interesting. How did that disagreement resolve?"
- A2: "We ended up doing X, but it took 6 months because..."
- Q3: "What's the version of that you're navigating
  right now?"

Three rounds of follow-up on a single question is the
*senior move*. It signals that you're listening, that
you want to understand, and that you can engage at the
level of strategy.

---

## Try it

Pick 5 reverse-interview questions from the list above.
Write them down. Rehearse them so you can deliver them
without notes.

The next time you do a mock interview (or a real one),
use 3 of them. Notice which ones get the most engaged
responses. Those are your keepers.
