# 01 — Welcome to Behavioral Interviewing for Engineers

> **Lesson 1 of 8 — Fast Track** · ~10 min

How to get the most out of this track, what the next 31 lessons
actually do, and the one mistake that wastes more candidate prep time
than anything else.

---

## 1. What this track is

This is a **prose-only** guide. There is no service to spin up, no
unit test to pass. The "running the test" is *you, telling a story
out loud, in front of another person, and watching their face*. That
is the entire feedback loop.

The 31 lessons are organized into 5 modules:

- **Module 01 (this one)** — the on-ramp. Mental model, common
  pitfalls, one full mock interview.
- **Module 02** — theory. STAR, taxonomy, signal vs noise.
- **Module 03** — story bank. 10-15 ready-to-tell stories.
- **Module 04** — practice. 4 mock interviews at 4 levels.
- **Module 05** — workshops. Story mining, quantification, delivery.

**Read in order. Do the "Try it" sections out loud.** That sentence
is the entire course.

---

## 2. The #1 mistake candidates make

They **read** about behavioral interviews but never **practice**
telling stories. They know the STAR method. They can recite the
"tell me about a time you had a conflict" rubric. They could write
down the four parts perfectly. And then in the actual interview, they
ramble for four minutes, blame their manager, leave out the impact
numbers, and the bar-raiser writes "no hire" on the rubric.

Why? Because **storytelling is a motor skill.** You can read every
book on swimming and you'll still sink. You have to *get in the
water*. Every lesson in this track ends with a "Try it" section
precisely so you can get in the water.

The minimum viable practice loop:

```
1. Read the lesson                              (10 min)
2. Close the lesson. Tell the story out loud.   (3 min)
3. Listen to the recording.                     (3 min)
4. Write down 1 thing to fix.                   (2 min)
```

That's 18 minutes per story. Do 3 stories a day for two weeks. By
the time you walk into the interview, the muscle memory is built.

## 3. Who this track is for

Calibrated for engineers interviewing at the **E4-E7 (Meta) /
L4-L7 (Google) / Senior-Staff-Principal (most)** level. EM candidates
(M5-M6) get value from the leadership and conflict stories but should
also do at least one EM-specific mock in Module 04.

If you have **less than 2 years of full-time experience**, this track
will still help, but you'll get more out of pairing it with general
interview prep — the senior+ framing assumes you have enough career
material to tell stories about.

If you have **15+ years** and a Staff+ target, focus extra attention
on the ambiguity and influence stories — those are where Staff and
Principal are differentiated from Senior.

## 4. The mental model in 60 seconds

Read this once. Reread it after Module 02.

Behavioral interviews exist to answer **one question**: *"If we hire
this person, what will they be like to work with when things get
hard?"* The interviewer is not testing your memory. They're testing
your **judgment** — how you make decisions under ambiguity, how you
treat people you disagree with, and what you do when you screw up.

Everything you do in this round should ladder up to that question.
Every story you tell should make the interviewer think: *"Yes, I
want to be on a 2am page with this person."*

---

## 5. A note on authenticity

There is a real risk in over-preparing for behavioral interviews:
you start sounding like a LinkedIn influencer. *"I orchestrated a
cross-functional alignment around a shared north-star outcome..."*

Don't do this. The bar-raisers and senior interviewers have heard
every variant of "I led a synergetic initiative to leverage
stakeholder bandwidth." It signals that you're performing, not
reflecting. **Specific beats general. Honest beats polished. Numbers
beat adjectives.**

If your story is real, you'll sound like yourself. If your story is
fabricated to fit a framework, you'll sound like a textbook. The
interviewer can tell the difference inside 30 seconds.

## Try it

Take the most interesting thing that happened to you at work in the
last 6 months. Set a timer for 90 seconds. Tell the story out loud
to yourself (or, better, to another person). No notes, no STAR
structure, no framework. Just tell it.

When the timer goes off, write down three sentences:

1. What was the **decision** I actually made?
2. What was the **impact** (number, time, money, people)?
3. What would I **do differently** next time?

If you can answer those three sentences cleanly, you have a story
worth refining. If you can't, you don't — and that's worth knowing
now, before the interview.
