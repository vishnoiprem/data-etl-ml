# Lesson 1: Executive Introduction

> **Week 1 · Day 1 · 90 min**
> **Deliverable:** A tested 60-second and 2-minute version of "Tell me about yourself."

## 🎯 Architect Level
Senior / Staff. (Junior and Mid versions exist, but you're interviewing for Director and above.)

## 🧠 Concept

The first 60 seconds of an executive interview set the frame. If you lead with technology, the interviewer files you as a senior IC. If you lead with outcomes, they file you as a candidate for the seat.

The two versions serve two different moments:

- **60-second** — the elevator. Used when the interviewer says "give me the short version" or "what do you do?"
- **2-minute** — the structured story. Used when the interviewer says "walk me through your background" or "tell me about yourself."

Both versions must:
1. Anchor on **outcomes**, not technologies.
2. Show a **progression** — the trajectory from where you started to where you are now.
3. End on a **forward beat** — what you want next and why you're ready for it.
4. Use only numbers you can substantiate (see `STORY_BANK.md` for the resume evidence table).

The structure is **Past → Present → Future** with the Present doing the heaviest lifting:

> **Past** (20%): 1 sentence on where you started.
> **Present** (60%): 2-3 sentences on what you do and the outcomes you've driven.
> **Future** (20%): 1 sentence on why you're here and what you want next.

## 🛠️ Build It

**Spec:** Produce two versions. Speak both out loud. Record. Listen back. Cut every word that doesn't earn its place.

**Acceptance:**
- 60s version: 50-65 seconds, 0 filler words, 0 technologies in the opening 20 words.
- 2min version: 110-130 seconds, 3 measurable outcomes, 0 acronyms in the opening 30 words.
- Both versions end with the same Future beat.

**Starter:** Use the candidate's resume evidence:
- 15+ years across Lazada, Xendit, SCB, DBS, PayPal, Apple (Exilant).
- Head of Data at Makro / CP Group; led the data platform for an $8B business.
- Ex-Lazada / Alibaba (5.5 years, 100M+ daily events, 600+ production tables).
- Track record of measurable outcomes: 40% cloud/Databricks cost reduction, 60% DBT/Spark runtime reduction, etc. (numbers that match `STORY_BANK.md`).

**Solution (60s):**
> *"I lead data and AI for an eight-billion-dollar retailer. I build the platforms that turn data into measurable business outcomes — cost, revenue, risk. Most recently I unified a fragmented data estate, cut our cloud and Databricks bill forty percent, and brought month-end close from five days to two hours. Before this I led data engineering at Lazada for five and a half years, scaling the platform to a hundred million daily events. I'm here because I want to do the same work at a larger scale, with a board-level seat at the table."*

**Solution (2min):**
> *"I lead data and AI for an eight-billion-dollar retailer. I started my career in data warehousing — PayPal, DBS, SCB — where I learned what 'reliable' means when regulators are watching. Then I moved to Lazada for five and a half years, where I led data engineering for the largest e-commerce platform in Southeast Asia. We scaled to a hundred million daily events, six hundred production tables, and I helped grow the team from a small group to a department that owned the full data stack.*
>
> *Most recently I'm at Makro, where I lead the data platform for an eight-billion-dollar business. The work I want to highlight: I unified a fragmented data estate across five business units into a single lakehouse. We cut incidents by sixty percent, cut our cloud and Databricks bill forty percent, and brought month-end close from five days to two hours. Those numbers are on my resume; the story behind them is in the way I lead — I align executives on a single number, I sequence delivery in waves, and I make the boring eighty percent of the work — contracts, governance, observability — non-negotiable.*
>
> *I'm here because I want to take this work to a larger scale, with a board-level seat. The roles I'm interviewing for are the ones where the CDO is a peer of the CFO and the CTO, not a function reporting to one of them."*

## 🏛️ Architect Notes

- **Trade-off:** Lead with the employer name vs. lead with the outcome. Lead with the outcome. The employer is in your resume; the outcome is what they came to hear.
- **Capacity / cost:** A 60-second version is approximately 150-160 words. A 2-minute version is approximately 280-300 words. Time yourself.
- **When NOT to use:** Don't use the 2-minute version for "what do you do?" — the interviewer asked for the short version.
- **Production checklist:**
  - [ ] No filler ("um", "uh", "so", "you know")
  - [ ] No apology ("I know this is long, but...")
  - [ ] No technology in the opening 20 words
  - [ ] No acronym in the opening 30 words
  - [ ] Three measurable numbers in the 2-minute version
  - [ ] Forward beat at the end

## 🌙 Reflect

- What felt hardest — cutting the technology, or compressing the outcomes?
- Which number on your resume did you choose to highlight, and why that one?
- Write 3 sentences you'll say out loud tomorrow. (Hint: the Past beat.)
