# Lesson 27: People leadership

> **Week 6 · Day 27 · 90 min**
> **Deliverable:** The 4 stages of people leadership — hire, grow, retain, let go.

## 🎯 Architect Level
Senior.

## 🧠 Concept

The CDO's job is to build a team that ships. People leadership has 4 stages, each with its own discipline:

| Stage | What it is | The CDO's job | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Hire** | Attract and select the right people | Define the bar; calibrate the loop; close | Hiring below the bar because "we need someone now" |
| **2. Grow** | Develop people from where they are to where they need to be | Coaching; stretch assignments; feedback | Promoting people into roles they can't yet do |
| **3. Retain** | Keep the people who are doing great work | Comp; mission; growth; recognition | Assuming comp is enough |
| **4. Let go** | Help people who aren't performing, or who need to move on | Direct feedback; PIP; off-ramp | Avoiding the conversation for 6+ months |

The single biggest mistake is to **hire below the bar**. A team of 10 B-players is not a team of 10 A-players. A-players hire A-players; B-players hire C-players. The first 5 hires set the trajectory of the team for 3 years.

The second biggest mistake is to **avoid the "let go" conversation**. The underperformer who stays 6 months past the inflection point costs the team 6 months of output and poisons the culture. The CDO who avoids it is the CDO who loses the team.

The third biggest mistake is to **confuse comp with retention**. Comp is hygiene. Mission, growth, recognition, and autonomy are motivation. The CDO who only competes on comp loses to the CDO who competes on mission.

The growth model: every person on the team has a **development plan** (3-5 skills, 12-month horizon), reviewed quarterly. The plan has 1-2 stretch assignments, 1 mentor, 1 training budget.

**Starter:** Use your resume evidence. Hiring 8 → 25 at Makro [CONFIRM]; losing people at the wrong times.

**Solution (people leadership, abridged):**

**Hire:**
- Bar per role: 5 must-haves, 3 nice-to-haves
- Loop: recruiter screen → hiring manager → 2 technical peers → cross-functional → exec
- Calibration: every loop debriefed against the bar; hire only if all say "yes"
- Closing: comp band + mission + growth + 1 person the candidate admires on the team

**Grow:**
- 30-60-90 plan for every new hire
- Quarterly 1:1; annual review
- Development plan: 3 skills, 12-month horizon, 1 stretch, 1 mentor
- Promotion: twice a year; calibration across managers

**Retain:**
- Comp bands per level, per geo, refreshed annually [CONFIRM: real numbers]
- Equity refresh for high performers every 2 years
- Top-5 high-potential program: exposure, exec sponsorship, conference budget
- "Stay interview" for any high performer considering leaving

**Let go:**
- 30-60-90 PIP for underperformers
- Off-ramp: severance, references, internal mobility
- Documentation: every conversation in writing, dated, signed
- 6-month no-regret check: did we make the right call?

**Trade-off (rigor vs. empathy):** The CDO holds both. The bar is the bar; the conversation is human. "I care about you and this isn't working" is the right tone.

## 🏛️ Architect Notes

- **Trade-off:** Promote from within vs. hire externally. Default: promote from within for the next 2 levels; hire externally for the level above. Brings both continuity and fresh blood.
- **Capacity / cost:** Recruiting cost: $30-80K per senior hire. Retention cost: 2-3× the comp delta. Investing in retention pays back.
- **When NOT to use:** Don't run a PIP as a paper trail. The PIP is a genuine attempt to help. If the person can't make it, the PIP is the off-ramp.
