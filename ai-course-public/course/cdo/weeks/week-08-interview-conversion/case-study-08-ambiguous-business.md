# Case Study 8: The Ambiguous Business

> **Week 8 · Case Study · 90 min**
> **Deliverable:** Defend your reasoning on an ambiguous business problem with no right answer in 30 minutes.

## The brief

You are 90 days into the CDO role. The CEO walks into your office on a Friday afternoon. "I have a problem and I need you to think about it over the weekend. I won't tell you what to do — I want your recommendation by Monday."

**The brief:**

A potential acquirer has approached the company. They want to buy the data business — the customer data platform, the data products, the AI/ML models — as a separate entity. The offer is $XM, which is 15% of the company's current valuation. The CEO is "intrigued but cautious." The board will discuss it in 30 days.

**The CEO asks you:**

"Three questions. One, what's our data actually worth? Two, what do we lose if we sell it? Three, would you recommend we sell?"

**The challenge:**
- There is no right answer.
- The "data business" is not a clean entity — it's embedded in the operating business.
- The 15% of valuation may be high or low — you don't know.
- The buyer is a competitor in some segments and a partner in others.
- The employees who work on the data would have to move with the data business — that includes 30 of your 50 people.

You have 30 minutes to walk the CEO through your reasoning. The CEO will challenge every assumption.

## Solution sketch (do not copy — internalize and re-speak)

**Reframe (3 min):**
- "The question is not 'what is our data worth' — the question is 'what is the right shape of this business for the next 5 years.' The data is a capability, not a product. Selling the data is selling a capability. We have to ask: is the capability worth more inside the operating business or as a standalone entity?"
- Three sub-questions: (1) what's the standalone value? (2) what's the strategic value inside? (3) what do we lose either way?

**The value (8 min):**
- Standalone value: $XM offer implies 15% of company value. But the data is embedded — to standalone, we'd need to:
  - Separate the data stack (1 platform, not 5 BUs)
  - Separate the people (30 of 50)
  - Separate the contracts (data use rights across 100+ vendors)
  - Standalone cost: 18-24 months, $Y in separation cost, $Z in run-rate overhead
  - Net standalone value: $XM - $Y - $Z = $A-$B (probably less than $XM)
- Plus: standalone = loss of synergy with the operating business (the data informs pricing, marketing, ops)
- Comparable transactions: [CONFIRM: get 2-3 comparables from the candidate's knowledge]

**The cost (8 min):**
- Strategic cost: the operating business loses the data advantage; competitive moat erodes; AI mandate dies
- Operational cost: the operating business would have to license back the data, at a cost we don't control
- People cost: 30 of 50 people move; the remaining 20 lose their core capability
- Customer cost: customer trust in the data partnership is at risk; the regulator may require notification (CCPA / GDPR data sharing)
- Reputational cost: a competitor owns our data; the market reads it as "we don't believe in our own data"

**The recommendation (6 min):**
- **Recommend: hold, with conditions.**
- Condition 1: a counter-offer from a financial buyer (PE) — 30-day exploration
- Condition 2: a license-back model — sell the data, license it back at a fixed cost; preserve the operating business advantage
- Condition 3: a 12-month option — if the operating business value of the data proves out, walk away
- Why: the strategic value inside is probably 2-3× the standalone value; the buyer knows this; the offer is a probe, not a final

**The risks (5 min):**
- Risk 1: the buyer walks; the operating business lost 30 days. Mitigation: keep the exploration quiet; no internal announcement until decision
- Risk 2: the board wants the $XM; the recommendation is hold. Mitigation: present the math; the board will follow the math
- Risk 3: the buyer raises the offer. Mitigation: re-evaluate at $XM + 30%; the math has to hold
- Risk 4: the operating business AI mandate dies if we sell. Mitigation: the license-back model preserves it
- Off-ramp: if the buyer won't do license-back, the answer is no

**Q&A — be ready for:**
- "What if the board overrides your recommendation?" (present the math; the board will follow the math; if not, document the dissent)
- "What if the operating business value proves out and we passed on $XM?" (we'd have $XM of NPV in the operating business value; the math has to show that)
- "What if a PE offers $X > $XM?" (re-evaluate; the answer is the highest-NPV path)
- "What about the people?" (30 of 50 move; 20 stay; we'd restructure; severance + outplacement for the displaced; communications plan)

## What to watch for when you re-listen

- **Did you reframe in the first 3 minutes?** If the CEO thinks you answered the wrong question, you've lost.
- **Did you do the math?** The CEO buys math, not narrative. Without numbers, the recommendation is opinion.
- **Did you name the recommendation with conditions?** A recommendation without conditions is reckless. A recommendation with conditions is decisive.
- **Did you end on time?** If you run over, the CEO concludes you can't think under ambiguity.

## Re-record until you score ≥ 90

A CDO who can't defend a recommendation on an ambiguous business case in 30 minutes is not a CDO. The ambiguous case is the hardest executive interview question because there is no right answer.
