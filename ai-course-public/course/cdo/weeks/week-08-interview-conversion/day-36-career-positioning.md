# Lesson 36: Career positioning

> **Week 8 · Day 36 · 90 min**
> **Deliverable:** A 1-page positioning statement you can hand to the recruiter, the hiring manager, and the board.

## 🎯 Architect Level
Staff.

## 🧠 Concept

Positioning is the answer to "in one paragraph, who are you and why does this role exist for you?" It is the single most important artifact in the conversion. Without it, every conversation starts from zero. With it, every conversation has momentum.

The 1-page positioning statement has 4 blocks:

1. **Headline.** 1 sentence. "I take fragmented data estates and turn them into platforms the business can build on."
2. **Evidence.** 3-4 bullet points with the resume evidence. Use real numbers (or [CONFIRM]).
3. **Why this role.** 1-2 sentences. The specific reason this role exists for you.
4. **Why this company.** 1-2 sentences. The specific reason this company.

The single biggest mistake is to **write a generic headline**. "I am a data leader with experience in…" is forgettable. "I take fragmented data estates and turn them into platforms the business can build on" is memorable.

The second biggest mistake is to **not tailor the "why this company."** Every company has a different reason. The candidate who says "I love your products" sounds like every candidate. The candidate who says "your 4 active-customer definitions is the same fragmentation I solved at [company]" sounds like they have done the work.

The third biggest mistake is to **lead with the title**. "I was the Head of Data at…" is what the resume already says. The positioning statement is the *through-line*, not the chronology.

## 🛠️ Build It

**Spec:** Build the 1-page positioning statement for the role you're interviewing for.

**Acceptance:**
- 4 blocks (headline, evidence, why role, why company)
- Real numbers (or [CONFIRM])
- 1 specific per block
- 1 paragraph on the trade-off (specificity vs. flexibility)

**Starter:** Use your real resume. Pick the through-line.

**Solution (positioning, abridged):**

**Headline.** I take fragmented data estates and turn them into AI-ready platforms the business can build on — 12 years across retail (Makro, Lazada) and fintech (Xendit), 4B+ events/day at peak.

**Evidence.**
- Makro: unified data platform transformation, 10B+ rows/day, 99.99% uptime [CONFIRM]
- Lazada: real-time customer 360, 20M+ insights/day, Flink + ClickHouse [CONFIRM]
- Xendit: scaled data team 8 → 30, FinOps -40% [CONFIRM]
- 1 AI in production per quarter across the last 8 quarters [CONFIRM]

**Why this role.** The Head of Data role is the right scale of the same problem I have been solving — fragmented data + AI mandate + executive pressure — and the company has the data volume + business commitment to make the transformation real.

**Why this company.** [Specific to the company. E.g., "your 4 active-customer definitions across 5 BUs is the same fragmentation I solved at Makro. Your 3 warehouses and 6 BI tools is the same consolidation I delivered at Lazada. Your AI mandate is the same Y1-deliverable pressure I have navigated three times."]

**Trade-off (specificity vs. flexibility):** The more specific, the more memorable. The more flexible, the more reusable. Default: specific. The 1-pager is for THIS company.

## 🏛️ Architect Notes

- **Trade-off:** Specificity vs. flexibility. Specific wins for the company you're targeting. Build 2-3 versions (per company type) if you're in a multi-company search.
- **Capacity / cost:** A positioning statement takes 2-4 hours to build well. The first one takes a day. Build 3.
- **When NOT to use:** Don't read the positioning statement in the interview. The statement is for the recruiter + the prep doc + the LinkedIn. In the interview, the statement becomes the through-line of your answers.
- **Production checklist:**
  - [ ] 4 blocks (headline, evidence, why role, why company)
  - [ ] Real numbers (or [CONFIRM])
  - [ ] 1 specific per block
  - [ ] Trade-off paragraph

## 🌙 Reflect

- Read your positioning statement out loud. Does it sound like you? If not, rewrite.
- Show it to a friend. Does it sound like you? If not, rewrite.
- Write 3 sentences you'll say out loud tomorrow.
