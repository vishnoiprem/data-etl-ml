# CS-08 (Stripe) — Should Issuing's data business be spun out? Defend the recommendation to Patrick

> **Mapping:** Original CS-08. Retargeted to Stripe's Issuing business.
> **Goal:** 30 minutes. Defend the recommendation on whether to spin out Issuing's data business.

## The brief

Patrick walks into your office on a Friday afternoon. "I have a problem. `[CONFIRM: a financial buyer / a strategic partner]` has approached us about spinning out Issuing's data business — the credit-decisioning data, the spend-management data, the Treasury analytics — as a separate entity. The offer is $XM, which is `[CONFIRM: N%]` of Issuing's current valuation. The board is intrigued. The Issuing lead is cautious. I need your recommendation by Monday."

**The CEO asks you three questions:**
1. What's Issuing's data actually worth?
2. What do we lose if we spin it out?
3. Would you recommend we sell?

**The challenge:**
- The "data business" is not a clean entity — it's embedded in Issuing + Treasury operations.
- Issuing + Treasury are growth-stage; the data is a fraction of the capex.
- The buyer is `[CONFIRM: who]`.
- The 30+ Issuing ML engineers would have to move.

## Solution sketch (do not copy — internalize and re-speak)

**Reframe (3 min).** "The question is not 'what is Issuing's data worth' — the question is 'what is the right shape of Issuing for the next 5 years.' The data is a capability, not a product. Spinning it out separates the capability from the operating business that creates it."

**The value (8 min).**
- Standalone value: $XM offer implies `[N%]` of Issuing valuation. But the data is embedded — to standalone, we'd need to:
  - Separate the data stack (credit decisioning, spend management, Treasury analytics)
  - Separate the people (30+ ML engineers)
  - Separate the contracts (bank-partnership data, BSA/AML data)
  - Standalone cost: 18-24 months, $Y in separation cost, $Z in run-rate overhead
  - Net standalone value: $XM - $Y - $Z = $A-$B (probably less than $XM)
- Plus: standalone = loss of synergy with Issuing + Treasury operations
- Comparable transactions: `[CONFIRM: 2-3]`

**The cost (8 min).**
- Strategic cost: Issuing loses the data advantage; competitive moat erodes; growth slows
- Operational cost: Issuing would have to license back the data
- People cost: 30+ ML engineers move
- Customer cost: merchant trust in the data partnership is at risk
- Reputational cost: `[CONFIRM: who]` owns our Issuing data

**The recommendation (6 min).**
- **Recommend: hold, with conditions.**
- Condition 1: a counter-offer from `[CONFIRM: a financial buyer]`
- Condition 2: a license-back model
- Condition 3: a 12-month option
- Why: the strategic value inside is 2-3× the standalone value; the buyer knows this

**The risks (5 min).**
- Risk 1: the buyer walks; we lost 30 days
- Risk 2: the board wants the $XM
- Risk 3: the buyer raises the offer
- Risk 4: Issuing growth regresses without the data
- Off-ramp: if no license-back, the answer is no

**Q&A — be ready for:**
- "What if the board overrides?" — Present the math; document the dissent.
- "What if Issuing's value proves out?" — $XM of NPV; the math has to show that.
- "What about the people?" — 30+ move; severance + outplacement.
- "What if `[CONFIRM: a competitor like Adyen / Block / Brex]` offers more?" — Re-evaluate; the answer is the highest-NPV path.

## Watch for when you re-listen

- **Did you reframe in the first 3 minutes?**
- **Did you do the math?** Patrick buys math, not narrative.
- **Did you name the recommendation with conditions?**
- **Did you end on time?**
