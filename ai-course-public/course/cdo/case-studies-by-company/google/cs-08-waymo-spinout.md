# CS-08 (Google) — Should Waymo's data business be spun out? Defend the recommendation to Sundar

> **Mapping:** Original CS-08 (ambiguous business case). Retargeted to Alphabet's "Other Bets" — specifically Waymo.
> **Goal:** 30 minutes. Defend your recommendation on whether to spin out Waymo's data business.

## The brief

Sundar Pichai walks into your office on a Friday afternoon. "I have a problem. `[CONFIRM: a financial buyer / a strategic partner / a sovereign fund]` has approached us about spinning out Waymo's data business — the ride data, the HD map data, the autonomy training data — as a separate entity. The offer is $XM, which is `[CONFIRM: N%]` of Waymo's current valuation. The board is intrigued. The Waymo CEO is cautious. I need your recommendation by Monday."

**The CEO asks you three questions:**
1. What's Waymo's data actually worth?
2. What do we lose if we spin it out?
3. Would you recommend we sell?

**The challenge:**
- The "data business" is not a clean entity — it's embedded in Waymo's operations.
- The valuation may be high or low — you don't know.
- The buyer is `[CONFIRM: who — competitor, partner, financial]`.
- The 200+ Waymo ML engineers would have to move with the data business.

## The structure (30 min)

| Section | Time | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| Reframe | 3 min | The question behind the question |
| The value | 8 min | What the data is worth; how to value it |
| The cost | 8 min | What we lose if we spin out |
| The recommendation | 6 min | Spin out, hold, or hybrid; with conditions |
| The risks | 5 min | What could go wrong; the off-ramps |

## Solution sketch (do not copy — internalize and re-speak)

**Reframe (3 min).**
- "The question is not 'what is Waymo's data worth' — the question is 'what is the right shape of Waymo for the next 5 years.' The data is a capability, not a product. Spinning out the data is separating a capability from the operating business that creates it. We have to ask: is the capability worth more inside Waymo or as a standalone entity?"

**The value (8 min).**
- Standalone value: $XM offer implies `[N%]` of Waymo valuation. But the data is embedded — to standalone, we'd need to:
  - Separate the data stack (the ride data, the HD map data, the autonomy training data)
  - Separate the people (200+ ML engineers)
  - Separate the contracts (data use rights across `[CONFIRM: partners]`)
  - Standalone cost: 18-24 months, $Y in separation cost, $Z in run-rate overhead
  - Net standalone value: $XM - $Y - $Z = $A-$B
- Plus: standalone = loss of synergy with Waymo's operations (the data informs the autonomy stack, the routing, the safety)
- Comparable transactions: `[CONFIRM: 2-3 comparables]`

**The cost (8 min).**
- Strategic cost: Waymo loses the data advantage; competitive moat erodes; autonomy R&D slows
- Operational cost: Waymo would have to license back the data, at a cost we don't control
- People cost: 200+ ML engineers move; the remaining Waymo team loses its core capability
- Customer cost: partner trust in the data partnership is at risk; the regulator (NHTSA / DMV) may require notification
- Reputational cost: `[CONFIRM: who — competitor]` owns our data; the market reads it as "we don't believe in our own autonomy"

**The recommendation (6 min).**
- **Recommend: hold, with conditions.**
- Condition 1: a counter-offer from `[CONFIRM: a financial buyer — e.g., Silver Lake, Blackstone]` — 30-day exploration
- Condition 2: a license-back model — spin out the data, license it back at a fixed cost; preserve Waymo's advantage
- Condition 3: a 12-month option — if Waymo's standalone autonomy value proves out, walk away
- Why: the strategic value inside is probably 2-3× the standalone value; the buyer knows this; the offer is a probe

**The risks (5 min).**
- Risk 1: the buyer walks; we lost 30 days. Mitigation: keep the exploration quiet; no internal announcement.
- Risk 2: the board wants the $XM. Mitigation: present the math; the board will follow.
- Risk 3: the buyer raises the offer. Mitigation: re-evaluate at $XM + 30%; the math has to hold.
- Risk 4: Waymo's autonomy stack regresses without the data. Mitigation: the license-back model preserves it.
- Off-ramp: if the buyer won't do license-back, the answer is no.

**Q&A — be ready for:**
- "What if the board overrides your recommendation?" — Present the math; document the dissent.
- "What if Waymo's value proves out and we passed on $XM?" — We'd have $XM of NPV in Waymo; the math has to show that.
- "What about the people?" — 200+ move; severance + outplacement; communications plan.
- "What if `[CONFIRM: a competitor like Cruise / Mobileye / Tesla]` makes a higher offer?" — Re-evaluate; the answer is the highest-NPV path.

## Watch for when you re-listen

- **Did you reframe in the first 3 minutes?** If Sundar thinks you answered the wrong question, you've lost.
- **Did you do the math?** Sundar buys math, not narrative. Without numbers, the recommendation is opinion.
- **Did you name the recommendation with conditions?** Without conditions = reckless. With conditions = decisive.
- **Did you end on time?** Running over signals you can't think under ambiguity.
