# CS-08 (Shopify) — Should the Shopify Capital data business be carved out as a separate entity? Defend the recommendation to Tobi

> **Mapping:** Original CS-08. Retargeted to Shopify's Capital business.
> **Goal:** 30 minutes. Defend the recommendation on whether to carve out Capital's data business.

## The brief

Tobi walks into your office on a Friday afternoon. "I have a problem. `[CONFIRM: a financial buyer / a strategic partner]` has approached us about carving out Shopify Capital's data business — the merchant underwriting data, the spend management data, the cash-flow analytics — as a separate entity. The offer is $XM, which is `[CONFIRM: N%]` of Capital's current valuation. The board is intrigued. The Capital lead is cautious. I need your recommendation by Monday."

**The CEO asks you three questions:**
1. What's Capital's data actually worth?
2. What do we lose if we carve it out?
3. Would you recommend we sell?

**The challenge:**
- The "data business" is not a clean entity — it's embedded in Capital operations.
- Capital is a growth bet; the data is a fraction of the capex.
- The buyer is `[CONFIRM: who]`.
- The 20+ Capital ML engineers would have to move.

## Solution sketch (do not copy — internalize and re-speak)

**Reframe (3 min).** "The question is not 'what is Capital's data worth' — the question is 'what is the right shape of Capital for the next 5 years.' The data is a capability, not a product. Carving it out separates the capability from the operating business that creates it."

**The value (8 min).**
- Standalone value: $XM offer implies `[N%]` of Capital valuation. But the data is embedded — to standalone, we'd need to:
  - Separate the data stack (merchant underwriting, spend management, cash-flow analytics)
  - Separate the people (20+ ML engineers)
  - Separate the contracts (bank-partnership data, lending-license data)
  - Standalone cost: 18-24 months, $Y in separation cost, $Z in run-rate overhead
  - Net standalone value: $XM - $Y - $Z = $A-$B (probably less than $XM)
- Plus: standalone = loss of synergy with Capital operations
- Comparable transactions: `[CONFIRM: 2-3]`

**The cost (8 min).**
- Strategic cost: Capital loses the data advantage; competitive moat erodes; growth slows
- Operational cost: Capital would have to license back the data
- People cost: 20+ ML engineers move
- Customer cost: merchant trust in the data partnership is at risk
- Reputational cost: `[CONFIRM: who]` owns our Capital data

**The recommendation (6 min).**
- **Recommend: hold, with conditions.**
- Condition 1: a counter-offer from `[CONFIRM: a financial buyer]`
- Condition 2: a license-back model
- Condition 3: a 12-month option
- Why: the strategic value inside is 2-3× the standalone value; the buyer knows this

**The risks (5 min).**
- Risk 1: the buyer walks; we lost 30 days
- Risk 2: the board wants the $XM
- Risk 3: the buyer raises the offer
- Risk 4: Capital growth regresses without the data
- Off-ramp: if no license-back, the answer is no

**Q&A — be ready for:**
- "What if the board overrides?" — Present the math; document the dissent.
- "What if Capital's value proves out?" — $XM of NPV; the math has to show that.
- "What about the people?" — 20+ move; severance + outplacement.
- "What if `[CONFIRM: a competitor like Brex / Stripe / Adyen]` offers more?" — Re-evaluate; the answer is the highest-NPV path.

## Watch for when you re-listen

- **Did you reframe in the first 3 minutes?**
- **Did you do the math?** Tobi buys math, not narrative.
- **Did you name the recommendation with conditions?**
- **Did you end on time?**
