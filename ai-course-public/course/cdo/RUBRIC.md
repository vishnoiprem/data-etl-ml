# Scoring Rubric — Data & AI Executive Leadership Masterclass

> **Target:** ≥ 90/100 in practice, with no major weakness in any core competency.
> **Cadence:** Score yourself every Friday. Track the trend, not the single sample.

## The 6 competencies

| # | Competency | Weight | What "great" looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Strategic and Business Thinking** | **25%** | Frames every answer in business terms. Connects technology to revenue, cost, risk, and customer outcomes. Anticipates CEO-level "why this, why now, what else could you do." |
| 2 | **Leadership and Executive Influence** | **20%** | Influences without authority. Manages up, down, and across. Handles conflict with senior stakeholders. Mentors, develops, and replaces underperformers. |
| 3 | **Technical Judgment and Architecture** | **20%** | Sufficient technical depth to lead architecture discussions with CTOs, principal engineers. Knows what to defer to specialists. Trade-offs are explicit. |
| 4 | **Commercial and Financial Understanding** | **15%** | Reads a P&L. Builds a business case (ROI, TCO, NPV, payback). Speaks the CFO's language. Distinguishes cost savings from realized business value. |
| 5 | **Communication and Structure** | **10%** | Answers are structured (Problem → Decision → Action → Impact). 60-second and 2-minute versions. No filler, no hedging, no jargon dump. |
| 6 | **Ownership and Measurable Impact** | **10%** | Every story ends with a number you can substantiate. Every initiative has an owner, a deadline, a metric. No "we tried." |
| | **Total** | **100%** | |

## Per-competency scoring guide

For each competency, score 0-10, then multiply by the weight. The total is your interview readiness score for that answer.

### 1. Strategic and Business Thinking (25%)

| Score | Signal |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | Talks about technology. No business framing. |
| 3-4 | Mentions business impact but doesn't quantify. |
| 5-6 | Quantifies impact for one stakeholder. |
| 7-8 | Quantifies across stakeholders; ties to a 1-yr / 3-yr strategy. |
| 9-10 | Frames the decision as a portfolio trade-off. Shows the option that was *not* chosen and why. |

### 2. Leadership and Executive Influence (20%)

| Score | Signal |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | "I told the team to do X." Dictator language. |
| 3-4 | "We aligned on X." Collaborative but vague on the conflict. |
| 5-6 | Names the specific stakeholder. Describes the influence approach. |
| 7-8 | Shows the conflict. Names the mechanism (data, framing, trade-off, escalation). Names the result. |
| 9-10 | Shows the second-order effect (team learned X, the relationship changed, the next ask got easier). |

### 3. Technical Judgment and Architecture (20%)

| Score | Signal |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | Vague. "We used a modern stack." |
| 3-4 | Names technologies but no trade-off. |
| 5-6 | Names 1-2 trade-offs. |
| 7-8 | Names the rejected alternative and why. Quantifies the cost. |
| 9-10 | Explains what they would do differently in hindsight. Names the technical debt they accepted. |

### 4. Commercial and Financial Understanding (15%)

| Score | Signal |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | No numbers. |
| 3-4 | One number, no baseline. |
| 5-6 | Has a baseline. No time horizon. |
| 7-8 | Has ROI / NPV / payback. Distinguishes savings from value. |
| 9-10 | Tracks realized vs projected. Names what the business case got wrong. |

### 5. Communication and Structure (10%)

| Score | Signal |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | Wanders. 3+ minutes for a 60-second question. |
| 3-4 | Some structure but filler words, hedging. |
| 5-6 | Clear structure. Under 90 seconds for a 60-second question. |
| 7-8 | Crisp. Stops on time. No filler. |
| 9-10 | Makes the listener's next question obvious and answers it preemptively. |

### 6. Ownership and Measurable Impact (10%)

| Score | Signal |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | "The team did X." |
| 3-4 | "I led X." No number. |
| 5-6 | "I led X; the result was Y." Y is a number, but no baseline. |
| 7-8 | "I led X; the result was Y (from A to B)." Baseline + outcome. |
| 9-10 | "I led X; the result was Y (from A to B) over N months; the team learned Z." |

## How to score a single answer

1. Re-listen to your recording.
2. For each of the 6 competencies, pick a 0-10 score.
3. Multiply by the weight, sum to a 0-100 total.
4. Note the lowest-scoring competency. That's your drill for next week.

Example: 7 + 8 + 6 + 7 + 8 + 9 = weighted = 7×0.25 + 8×0.20 + 6×0.20 + 7×0.15 + 8×0.10 + 9×0.10 = 1.75 + 1.60 + 1.20 + 1.05 + 0.80 + 0.90 = **7.30 / 10 (73%)**.

## How to score a single competency across a week

Use the same 0-10 per question, average the week's questions, then multiply by weight.

## Pass / fail thresholds

| Score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| < 60 | Not ready. Stop and re-do the day. |
| 60-74 | Emerging. Drill the lowest competency. |
| 75-89 | Ready for early-round screens. Rehearse hardest 3 answers. |
| 90-100 | Ready for final rounds. Maintain. |

## The single rule

> **If the lowest of the 6 scores is below 5, the answer is not ready — regardless of the total.**

A 95 with a "1" in Ownership is a disqualifier. A CDO who cannot claim ownership of a real outcome is not a CDO.

## Anti-patterns (instant 1-2 score)

- "I was responsible for the data platform" with no number, no business outcome, no team.
- "We used Kafka, Spark, and Snowflake" — the tech stack is not the story.
- "It was a great learning experience" — never say this.
- "I would do the same thing again" — without a what-you'd-change beat, this signals no growth.
- "The CEO was aligned" — without showing the conflict that preceded alignment, this signals no influence.
