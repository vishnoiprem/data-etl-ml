## The 4 use case criteria (the filter, before you read any of the 5)

**Before you automate anything, score it against these 4 criteria.** If a workflow doesn't hit all 4, don't automate it — the AI will be worse than you, and you'll conclude AI "doesn't work":

1. **Repetitive.** The task happens > 5 times/week. AI's value scales with volume; a task that happens once a week is not worth automating.
2. **Language-heavy.** The task is text or speech. AI's strength is language; tasks that don't involve language (e.g., physical inventory, image classification) are not the right fit.
3. **Tool-using (or paste-able).** The task requires reading a file, an inbox, a chat — or you can copy-paste the input. The wrong choice is to ask the AI to "go do this." The right choice is to copy-paste the day's inputs in.
4. **Measurable.** You can tell in 5 seconds whether the output is good or bad. Tasks where success is subjective (e.g., "is this brand voice good?") are hard to measure; tasks where success is objective (e.g., "did the reply answer the question?") are easy.

The 5 workflows below pass all 4. The 5 things on your to-do list that *don't* pass — strategy, hiring, vendor negotiation, the actual product decision — should stay on your list. The AI is for the boring stuff.

## Workflow 1: Customer email drafting (saves 2-3 hours/week)

**The boring work:** you receive 20-100 customer emails a week. Each one needs a thoughtful reply. The reply takes 5-10 minutes if you think carefully. That's 1.5-15 hours/week of writing "I'm sorry, let me check on that" in 5 different ways.

**The AI workflow:** you paste the customer's email into ChatGPT with a 4-line prompt. You get a 3-5 sentence draft in 10 seconds. You read it, edit 1-2 lines, hit send. **30 seconds per email instead of 5-10 minutes.**

**The prompt (lift this verbatim, then customize on day 3):**

> *You are [YOUR NAME], the owner of [YOUR BUSINESS]. You write in [LANGUAGE], in a warm but professional tone. You never make promises about shipping, refunds, or timelines without checking first. You always end with a question to keep the conversation going.*
>
> *The customer's message:*
> *[PASTE EMAIL HERE]*
>
> *Your reply (3-5 sentences, warm, no promises, end with a question):*

**Time saved:** 2-3 hours/week. **Cost:** $0.001-0.01 per email.

**The guardrail:** **never send without reading.** Every output is a draft. You read + click send. The wrong choice is auto-send. The right choice is human-in-the-loop.

## Workflow 2: WhatsApp / social replies (saves 2-3 hours/week)

**The boring work:** you receive 50-200 WhatsApp messages a week. Customers asking "do you have this in stock?" "what's the price?" "are you open Sunday?" Each one deserves a thoughtful 1-2 sentence reply. You're typing the same 5 replies all day.

**The AI workflow:** you paste the WhatsApp into ChatGPT with a 3-line prompt. You get a 1-2 sentence reply in 10 seconds. You read it, paste into WhatsApp, hit send.

**The prompt:**

> *You are the owner of [YOUR BUSINESS], replying on WhatsApp. The customer wrote in [LANGUAGE]. Reply in the same language, 1-2 sentences max, no emojis, end with one clarifying question. If the customer is asking about price, availability, or location, give a direct answer first, then the question.*
>
> *The customer's WhatsApp message:*
> *[PASTE WHATSAPP HERE]*
>
> *Your reply (1-2 sentences, in [LANGUAGE]):*

**Time saved:** 2-3 hours/week. **Cost:** $0.001-0.005 per message.

**The guardrail:** **never paste customer PII.** Replace the customer's name with `[NAME]`, phone with `[PHONE]`, order number with `[ORDER]`. The wrong choice is to paste the actual message into ChatGPT. The right choice is to anonymize first, then paste.

## Workflow 3: Long-form → 30 social posts (saves 3-4 hours/week)

**The boring work:** you write 1 long-form piece a week (a newsletter, a blog post, a thought-leadership LinkedIn post). You then need 30 social posts to promote it across LinkedIn, Facebook, and Instagram. You sit down on Saturday morning to write them, you write 8, you give up, you have a content gap.

**The AI workflow:** you paste the long-form into ChatGPT with a 3-line prompt. You get 30 posts in 60 seconds. You edit 5-10 of them, schedule them across the week. **30 social posts in 10 minutes instead of 4 hours.**

**The prompt:**

> *You are a social media manager for [YOUR BUSINESS]. Your job is to turn 1 long-form piece (article, blog post, transcript) into 30 social posts: 10 LinkedIn, 10 Facebook, 10 Instagram. Each post has a hook (first line), a body (2-4 sentences), and a CTA (last line). Each post stands alone (no "continued in comments").*
>
> *The long-form piece:*
> *[PASTE LONG-FORM HERE]*
>
> *Output: 30 posts, numbered 1-30, labeled LinkedIn / Facebook / Instagram.*

**Time saved:** 3-4 hours/week. **Cost:** $0.01-0.05 per long-form.

**The guardrail:** **never trust the output.** The AI will invent a quote you didn't make, misattribute a stat, or add a hashtag that doesn't apply. Read every post. The wrong choice is to schedule all 30. The right choice is to edit 5-10 and discard the rest.

## Workflow 4: Weekly report (saves 1-2 hours/week)

**The boring work:** every Friday you owe your partner, your board, or yourself a 1-page "what happened this week" report. You have the data (revenue, new customers, top product) but you dread writing it.

**The AI workflow:** you paste the week's numbers into ChatGPT with a 2-line prompt. You get a 200-word report in 30 seconds. You edit, send.

**The prompt:**

> *You are the operations manager for [YOUR BUSINESS]. You write a weekly report every Friday. The report has 3 sections: (1) This week's numbers (revenue, new customers, top product), (2) What worked, (3) What to fix next week. The report goes to the owner; it should be 200 words max, no jargon, no fluff.*
>
> *This week's data:*
> *[PASTE DATA — revenue, new customers, top product, top issue]*
>
> *The weekly report (200 words, 3 sections):*

**Time saved:** 1-2 hours/week. **Cost:** $0.005-0.01 per report.

**The guardrail:** **never skip the 1-hour-per-day ramp.** The weekly report is a Friday habit, not a Saturday binge. The wrong choice is to write the report on Sunday night. The right choice is Friday afternoon, 1 hour, done.

## Workflow 5: Competitor + news brief (saves 1-2 hours/week)

**The boring work:** you should be reading industry news and checking competitors every week. You don't, because the 5 minutes of scrolling turns into 30 minutes and you still don't know what changed.

**The AI workflow:** every Monday morning, you spend 15 minutes scanning 3 news sites and 1 competitor. You paste the headlines + URLs into ChatGPT with a 3-line prompt. You get a 5-bullet brief in 30 seconds. You read it, you're done for the week.

**The prompt:**

> *You are a research analyst for [YOUR BUSINESS]. Every Monday you read 3 news sources and 1 competitor's website. You write a 5-bullet brief: what happened in the industry, what the competitor did, what [YOUR BUSINESS] should do this week. The brief is 200 words max.*
>
> *This week's news + competitor activity:*
> *[PASTE HEADLINES OR TEXT]*
>
> *The brief (5 bullets, 200 words):*

**Time saved:** 1-2 hours/week. **Cost:** $0.01-0.05 per brief.

**The guardrail:** **never run 24/7.** This workflow runs once a week, on Monday morning, with you in the loop. The wrong choice is to set up an auto-scraper and let it run. The right choice is the weekly 15-minute manual scan + AI summary.
