## Thread 6: Common AI Mistakes

**Tweet 1:**
I've made every AI mistake possible.

Here are the 7 most common (and how to fix them) - 8 tweets:

**Tweet 2:**
Mistake #1: Using AI without context

Bad: "Write a marketing plan"
Good: "I'm a [type] business in [city] targeting [audience]. My current revenue is [$X]. Create a 90-day marketing plan to reach [$Y]."

Fix: Always add context.

**Tweet 3:**
Mistake #2: Accepting the first output

AI's first response is rarely the best. Always:
1. Read the response
2. Ask for revisions
3. Refine 2-3 times

Fix: Treat AI like a first draft, not a final product.

**Tweet 4:**
Mistake #3: Not saving good prompts

When you find a prompt that works, save it. Build a library.

I have 200+ saved prompts organized by use case.

Fix: Use a prompt management tool or Google Doc.

**Tweet 5:**
Mistake #4: Trying to automate everything at once

Pick ONE task. Master it. Then add another.

Customer service first. Then content. Then email. Then...

Fix: One system at a time.

**Tweet 6:**
Mistake #5: Ignoring brand voice

AI's default tone is generic. Your brand isn't.

Add to every prompt: "Match this tone: [paste example of your writing]"

Fix: Train AI on your voice.

**Tweet 7:**
Mistake #6: Not reviewing AI output

AI makes mistakes. Always fact-check.

Especially for:
- Customer emails (wrong info = bad)
- Numbers and stats (AI hallucinates)
- Legal/medical/financial advice

Fix: Review before sending.

**Tweet 8:**
Mistake #7: Giving up too soon

Most people try AI for 3 days, get mediocre results, and quit.

Mastery takes 30-90 days.

Stick with it. Refine. Learn. You'll get there.

Fix: Commit to 30 days minimum.
