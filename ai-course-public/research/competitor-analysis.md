# Competitor Analysis Framework

Complete guide to analyzing competitors in the AI teaching space.

## Why Analyze Competitors

✅ Learn what works (don't reinvent the wheel)
✅ Identify gaps in the market (your opportunity)
✅ Understand pricing and positioning
✅ Find differentiation angles
✅ Avoid their mistakes

**Remember:** Competition is good. It validates the market. Your job is to be different, not better.

## Differentiation Strategies

### 1. Audience-Specific
Don't be "AI for everyone" → Be "AI for [specific audience]"
- AI for Vietnamese shop owners
- AI for F&B businesses in Singapore
- AI for solo consultants in Asia

### 2. Tool-Specific
Don't cover everything → Master one stack
- Just ChatGPT + Canva + Notion
- Just Make.com automation
- Just voice AI agents

### 3. Outcome-Specific
Don't teach features → Teach outcomes
- "Save 10 hours/week" (time)
- "Replace your VA" (money)
- "Get 100 customers" (revenue)

### 4. Format-Specific
Don't do standard video → Try different format
- Cohort-based (live)
- Challenge-based (5 days)
- Workshop-style (1 hour lessons)
- Story-based (case studies)

### 5. Price-Specific
Don't compete on features → Compete on price
- Premium tier ($500+)
- Mid-tier ($97-297)
- Budget tier ($27-47)
- Free tier (lead magnet)

### 6. Language-Specific
Don't teach in English only → Teach in local language
- Vietnamese
- Thai
- Chinese
- Bahasa

### 7. Geography-Specific
Don't be global → Be local
- Singapore only
- Southeast Asia focus
- Asian market specialist

## Competitive Positioning Map

Create a 2x2 matrix:

```
         HIGH PRICE
            |
   Premium  |  Luxury
   Quality  |  Exclusive
------------|------------
   Budget   |  Standard
   Basic    |  Average
            |
         LOW PRICE

         LOW QUALITY → HIGH QUALITY
```

**Plot your competitors and yourself.**

**Goal:** Find empty quadrant.

**Examples:**
- High quality, low price = "Best value" (hard to sustain)
- High quality, high price = "Premium" (establish)
- Low quality, low price = "Budget" (commodity)
- Low quality, high price = "Avoid" (won't last)

**Your position:** Choose deliberately based on:
- Your expertise (can you teach high quality?)
- Your audience (what can they pay?)
- Your scale (how many students?)
- Your goals (revenue, impact, both?)

## Example: My Competitor Analysis

After analyzing 10 competitors, here's what I learned:

**Market standard:**
- Price: $97-297
- Length: 5-8 hours
- Format: Video + PDF
- Guarantee: 30 days
- Community: 50% have one

**Gaps I found:**
1. **No Asian focus** - All use Western examples
2. **No free tools emphasis** - All push paid software
3. **No local language** - All English only
4. **No implementation support** - All self-paced only
5. **Outdated quickly** - Most updated 6+ months ago
6. **Generic** - All try to serve everyone

**My positioning:**
"AI for Small Business Owners in Asia - taught with Asian examples, using only free tools, with implementation support, updated monthly, at an affordable price."

**That's my differentiator. That's my niche. That's my course.**

## Final Competitive Analysis Checklist

Before launching, you should know:

✅ Who are your top 5 competitors?
✅ What do they charge?
✅ What do they teach?
✅ What do they do well?
✅ Where do they fall short?
✅ How will you be different?
✅ Why would students choose you over them?
✅ Can you sustain that difference?

If you can answer all 8, you have a competitive advantage.
