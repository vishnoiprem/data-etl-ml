## 60–90 Second Spoken Answer (lead with this)

> A Head of Data's leadership shows up in three places: **how they communicate**, **how they align**, and **how they inspire**.
>
> On **communication**: I write a lot. Decisions live in documents, not in DMs. I tailor the message to the audience — same data, different language, for the CEO / CFO / CTO / enterprise buyer. I say "I don't know" quickly and follow up. I never let a surprise reach the executive before I do.
>
> On **alignment**: I use one intake, one scoring model, and a published ranked backlog. When two executives disagree, I diagnose the gap (definition, source, transformation) and propose a standard — not a verdict. I say "yes, and here's the trade-off" instead of silent over-commitment.
>
> On **inspiration**: a data-driven and AI-enabled culture isn't a slogan — it's a set of habits. The paved road is easy. The certified metrics are visible. The AI lighthouse is real, with a verified outcome. The team has ownership, not a ticket queue. New hires are an upward bar, not a hire to fill a seat.
>
> Passion isn't loudness. It's **showing up** for the team, the partners, the customer, and the work — especially when the work is hard.

⏱️ ~100 seconds. Trim if rushed.

## CLEAR Communication Framework

| Letter | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **C**oncise | One idea per slide, one ask per email | "Approve $200k for Q1, ROI $1.2M by EOY" |
| **L**istener-first | Lead with their goal, not yours | "Here's the impact on your Q2 plan" |
| **E**vidence | Numbers, citations, lineage | "Per the certified metric, with 95% CI…" |
| **A**ction | Specific ask, owner, deadline | "Need approval by Friday, owner: Duke" |
| **R**epeat | Restate in writing after the meeting | One-pager sent same day |

🔵 **Hook: "If they can't repeat your ask, you didn't make one."**

## The Three Layers of Executive Presence

| Layer | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Communicate** | Writes well, speaks concisely, tailors to audience, never hides bad news |
| **Align** | Diagnoses disagreement, proposes standards, makes trade-offs explicit, ships decisions |
| **Inspire** | Sets a bar people want to meet, makes the work meaningful, builds the team they want to join |

**The trap:** being strong in one and weak in the others. The CTO who communicates but can't align. The CFO who aligns but can't inspire. The founder who inspires but can't communicate the boring middle.

🔵 **Hook: "The boring middle is where most leaders fail. Be the one who doesn't."**

## How I Build a Data-Driven Culture

**Three layers:**

| Layer | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Make good data easy** | Paved road, semantic layer, certified metrics, embedded analysts | Self-service templates, office hours |
| **Make bad data visible** | Freshness + quality badges, public glossary, metrics review | "Data Health" section on every dashboard |
| **Make it teachable** | Trainings, lunch-and-learns, code/SQL reviews | Monthly "How we use data" all-hands |

**Rules:**

- Never trust a number that hasn't been challenged.
- "I don't know" is the most data-driven sentence in the language.

🔵 **Hook: "Culture is the result of habits. Build the habits, the culture follows."**

## How I Build an AI-Enabled Organization

**Three layers:**

| Layer | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **AI is product** | Visible AI features, with verified outcomes | Failure triage, test selection, recommendations |
| **AI is platform** | Shared eval harness, retrieval, governance | One foundation, many features |
| **AI is practice** | Every team has an "AI angle" | Internal copilots, text-to-SQL, automation |

**Rules:**

- Eval before ship. Lighthouse before scale.
- Show the team the wins — adoption, time saved, decisions made better.

🔵 **Hook: "AI is a muscle. Use it on real work and it grows. Spectate and it doesn't."**

## The Hard Communications

### Bad news, early

🟢 *"Business impact first, root cause second, prevention third, technical detail in appendix. Same shape every time. People don't shoot the messenger who told them first."*

### "I don't know"

🟢 *"I don't know yet, here's how I'll find out, by when, and what I'll do in the meantime. No bluffing, no padding, no fake confidence."*

### "I was wrong"

🟢 *"Here's what I said, here's what I learned, here's what I'm changing. No hedging, no blame on the data, no quiet correction weeks later."*

### "I made a mistake that affected customers"

🟢 *"I'm telling you before you see it elsewhere. Here's what happened, what we know, what we're doing, who to contact, and when the next update lands. Same shape externally to customers if user-visible."*

🔵 **Hook: "Trust is built in the second bad-news conversation. The first one is luck."**

## Giving Feedback (the format)

🟢 **Specific. Behavioral. About the work, not the person. Forward-looking.**

- *"In yesterday's review, you called out X as Y, but the data shows Z. The gap is in the join. Next time, let's check the join before we call it. Here's how."*
- Not: *"Be more rigorous."* (vague)
- Not: *"You're sloppy."* (about the person)

**Cadence:** real-time for small things, weekly for accumulated, quarterly for career-level.

🔵 **Hook: "Feedback is a gift, but only if the receiver can open it. Make it specific, behavioral, and forward-looking."**

---

## Receiving Feedback

🟢 **Listen. Thank. Reflect. Act.**

- Don't defend in the moment.
- *"Thank you. I'm going to sit with that. Can I come back next week with what I heard and what I'm changing?"*
- Public follow-up: *"Duke told me X, here's what I'm doing about it."*

🔵 **Hook: "How you receive feedback is how your team will receive feedback. Model it."**

**Visible artifacts:**
- Certified metrics in every exec decision
- Dashboards with freshness + quality badges
- A glossary that's actually used

🔵 **Hook: "The artifacts are the scoreboard. The habits are the team. Build the habits."**

## Common Probes — Pre-Rehearsed Answers

### Q1: "What's your management style?"

🟢 *"Servant-leader, high bar, low ego. I set the bar in the rubric and the reviews. I grow people by giving them ownership of real artifacts — domains, decisions, roadmaps — not tasks. I remove blockers: low performers, bad processes, meetings that shouldn't exist. I write a lot; decisions live in documents, not DMs. The litmus test: if I disappeared for two weeks, would the team still make the right calls?"*

### Q2: "How do you communicate bad news?"

🟢 *"Tell the executive first, in business language. Impact, scope, what we know, what we're doing, when the next update lands. Same shape every time. Then technical detail in the appendix. People don't shoot the messenger who told them first."*

### Q3: "How do you build a data-driven culture?"

🟢 *"Make good data easy, bad data visible, and the practice teachable. Easy: paved road, certified metrics, embedded analysts, self-service templates. Visible: freshness + quality badges, public glossary, regular metrics review. Teachable: trainings, lunch-and-learns, code/SQL reviews. Lead by example — my own recommendations always show the data, the assumption, and what would change my mind."*

### Q4: "How do you create passion for AI in a team that doesn't have it?"

🟢 *"Show, don't preach. Build one lighthouse AI feature with a verified outcome. Demo it. Show the time saved, the decision improved, the customer delighted. Then teach the team how to build the next one. The eval harness, the retrieval pipeline, the safety guardrails — they become the next person's starting point. Passion is the byproduct of capability, not the prerequisite."*

### Q5: "Tell me about a time you had to align a difficult stakeholder."

🟢 *[STAR-R, ~90s. Anchor on: specific stakeholder, specific disagreement, the diagnosis (definition vs source vs transformation), the proposal, the resolution, the long-term fix, the measurable outcome.]*

### Q6: "How do you give feedback?"

🟢 *"Specific, behavioral, about the work, not the person, forward-looking. 'In yesterday's review you said X, but the data shows Y. The gap is in the join. Next time let's check the join first.' Real-time for small things, weekly for accumulated, quarterly for career-level. I never save up feedback for a performance review."*

### Q7: "How do you receive feedback?"

🟢 *"Listen. Thank. Reflect. Act. Don't defend in the moment. 'Thank you, I'm going to sit with that, can I come back next week with what I heard and what I'm changing?' Then follow up publicly so the team sees that feedback changes things. How I receive feedback is how the team will receive feedback."*

### Q8: "What does a healthy data team look like to you?"

🟢 *"Three signals. (1) Outcomes: % Tier-1 SLOs met, AI verified lift, MTTR, adoption, cost per tenant — all trending the right way. (2) Health: low regrettable attrition, high internal mobility, promotions earned, hiring bar maintained. (3) Reputation: other teams ask for the data team by name, execs trust the numbers, the team is invited into decisions early."*

### Q9: "How do you build a team people want to join?"

🟢 *"By being the team I'd want to join. Clear charter, real ownership, low-ego leadership, fair pay, growth, no surprises. The reputation compounds — every great hire makes the next one easier. The reverse is also true. Hire deliberately, even when it's slow. The team's reputation in the market is the recruiting engine."*

### Q10: "How do you balance confidence with humility?"

🟢 *"Confidence in the work, humility in the self. The work is rigorous, the answer is the best we have today, the data is the data. The self is a work in progress — 'I don't know' is safe, 'I was wrong' is expected, 'I changed my mind' is respected. Confidence without humility is arrogance. Humility without confidence is paralysis. The job is both."*

### Q11: "How do you communicate to different audiences?"

🟢 *"Same data, different language. CEO = outcome, dollars, quarter, risk. CFO = cost per insight, ROI, baseline, sensitivity. CTO = architecture, reliability, velocity. CMO = funnel, conversion, attribution. Lawyer = risk, evidence, controls. Enterprise buyer = security, compliance, audit. The data is the same. The translation is the work."*

### Q12: "How do you handle a stakeholder who wants you to ship a number that isn't ready?"

🟢 *"Don't. I say what I can ship today, what's not ready, and why. I offer a v0 (rules-based, clearly labelled) and a plan to the v1 (model, with eval). The 'ship a number that isn't ready' request always costs more later than it gains now. If the stakeholder insists, the failure is mine — I haven't built the trust or the alternative."*

### Q13: "How do you onboard a new executive to data?"

🟢 *"Three meetings. (1) The state of data — what works, what doesn't, what's at risk, what we're investing in. (2) The data they personally need — their top 5 decisions, the metrics, the cadence. (3) How to work with me — what to escalate, what not to, how I communicate, how I want to receive feedback. No surprises after that."*

### Q14: "How do you create urgency without panic?"

🟢 *"Name the gap, the cost, the deadline, the owner. 'Without this, $X is at risk by Q3. We can close it by Q1 with $Y investment. The owner is Z. The decision is yours.' Urgency is the gap between the cost and the deadline. Panic is the same gap without a plan. The plan is the difference."*

### Q15: "What do you do when you don't have the answer?"

🟢 *"Say so. Then say how I'll find out, by when, and what I'll do in the meantime. 'I don't know' is the most data-driven sentence in the language. Bluffing costs more than one wrong number — it costs the next ten."*

## Anti-Patterns (red-flag answers)

- ❌ "I'm a player-coach" without saying what you coach or what you play
- ❌ "I have an open-door policy" — pair it with structured 1:1s
- ❌ "I delegate everything" — that's abdication, not leadership
- ❌ "I don't do politics" — leadership is influence; politics is part of the job
- ❌ "My team is a family" — teams are teams; families don't fire each other for performance
- ❌ "I say yes to everything" — that's not partnering, that's people-pleasing
- ❌ "I'm passionate" without a verifiable outcome to back it
- ❌ "I'd never let someone go" — that's protecting yourself, not the team
- ❌ Inventing your team size, comp, or your past outcomes
