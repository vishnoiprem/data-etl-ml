## PART A — Work Style Assessment: how to answer

You'll choose between two statements or rate agreement. Default toward the **LP-aligned** option, while staying honest and consistent (the test repeats themes to catch contradictions).

| If torn between… | Lean toward (LP) |
|------------------|------------------|
| "wait for full info" vs "act with 80% and adjust" | **act** (Bias for Action) |
| "follow the plan" vs "dig into the data myself" | **dig in** (Dive Deep) |
| "keep the peace" vs "raise a concern respectfully" | **raise it** (Earn Trust / Backbone) |
| "good enough" vs "highest possible quality" | **highest** (Insist on Highest Standards) |
| "my team's goal" vs "the company's goal" | **company** (Ownership) |
| "stick to what I know" vs "learn something new" | **learn** (Learn and Be Curious) |
| "competitor focus" vs "customer focus" | **customer** (Customer Obsession) |

**Rule:** be the leader who is customer-first, takes ownership, uses data, acts fast, holds a high bar, and tells the truth. Don't game it into contradictions — pick the *honest* answer closest to the LP.

## PART B — Work Sample Simulations: how to decide

When you get a scenario (email, chart, competing tasks), silently ask:
1. **What's best for the customer?** (almost always the deciding factor)
2. **What would an owner do?** (not "escalate and wait" — take responsibility)
3. **What does the data say?** (dive into the numbers before deciding)
4. **Can I act now?** (bias for action — but calculated, not reckless)
5. **Am I being honest?** (earn trust — flag bad news, don't hide it)

**Prioritisation tasks:** customer-impacting + urgent first → high-impact → everything else. A blocked customer or a production issue beats an internal nice-to-have.

**Data interpretation tasks:** read the axes carefully, find the trend/outlier, pick the answer the data *actually* supports — don't over-infer. (Your daily job — you'll be strong here.)

**"Respond to a customer/colleague" tasks:** pick the option that's honest, helpful, owns the problem, and follows up — over options that deflect, over-promise, or go silent.
