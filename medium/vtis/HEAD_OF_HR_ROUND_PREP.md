# VTI Group — Head of Data & Analytics
## Head of HR Round: Prep Sheet (Prem Vishnoi)

> This is **not** the recruiter screen. A Head of HR round screens for four things:
> **(1) leadership maturity — can you manage people, not just pipelines**
> **(2) fit with a Vietnamese family-founded conglomerate**
> **(3) real commitment — relocation, notice period, will you actually sign**
> **(4) compensation reality — can they afford you, and are you anchored sanely**
>
> Technical depth is *not* being tested. Do not lead with Spark, Flink, or Unity Catalog.

---

## 1. Your positioning in one line

> "I build the data *function*, not just the platform — I've done it twice at $8B+ scale in Southeast Asia, at Lazada and at CP Group, and I've scaled the team from 8 to 25 while cutting cost 40%."

That single sentence carries: seniority, scale, region, people leadership, and commercial discipline. Everything else hangs off it.

### 90-second opening (personalised to your actual record)

"I've spent 15 years in data, the last 8 of it in Southeast Asian retail and e-commerce at scale — VP Data Engineering at Lazada under Alibaba, and currently Head of Data at Makro, CP Group, where I own the platform behind an $8 billion retail and e-commerce business across three countries.

What I've learned is that the hard part isn't the architecture. It's building a function the business actually pulls from. At Makro I grew the team from 8 to 25, took month-end financial close from five days to two hours across three countries, and cut cloud spend 40% at the same time.

What draws me to VTI is that it's a portfolio problem, not a single-brand problem. Highlands Coffee at 900-plus outlets is one data business; the retail brands are another; food courts are a third. Building one group capability that serves all of them — that's harder and more interesting than what I do today, and it's the natural next step from division head to group head."

**Why this works:** it answers "why you", "why us", and "why now" in one breath, and it names the *step up* explicitly so HR doesn't have to wonder if the role is a lateral move for you.

## 2. The three questions you must not fumble

These are the ones HR is paid to probe. Rehearse them out loud.

### 🔴 A. "You were at Xendit for only six months."

Do not explain it away nervously. Own the shape of it.

> "Fair question, and I'll be direct. Xendit was a Singapore fintech role I joined as Principal Data Engineer in January 2024. I delivered what I was brought in for — a FinOps programme that cut AWS and Databricks spend 40%, and refactored the payments pipelines to cut runtime 60%. In parallel, CP Group approached me with a Head of Data mandate: own the platform for an $8B business, build the team, own the budget. That was the leadership scope I'd been working toward for years, and it wasn't going to come around again on my timetable. I took it. It's the only short tenure in fifteen years — before that I was eight years at Lazada, two-plus at Standard Chartered."

Then **stop talking.** Do not over-justify. The eight years at Lazada is your defence and it's a strong one.

### 🔴 B. "Why leave CP Group after two years?"

The trap is sounding like you're running from something. You must sound like you're running *toward* something.

> "Nothing's wrong at Makro — the platform is stable, the team's built, 99.99% uptime, and I'd hand it over in good shape. But my mandate there is a division mandate. I run data for one business. What I want next is group scope: multiple brands, multiple business models, setting the strategy rather than executing someone else's. VTI is one of the few places in the region where that role genuinely exists at the size I want."

**Never say:** reorg, politics, my boss, no budget, no support, I'm bored.

### 🔴 C. "This role is in Vietnam. Are you able to relocate?"

This is the single highest-stakes question in the round. HR will not advance a candidate who sounds tentative. Decide your answer *before* you walk in.

> "Yes. I've built my whole career in Southeast Asia — Singapore, Thailand, regional across Lazada's six markets — so relocating in the region is normal for me, not a leap. I'd want to work through timing and the practical side with you, but on the question of whether I'm willing to be based in Vietnam: yes, and I'd expect to be there full time. This isn't a role you do remotely."

Have ready, in your head:
- **Notice period** at Makro — say the number.
- **Earliest start date** — say a date, not "depends".
- **Family situation** — one sentence, volunteered on your terms. HR isn't allowed to interrogate it; pre-empting it removes the doubt.
- **Language** — "I don't speak Vietnamese. I've led multi-country teams through English as the working language at Lazada and CP, and I'd start learning. For a group-level role I'd expect English to be workable, but tell me how much that's a real constraint on the ground."

Asking that last question back is a strength move — it shows you're thinking about execution, not just the offer.

## 3. Compensation — how to handle it

Vietnam specifics that actually matter:

| Factor | What to know |
|---|---|
| **Personal income tax** | Vietnam PIT is progressive to **35%**. If you're comparing against Singapore (~15–22% effective), a same-gross offer is a large net pay cut. Compare **net**, not gross. |
| **Currency** | Ask whether the package is VND or USD-denominated. USD-linked protects you from VND depreciation. |
| **Package components** | Housing allowance, international school fees, annual home flights, relocation lump sum, tax equalisation or gross-up, work permit sponsorship. At Head level these are negotiable and often worth 30–40% on top of base. |
| **Bonus** | Ask target %, and whether it's paid on group or brand performance. |

**Your script:**

> "Rather than anchor on a number cold, can you share the band budgeted for this role, and whether it's structured local or as an expat package with housing and relocation? Vietnam's tax profile is different from where I'm sitting today, so I'd be looking at total net rather than headline base — and I'd rather we're both efficient about whether we're in the same range."

If they push you first, give a **range on total package**, say it once, and don't soften it. Do your homework on your own current total comp number *before* the call so you're not calculating live.

> ⚠️ Don't quote a monthly VND figure from memory or from a generic benchmark — Head-of-function bands at Vietnamese conglomerates vary hugely by whether the hire is local or expat. Get their band first. That's the whole play.

## 4. Leadership & people questions — your STAR bank

HR rounds are won on stories. Here are yours, pre-built from your actual record. Learn the *numbers*, improvise the words.

### "Tell me how you build a team."
**Makro: 8 → 25 engineers.**
- Situation: inherited an 8-person team owning a platform for an $8B business — badly under-resourced, reactive, firefighting.
- Action: split into platform / product / reliability pods so ownership was unambiguous; hired for curiosity and business fluency over tool knowledge; put engineers *into* the finance and supply chain teams rather than behind a ticket queue.
- Result: 25 engineers, 500+ daily jobs, 99.99% uptime, **incident volume down 60%** — and the incident drop came from structure and SRE discipline, not headcount alone.
- Learning to voice: "The hiring was the easy half. The hard half was making sure 25 people didn't become slower than 8."

### "Tell me about delivering something the business felt."
**Month-end close: 5 days → 2 hours, across 3 countries.** Finance controllers were the customer. The win wasn't the pipeline — it was that finance stopped budgeting a week of their month for it. Use this one when asked about business partnership; it's your most CFO-legible story.

### "How do you handle budget and cost discipline?"
**$2M annual cloud/vendor budget; 40% cost reduction** via instance tuning and spot utilisation — while data volume grew. Frame it as: "I treat cloud spend as a P&L line I own, not an IT cost someone else worries about." Conglomerate HR loves this; family-founded businesses are cost-conscious by culture.

### "Tell me about a conflict or a difficult stakeholder."
Pick one real instance — a business unit that didn't want to give up its own spreadsheets or shadow reporting for the central platform. Structure: their fear was losing control and speed → you didn't mandate, you shipped one thing they needed faster than they could → they migrated voluntarily → then you set the standard. **Do not** tell a story where you won by escalating.

### "Tell me about a failure."
Have one prepared where **you** were the cause, not the environment. Best shape for you: a platform or migration you pushed on technical merit before the business was ready to adopt, so utilisation lagged. Lesson: adoption is part of the deliverable, and you now sequence change management alongside build. This lands well precisely because it's the classic Head-of-Data failure and shows you've moved past it.

### "What's your leadership style?"
> "I hire for curiosity over credentials. I embed engineers with the business instead of running a service desk. I'm direct — people know where they stand with me. And I'm ruthless about the 20% of analytics work that changes decisions; I'll kill projects that don't land rather than let a team quietly spend a year on them."

### "How do you retain people?"
An HR head *will* ask this, and most candidates have no answer. Yours: growth paths and real ownership. At Makro the pod structure meant engineers owned a domain end-to-end rather than a task queue — that's what keeps good data engineers, not salary alone. Mention attrition if your number is good.

## 5. Fit with VTI specifically

Read the room correctly. VTI is a **Vietnamese founder-led conglomerate**, not a tech company:

- **Show respect for hierarchy and relationships.** Decisions run through trust and through the founder/board level. Signal that you build relationships before you build mandates.
- **Frame data as commercial, never as IT.** Speak in outlets, baskets, footfall, loyalty members, stock-outs, margin — not in tables and clusters.
- **Long-term over quick-flip.** They will worry about a regional expat hire treating this as a two-year stop. Your Lazada tenure of eight years is your proof point — use it deliberately: *"I don't move often. When I commit to a business I stay and build."*
- **Multi-brand humility.** F&B, fashion retail, hospitality, food courts — say plainly that these are different data businesses and that you'd expect to earn each brand's trust separately rather than impose one platform from day one.

### Things to verify before you speak, not assert

Say these as *questions*, not as facts — getting corporate structure wrong in front of HR is worse than not mentioning it:
- The current ownership relationship between VTI and Highlands Coffee (there is outside investment in the coffee business — I'd let them describe it rather than state it).
- Which brands sit inside the group today versus which are distribution/franchise arrangements.
- Whether "Group Head" covers all brands or a defined subset.

Asking is the strong move here. It reads as diligence.

## 6. Your questions for the Head of HR

Pick 3. These are HR-appropriate — save architecture and roadmap questions for the hiring manager.

1. "Is this a newly created function, or a replacement? And if new — what triggered the decision to build it now?"
2. "Who does this role report to, and does it sit at the group leadership table?"
3. "What does success look like at 6 and 12 months — in the words the leadership team would use?"
4. "How are decisions made here? I'm trying to understand how much of the job is influence versus mandate."
5. "What's the structure of the package — local or expat, and what's included beyond base?"
6. "What are the remaining steps and the timeline you're working to?"

Always close with #6. It gets you the process map and signals you're serious.

## 7. The last five minutes

Close explicitly. Most candidates don't, and HR notices.

> "To be clear about where I stand — I'm interested, I'm prepared to relocate to Vietnam, and this is the group-level scope I've been working toward. What would you need from me to move to the next stage?"

## Pre-call checklist

- [ ] Notice period — exact number of days/months
- [ ] Earliest realistic start date
- [ ] Your current **total** comp (base + bonus + allowances), net
- [ ] Your target total package range for Vietnam — one range, stated once
- [ ] Three numbers on the tip of your tongue: **8→25**, **$8B**, **5 days → 2 hours**
- [ ] One failure story where you were the cause
- [ ] One conflict story you won without escalating
- [ ] Quiet room, wired connection, camera at eye level, phone off

**The whole round comes down to two signals: are you a leader, and will you actually take the job.** Be decisive, be warm, be specific with numbers, and don't hedge on Vietnam.
