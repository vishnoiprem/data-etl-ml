VTI Group — Group Head of Data & Analytics | HR Round Prep
The Company in 30 seconds (know this cold)

Viet Thai International is Vietnam's leading multi-brand consumer conglomerate — founded 1998. Flagship is Highlands Coffee (900+ outlets). Also runs Meet & Eat Food Courts, 1911 Restaurant, and represents international brands (Nike, Emporio Armani, Hard Rock Cafe, Swarovski, ALDO). Their strategic bet: winning Vietnam's rising middle class through digital-first consumer experiences. Data is central to that — loyalty apps, personalized offers, supply chain optimization, real-time pricing.

Your 90-second opening (memorize a version of this)

"I'm a data and AI leader with [X years] building analytics capabilities at scale — not just building models, but building the function: strategy, team, governance, and the data products that business teams actually use. What draws me to VTI is the portfolio complexity — you have F&B, fashion, hospitality all under one group, and that's actually harder than a single vertical. The opportunity to build a group-level analytics capability that serves all those brands, while Highlands Coffee is at an inflection point on digital — that's the kind of mandate I want."

Questions they'll ask + what to say

"Walk me through your background."
Lead with impact, not CV recitation. Structure: current scope → key achievement → why now. E.g. "I currently head AI and data at [X], where I've built [team size], [key outcome — e.g., saved $Xm, grew X metric]. I'm looking for a group-level role with a broader mandate across brands and geographies."

"Why VTI? Why this role?"

Highlands Coffee's digital transformation is real — 900+ outlets, loyalty app, AI pricing. You want to be the one building the backbone that powers that.
Multi-brand portfolio means you'll solve problems that don't exist in single-brand companies (shared data infrastructure vs. brand-specific, cross-brand customer journey).
Vietnam's market is at an interesting inflection — rising middle class + digital penetration + modern retail = rare growth context.

"What's your leadership style?"
Keep it concrete: "I hire for curiosity, not just skill. I build teams that embed with the business, not sit in a back office. And I'm ruthless about the 20% of analytics work that drives 80% of decisions — I don't let teams disappear into projects that don't land."

"What would you do in the first 90 days?"
This is a differentiator answer. Say something like:

First 30 days: listen mode — understand each brand's current data maturity, what decisions are being made on gut vs. data, where the biggest pain is
Days 30–60: quick wins — pick one brand, one problem, ship something visible
Days 60–90: present a group data strategy — prioritization, team structure, platform needs, 12-month roadmap

"What's your salary expectation?"
Research VND market rates beforehand. At Head level in Vietnam this role typically benchmarks between VND 100–180M/month depending on scope. State a range confidently, not apologetically. Or ask them to share the budgeted band first if you're uncertain.

"What are your weaknesses?"
Pick one that's real but not fatal for this role (e.g., "I move fast and sometimes need to slow down to bring all stakeholders along — I've gotten better at stakeholder management as I've grown into more senior roles").

"Tell me about a failure."
Have one ready. The structure: situation → what went wrong → what you did → what you learned. Don't deflect. HR trusts people who can reflect.

Questions to ask them (pick 2–3)
"What does success look like for this role at 6 months and 12 months?"
"Where does data sit today — is there an existing team or is this a build from scratch?"
"How much of my mandate is cross-group strategy vs. hands-on delivery?"
"What's driving the decision to hire for this role now — is this a new function or a replacement?"
"Who would I be reporting to, and how does data currently factor into group-level decisions?"
Things NOT to say
Don't over-index on technical tools (they want a business leader, not a Spark admin)
Don't trash your current employer
Don't say "I'm just exploring" — commit to genuine interest
Don't ask about salary first; let them bring it up or ask late in the conversation

The single most important thing for an HR round: be decisive and energized. HR is screening for cultural fit, motivation, and communication clarity — not deep technical depth. Show you understand their business, have a point of view on the role, and are excited about this company specifically.
