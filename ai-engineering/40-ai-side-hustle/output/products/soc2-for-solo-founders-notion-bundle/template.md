# SOC2 For Solo Founders — Notion Bundle

**The complete SOC2 compliance workspace for a solo founder, in 15 pre-built Notion databases.** Clone the workspace, customize 6 fields per database, and you have a working compliance program that auditors can navigate. No spreadsheets to build. No templates to design.

---

## What you get

**1. Master Dashboard** — The landing page. Shows every active vendor, every open policy gap, every upcoming audit task, and a green/red status indicator for all 5 controls. One glance = you know where you stand.

**2. Policies Database** — All 5 SOC2 policies pre-written and ready to customize (Information Security, Access Control, Change Management, Incident Response, Vendor Management). Each is a Notion page with: scope, owner, last reviewed date, control statements. Add your company name + sign-off, done.

**3. Vendor Inventory** — A Notion database with one row per third-party processor. Pre-populated with the 15 most common SaaS vendors (AWS, Stripe, GitHub, OpenAI, etc.) and the SOC2/DPA status field. Add your own vendors, fill in the gap, move on.

**4. Offboarding Checklist Database** — One row per terminated employee with date, name, all the systems they had access to, and a checkbox per system. The Friday reminder bot (template included) flags any row with unchecked boxes 24h after the termination date.

**5. Friday Compliance Review** — A templated Notion page with the 10-item weekly checklist. Duplicate every Friday, walk through it, check the boxes. Auditors can pull the last 52 weeks of these in 5 minutes.

**6. Change Log Database** — One row per code release. Fields: release version, date, PR link, test results, approver, rollback plan. Pre-populated with the format auditors expect.

**7. Incident Log** — Database of every incident and near-miss. Date, type, severity, root cause, mitigation, post-mortem link. The "no incidents" log is as valuable as the "many incidents" log — it shows the program is operational.

**8. Access Review Quarterly** — Templated page for the quarterly 90-minute access review meeting. Lists every employee, every system, every permission. Pre-formatted to disable dormant accounts.

**9. Backup Test Log** — Database of monthly backup restore tests. Date, system, success/fail, restore time, who verified. Auditors love this one.

**10. Penetration Test Tracker** — Database of pen tests. Date, vendor, scope, findings count, remediation status. Auto-flags any test older than 12 months.

**11. Evidence Collection Calendar** — Shows which evidence your compliance platform (Vanta/Drata) needs each week, who owns collecting it, and when it's due. Pre-mapped to the SOC2 controls.

**12. Audit Day Playbook** — A one-page Notion doc with everything you need on audit day: which evidence to pull first, how to navigate the auditor's request list, what to say when you don't know an answer, and the post-audit timeline.

**13. Auditor Question Bank** — 50 of the most common SOC2 audit questions with the answer an auditor expects to hear. Read this the week before your audit so you're not thinking on your feet.

**14. Security Awareness Training Tracker** — Database of every employee, training modules completed, completion date. SOC2 requires annual security training; this is your proof.

**15. Roadmap to Type 2** — A quarterly view of the steps to upgrade from Type 1 to Type 2, with owners and dependencies. Once you ship Type 1, this is your Q1-Q4 plan.

## How to use it

**Day 1 (1 hour):** Duplicate the template workspace into your Notion. You'll have 15 empty databases. The structure is pre-built — you just need to add data.

**Week 1 (4-6 hours):** Fill in the 5 policies. They're pre-written; you add your company name, scope, and owner. Fill in the vendor inventory (start with the 15 pre-populated vendors).

**Week 2-3:** Connect the Friday Compliance Review template to your weekly calendar block. Run your first review. Connect the change log to your GitHub releases via Zapier (template included).

**Week 4:** Run your first internal audit using the Audit Day Playbook. You'll find gaps. Fix them. By the end of week 4 you have a working compliance program.

**Day 90:** Your external audit. The auditor opens your Notion workspace, navigates the 15 databases, and finds 80% of what they need without a single follow-up email. You pass.

---

## What's NOT included

- **The audit itself.** This is the workspace, not the auditor. You'll still need Vanta/Drata or a similar platform, plus an external auditor.
- **The legal advice.** The policies are pre-written, but they're templates, not legal review. If your customers have specific contractual requirements, have a lawyer check.
- **The integrations.** The change log and evidence collection work best when connected to GitHub/AWS/HR via Zapier. The Zapier template is included; the time to set it up is not.

## Why a Notion bundle, not a PDF

A PDF of "here are 5 SOC2 policies" is what every consultant sells. You print it, file it, never look at it again. A Notion workspace is something you actually use — every Friday for the review, every release for the change log, every onboarding for the security training. The audit becomes a 30-minute export instead of a 3-week panic.

The compliance programs that survive 12 months are the ones that live where the work lives. Notion is where you already do everything else. Put compliance there too.
