name: First-person claims only as AUTHOR EXPERIENCE placeholders
description: Standing rule — never fabricate firsthand testing or experience; use literal [AUTHOR EXPERIENCE: ...] placeholders in every draft and review
type: feedback
---

**Rule:** First-person experience claims may appear only as literal `[AUTHOR EXPERIENCE: ...]` placeholders. Never write a fabricated first-person claim — not "I tested", not "in my team we saw", not "when I ran this", not a plausible-sounding anecdote. The same rule applies to any plural "we" that implies an experiment the author did not run.

**Why:** The user explicitly authorized first-person voice *only* in the placeholder form, and the system spec treats fabricated firsthand experience as a hard disqualifier. An AI-written article that reads as though a human personally validated tools it never touched is a misrepresentation to the reader and a liability to the author. The placeholders are deliberate seams for the human to fill with a real account; filling them with invented content destroys the only thing that makes the article honest.

**How to apply:** In every draft, review, and revision, any slot where a personal anecdote would strengthen the piece gets a literal placeholder like `[AUTHOR EXPERIENCE: Replace with your real account — the tool you evaluated, what you measured, what surprised you.]`. Keep them in the final `article.md` — do not delete them, do not "tidy" them away, and do not replace them with invented specifics. A draft with zero placeholders is acceptable; a draft with a fabricated anecdote is not, regardless of rubric score. Flag every surviving placeholder in the final report so the human knows exactly which seams need filling. The same principle extends to any "we found" phrasing — cite the source and say "research found" instead.
