# Lesson 13: Business models and industry economics

> **Week 3 · Day 13 · 90 min**
> **Deliverable:** The unit economics of 3 industries, and the data/AI play in each.

## 🎯 Architect Level
Senior.

## 🧠 Concept

Every industry has a different unit economics. The data and AI investment that pays back in retail is not the one that pays back in banking. If you interview at three companies in three industries and give the same answer, you've lost.

The four dominant business model shapes:

1. **Retail / e-commerce.** Thin margins (2-10% net), high volume, inventory risk. Data wins: demand forecasting, dynamic pricing, personalization, supply chain.
2. **Financial services.** Fat margins (20-40%), regulated, risk-heavy. Data wins: credit decisioning, fraud detection, AML, customer 360, retention.
3. **SaaS / subscription.** Negative or thin margins early, expansion-driven. Data wins: NRR, churn prediction, product analytics, GenAI for productivity.
4. **Marketplace / platform.** Take-rate model, two-sided. Data wins: trust & safety, search/recommendation, pricing, GenAI for listings.

For each, you must know:
- The unit economics (CAC, LTV, gross margin, cash conversion)
- The dominant cost drivers
- The dominant revenue levers
- The data and AI investments that move each

The interview question is not "what would you do with our data?" It is "**how would you move our unit economics?**" If you don't know the unit economics, you can't answer.

## 🛠️ Build It

**Spec:** Build a 1-page model of the unit economics for 3 industries: retail, banking, SaaS.

**Acceptance:**
- 3 industries × unit economics table
- 3-5 data/AI plays per industry
- Each play tied to a unit economic lever
- Quantified $ range or [CONFIRM]

**Starter:** Use your existing resume domains. Retail = Makro. Banking = SCB / DBS / PayPal. SaaS = Xendit / Katalon.

**Solution (3 industries, abridged):**

**Retail (Makro / grocery):**
- GM: 20-30%; net margin: 2-6%
- Levers: same-store sales, private label mix, inventory turn
- Plays: demand forecasting (-20% stockouts), dynamic pricing (+1-3% margin), markdown optimization (-15% markdowns)

**Banking (SCB / DBS):**
- Net interest margin: 1.5-3.5%; cost-to-income: 40-55%
- Levers: fee income, deposit growth, credit losses, ops cost
- Plays: credit decisioning (-10-20% losses), fraud/AML (-30-50% false positives), GenAI ops (-20-40% handling time), customer 360 (+5-10% wallet share)

**SaaS (Xendit / Katalon):**
- GM: 60-80%; CAC payback: 12-24 months; NRR: 100-130%
- Levers: new logos, expansion, churn, support cost
- Plays: churn prediction (-2-5% churn), product-led growth signals, GenAI support (-30% ticket cost), sales intelligence

The shape is the same; the specific plays differ. The CDO who knows the unit economics and can map plays to levers is the CDO who gets the offer.

## 🏛️ Architect Notes

- **Trade-off:** Industry depth vs. cross-industry breadth. For the interview, breadth beats depth. Know 3 industries well; know 1 in depth (the one you're interviewing for).
- **Capacity / cost:** A 1-page industry brief takes 4-6 hours of research. Build 3 before the interview loop.
- **When NOT to use:** Don't quote industry benchmarks without a source. Use [CONFIRM] markers for numbers you don't have.
