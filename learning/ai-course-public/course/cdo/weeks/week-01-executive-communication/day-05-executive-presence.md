# Lesson 5: Executive Presence

> **Week 1 · Day 5 · 90 min**
> **Deliverable:** A stress-test Q&A: 5 questions, 60 seconds each, recorded.

## 🎯 Architect Level
Staff.

## 🧠 Concept

Executive presence is what survives when the prepared answer runs out. It's the way you hold the room when:

- The interviewer asks a question you didn't prepare for
- The interviewer pushes back on something you said
- You genuinely don't know the answer
- The room is hostile (e.g., a CTO who thinks CDOs are overhead)
- You've been interrupted three times in two minutes

The five moves of executive presence:

1. **Pause** — when you don't know, pause for one beat. Don't fill the silence. Filling the silence is what makes you look uncertain.
2. **Reframe** — convert the hostile question to a question you can answer. *"That's a great question. The way I'd approach that is..."*
3. **Defer with conviction** — when you don't know, say so, and say what you would do. *"I don't have a number for that. Here's how I'd get one in the first 30 days."*
4. **Hold the line** — when pushed, don't reverse. Acknowledge the push, restate your position, and ask for the underlying concern. *"I hear you. The reason I'd still recommend X is Y. What's the concern that's driving that?"*
5. **Close cleanly** — when you're done, stop. Don't trail off. Don't apologize. Don't ask "does that make sense?"

The biggest single mistake is to **answer the question you wanted to be asked instead of the one you were asked**. The interviewer wrote the question on purpose. Answer it.

## 🛠️ Build It

**Spec:** Run 5 stress-test questions, 60 seconds each. Record. Listen back. Score against the rubric.

**Acceptance:**
- 5 questions, each 50-65 seconds
- 0 filler words
- 1 question where you don't know the answer — handle it with the defer move
- 1 question where the interviewer pushes back — handle it with the hold-the-line move
- 1 question where you reframe a hostile ask

**Starter:** Pick 5 from the question bank. The stress-test set:
- Q45: "How would you handle a CEO who demands unrealistic timelines?"
- Q46: "What if your AI strategy produces no ROI after six months?"
- Q47: "How would you cut your department budget by 30%?"
- Q48: "What would you do if the CTO rejected your architecture?"
- Q49: "How would you balance innovation, security, and profitability?"

**Solution (Q47, 60s):**
> *"Cut the lowest-ROI programs first. Renegotiate vendor contracts second. Look at org shape third. Never cut reliability.*
>
> *In practice, the order matters because each lever is harder than the last and the political cost compounds. Cutting a low-ROI program is a one-time decision with a clear paper trail. Renegotiating a vendor contract is a relationship move that takes 60-90 days. Restructuring the org is a people decision that takes a quarter and burns trust.*
>
> *The one thing I would not cut is the reliability practice — on-call rotation, observability, incident review. Those are the systems that prevent the next quarter from being more expensive than this one."*

## 🏛️ Architect Notes

- **Trade-off:** Confidence vs. humility. The right answer is: confidence in the structure, humility about the specifics. *"Here's how I'd think about it. I don't have the exact number."*
- **Capacity / cost:** 60 seconds per question. Five questions. 5 minutes total. Add 5 minutes for setup. 10 minutes of recording per drill.
- **When NOT to use:** Don't use these moves for routine questions where your prepared answer fits. Save the moves for when the question surprises you.
- **Production checklist:**
  - [ ] One question where you pause and don't fill the silence
  - [ ] One question where you reframe
  - [ ] One question where you defer with conviction
  - [ ] One question where you hold the line
  - [ ] One question where you close cleanly
  - [ ] Total time under 5:30

- When you didn't know the answer, what did you say? Could you make it crisper?
- Write 3 sentences you'll say out loud tomorrow. (Hint: the reframe.)
