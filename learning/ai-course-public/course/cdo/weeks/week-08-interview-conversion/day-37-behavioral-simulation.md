# Lesson 37: Behavioral simulation

> **Week 8 · Day 37 · 90 min**
> **Deliverable:** The 20 STAR stories, all rehearsed out loud.

## 🎯 Architect Level
Staff.

## 🧠 Concept

Behavioral questions are the most-predictable part of the executive loop. The questions change; the underlying competencies don't. The candidate who has 20 STAR stories rehearsed is the candidate who lands the offer.

The 20 STAR stories cover the 6 competencies in the rubric. Use Day 35's question list as the menu:

| Competency | Stories needed | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic & business | 4 | Unified data platform, AI portfolio, 3-year strategy, business case |
| Technical leadership | 3 | Architecture decision, build-vs-buy, reliability incident |
| Leadership & org | 4 | Hire, fire, develop, transform |
| Governance & risk | 3 | Data quality incident, security breach, AI Act posture |
| Commercial & financial | 3 | Cost optimization, ROI delivery, vendor negotiation |
| Communication & presence | 3 | Board update, 3-C mediation, public talk |

The single biggest mistake is to **have fewer than 20 stories**. The interviewer will find the gap. Build 20.

The second biggest mistake is to **have stories that all sound the same**. 20 stories about "I shipped a big project" is 1 story. The 20 stories should span the 6 competencies, the 4 levels of seniority, and the 3 outcome types (cost-out, revenue-up, risk-down).

The third biggest mistake is to **rehearse only in your head**. The interview is out loud. Rehearse out loud. Record yourself. Listen. Rewrite.

The STAR frame for each story:

- **S (Situation).** 15 sec. The context, the company, the constraint.
- **T (Task).** 15 sec. The specific thing you had to do.
- **A (Action).** 60 sec. The 3-5 things you did. (This is the meat.)
- **R (Result).** 30 sec. The outcome, the number, the lesson.

Total: 2 minutes per story. Build all 20.

## 🛠️ Build It

**Spec:** Build the 20 STAR stories, each in 2 minutes, with a 30-second follow-up defense.

**Acceptance:**
- 20 stories, 6 competencies covered
- Each: 2 minutes, STAR, 1 number, 1 lesson
- Each: 30-second follow-up
- 1 paragraph on the trade-off (depth vs. variety)

**Starter:** Map your resume to the 6 competencies. For each, pick 3-4 real stories. Trim to 20.

**Solution (sample 3, abridged):**

**Story: Unified Data Platform transformation (Makro)**
- S: 5 BUs, 3 warehouses, 6 BI tools, 4 "active customer" definitions, $2B revenue
- T: design and deliver a 3-year transformation to a single platform
- A: stakeholder map (CEO + 5 BUs + CFO); hub-and-spoke org; contracts on top 5 sources; semantic layer MVP; 1 AI in production Y1; cost discipline
- R: 60% incident drop; $XM run-rate ROI; 1 AI in production by month 12 [CONFIRM: get real numbers]
- Follow-up: "What was the biggest pushback?" — "The 3-warehouse consolidation. The BU heads saw it as a loss of control. I co-owned delivery with each BU; the consolidation saved $X/year; the BU heads got faster delivery in return."

**Story: AI portfolio defense (Lazada / Makro)**
- S: AI mandate from the board, 0 in production, fragmented data
- T: build a 5-7 use case portfolio, defend the cuts
- A: 4-dimension scoring (value, readiness, complexity, risk); 1-2 quick wins, 2-3 strategic, 1 moonshot; explicit cuts with reasons
- R: 1 quick win in production in 6 months; 3 use cases in production in 12 months [CONFIRM]
- Follow-up: "Why did you cut the support AI?" — "Risk > return at our data maturity. We deferred to Y2. We did the doc intelligence instead — same team, lower risk, faster path."

**Story: Cost optimization (Xendit / Makro)**
- S: $XM/year in cloud + SaaS spend; CFO wants 20% cut
- T: deliver 20% cut without losing capability
- A: FinOps framework (visibility, optimization, governance); 3 moves (reserved instances, rightsizing, vendor renegotiation); committed spend discount; showback per team
- R: -40% on storage + compute; -20% on SaaS; total -25% on data bill [CONFIRM]
- Follow-up: "What did the team push back on?" — "Showback. The team saw it as surveillance. I reframed: 'this is your budget, this is your optimization lever, this is your win.' Adoption followed."

**Trade-off (depth vs. variety):** The 20 stories should be 70% variety, 30% depth. A few deep stories (the ones that define you) and many shorter ones (the ones that round you out).

## 🏛️ Architect Notes

- **Trade-off:** Depth vs. variety. Default: 4-5 deep stories, 15-16 shorter stories.
- **Capacity / cost:** 20 stories × 2 minutes = 40 minutes of speaking. Build over 4-5 sessions, not in one sitting.
- **When NOT to use:** Don't use the same story twice in the same loop. The interviewer will notice.
- **Production checklist:**
  - [ ] 20 stories
  - [ ] 6 competencies covered
  - [ ] 2 minutes per story
  - [ ] STAR frame
  - [ ] 1 number, 1 lesson
  - [ ] 30-second follow-up
  - [ ] Trade-off paragraph
