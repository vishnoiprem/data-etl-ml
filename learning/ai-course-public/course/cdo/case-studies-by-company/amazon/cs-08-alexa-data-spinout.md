# CS-08 (Amazon) — Should the Devices (Alexa) data business be carved out as a separate entity? Defend the recommendation to Andy

> **Mapping:** Original CS-08. Retargeted to Amazon's Devices (Alexa) data business.
> **Goal:** 30 minutes. Defend the recommendation on whether to carve out Alexa's data business.

## The brief

Andy walks into your office on a Friday afternoon. "I have a problem. `[CONFIRM: a financial buyer / a strategic partner]` has approached us about carving out the Devices (Alexa) data business — the voice interaction data, the smart-home data, the Echo / Ring data — as a separate entity. The offer is $XM, which is `[CONFIRM: N%]` of Devices' current valuation. The board is intrigued. The Devices lead is cautious (after the 2023 downsizing). I need your recommendation by Monday."

**The CEO asks you three questions:**
1. What's Alexa's data actually worth?
2. What do we lose if we carve it out?
3. Would you recommend we sell?

**The challenge:**
- The "data business" is not a clean entity — it's embedded in Devices operations.
- Devices is a long-term bet; the data is a fraction of the capex.
- The buyer is `[CONFIRM: who]`.
- The 100+ Devices ML engineers would have to move.

## Solution sketch (do not copy — internalize and re-speak)

**Reframe (3 min).** "The question is not 'what is Alexa's data worth' — the question is 'what is the right shape of Devices for the next 5 years.' The data is a capability, not a product. Carving it out separates the capability from the operating business that creates it."

**The value (8 min).**
- Standalone value: $XM offer implies `[N%]` of Devices valuation. But the data is embedded — to standalone, we'd need to:
  - Separate the data stack (voice interaction, smart-home, Echo / Ring)
  - Separate the people (100+ ML engineers)
  - Separate the contracts (Ring partnerships, smart-home integrations)
  - Standalone cost: 18-24 months, $Y in separation cost, $Z in run-rate overhead
  - Net standalone value: $XM - $Y - $Z = $A-$B (probably less than $XM)
- Plus: standalone = loss of synergy with Devices operations
- Comparable transactions: `[CONFIRM: 2-3]`

**The cost (8 min).**
- Strategic cost: Devices loses the data advantage; competitive moat erodes; growth slows
- Operational cost: Devices would have to license back the data
- People cost: 100+ ML engineers move
- Customer cost: customer trust in the data partnership is at risk
- Reputational cost: `[CONFIRM: who]` owns our Alexa data

**The recommendation (6 min).**
- **Recommend: hold, with conditions.**
- Condition 1: a counter-offer from `[CONFIRM: a financial buyer]`
- Condition 2: a license-back model
- Condition 3: a 12-month option
- Why: the strategic value inside is 2-3× the standalone value; the buyer knows this

**The risks (5 min).**
- Risk 1: the buyer walks; we lost 30 days
- Risk 2: the board wants the $XM
- Risk 3: the buyer raises the offer
- Risk 4: Devices growth regresses without the data
- Off-ramp: if no license-back, the answer is no

**Q&A — be ready for:**
- "What if the board overrides?" — Present the math; document the dissent.
- "What if Devices' value proves out?" — $XM of NPV; the math has to show that.
- "What about the people?" — 100+ move; severance + outplacement.
- "What if `[CONFIRM: a competitor like Apple / Google / Microsoft]` offers more?" — Re-evaluate; the answer is the highest-NPV path.

## Watch for when you re-listen

- **Did you reframe in the first 3 minutes?**
- **Did you do the math?** Andy buys math, not narrative.
- **Did you name the recommendation with conditions?**
- **Did you end on time?**
