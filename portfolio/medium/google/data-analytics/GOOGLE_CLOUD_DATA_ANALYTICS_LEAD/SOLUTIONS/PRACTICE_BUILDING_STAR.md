## 🎯 STAR Framework

### **SITUATION**
> **Context:** Joined a regional Google Cloud consulting practice (SEA) as Data & Analytics Practice Lead. The practice was at **$2.1M ARR**, **58% utilization**, **65% margin**, with **3 consultants** and **no partner ecosystem**. Client engagements were ad-hoc, no reusable assets, and every project started from scratch.
> 
> **Market Opportunity:** Singapore FSI & Telco sectors accelerating cloud migration (MAS-TRM 2025 deadline), but no dedicated Google Cloud data migration factory in region. Competitors (AWS, Azure) had established migration programs with SI partners.

### **TASK**
> **Mandate from Director:** "Build a scalable Data & Analytics consulting practice that hits **$10M ARR at >30% margin within 18 months**, with a partner ecosystem that delivers 60% of revenue."
> 
> **My Objectives:**
> 1. **Revenue:** $2.1M → $10M ARR (4.7x)
> 2. **Margin:** 65% → 30%+ (sustainable consulting margin)
> 3. **Utilization:** 58% → 85% (industry benchmark)
> 4. **Team:** 3 → 15 consultants (hiring + retention)
> 5. **Partners:** 0 → 5 strategic SIs (Accenture, TCS, Deloitte, local)
> 6. **Assets:** 0 → 10 reusable IP assets (migration factory, governance packs, accelerators)

### **ACTION**

#### **1. Practice Strategy & P&L Design (Month 1-2)**
```
P&L MODEL DESIGN:
├── Revenue Streams
│   ├── Architecture & Advisory (High margin, 40%): $4M target
│   ├── Co-delivery with SIs (Medium margin, 45%): $4.5M target  
│   ├── Managed Services / Retainers (Recurring, 10%): $1M target
│   └── Asset Licensing / Training (High margin, 5%): $500K target
├── Cost Structure
│   ├── Consultant Compensation (60% of revenue): $6M
│   ├── Enablement & Certifications (5%): $500K
│   ├── Business Development (8%): $800K
│   ├── Asset Development (7%): $700K
│   └── Overhead / Travel (20%): $2M
└── Target Margin: 30% ($3M EBITDA)
```

**Key Decisions:**
- **Pricing Model:** Shifted from T&M only → **Fixed-fee milestones** (migration waves) + **Outcome-based bonuses** (cost savings, performance gains)
- **Partner Commercial Model:** 
  - **Strategic (Google-led):** Google 30% architecture/quality, SI 70% delivery → 60/40 margin split
  - **Select (Partner-led):** SI delivers, Google quality gates → Google 15% enablement fee
  - **Registered:** Transactional, volume-based

## 🔴 Follow-up Questions & Answers

**Q: "How did you handle the margin pressure when shifting to fixed-fee?"**
> "We piloted fixed-fee on 3 migrations where we had high confidence (reusable assets, known patterns). Tracked actual vs estimate weekly. Built 20% contingency into first 2 fixed-fee deals. After 3 successful deliveries, margin stabilized at 32%. Now 70% of revenue is fixed-fee."

**Q: "What was your biggest partner failure and how did you fix it?"**
> "One Select partner missed 3 milestones on a $800K engagement. Root cause: they assigned junior resources not in SoW. We invoked escalation L2→L3, embedded Google architects in their pods, re-baselined with 2-week sprints. Delivered 2 weeks late vs 8 originally. Updated partner contract: certified resource names required, substitution clause with 5-day notice."

**Q: "How do you decide which assets to build?"**
> "Asset investment committee (me + Practice Manager + 2 Senior Architects) meets monthly. Criteria: (1) Used in ≥2 engagements, (2) Saves ≥40 hours/engagement, (3) Differentiates us in sales, (4) Maintainable by 1 person. We kill assets that don't hit 3+ reuse within 6 months."

**Q: "How do you balance Google IP vs Partner IP?"**
> "Clear demarcation: Google owns architecture patterns, governance frameworks, methodology. Partner owns delivery accelerators, industry templates, migration scripts. Joint IP (co-created) goes to shared repo with mutual license. Prevents conflict, encourages collaboration."

**Q: "What would you do differently?"**
> "Invest in partner enablement earlier — we started month 4, should have been month 1. Also, should have hired a Practice Manager at month 3 not month 8; I spent too much time on operations vs strategy."
