```
APAC Data & Analytics Strategy (2024-2027):

MARKET PRIORITIES:
1. Singapore FinServ (MAS-TRM wave) - Defend & expand
2. Australia Banking (APRA CPS 234) - Attack AWS stronghold
3. Japan Manufacturing/Telco (Data residency + AI) - Build with NTT/Fujitsu
4. India FinServ/Retail/Startups (RBI + DPDP) - Scale with TCS/Infosys
5. Indonesia/Malaysia/Thailand - Plant flags, enable local partners

INVESTMENT PRIORITIES:
├── Reusable IP (40%): Migration Runbook, Terraform, Templates, Policy Packs

├── Talent (20%): 500+ certified consultants, hub in Singapore
└── Customer Success (10%): Dedicated CSMs for top 50 accounts

SUCCESS METRICS 2027:
├── Revenue: $500M+ | Margin: >40% | Partners: 20+ Specializations
├── Consultants: 500+ certified | References: 100+
```

#### Q5: "Top performer wants to be People Manager. They're a brilliant IC. Advice?"
**What They're Looking For:**
- Dual career ladder philosophy
- Management = different skills, not promotion
- Assessment criteria for management potential

**Strong Answer:**
```
DUAL CAREER LADDER PHILOSOPHY:
├── IC Track: Consultant → Senior → Staff → Principal (same comp bands as management)
├── Management Track: Lead → Manager → Director → VP
├── Not a hierarchy - parallel paths with equal prestige

MANAGEMENT POTENTIAL ASSESSMENT:
├── Mentoring track record (formal + informal)
├── Conflict resolution examples
├── Team outcomes (not individual heroics)
├── Willingness to do "unglamorous" work (career conversations, perf reviews, hiring)
├── Psychological safety creation

MY ADVICE: "Try mentoring 2-3 people formally for 6 months. Lead a small initiative. 
If you enjoy the *team's* success more than your own code - management path.
If you miss the technical depth - Staff/Principal IC path. Both valued equally here."
```

#### Q10: "Scope creep: Customer adds 8 requirements mid-migration. Fixed timeline/budget. Partner said yes to all. You?"
**What They're Looking For:**
- Immediate intervention
- MoSCoW with customer
- Partner accountability
- Change control process

**Strong Answer:**
```
IMMEDIATE ACTION:
1. **Stop the bleeding:** Call with Customer PM + Partner PM + Me. "We need to align on scope."
2. **MoSCoW Workshop (2 hours):** All 8 requirements on whiteboard. Customer ranks. We estimate impact.
3. **Partner Accountability:** "Partner, you committed to original SoW. New scope = change order or defer."
4. **Decision:** 3 deferred to Phase 2 (became $1.5M expansion). 2 descoped. 3 absorbed with risk documented.
5. **Process Fix:** Rigorous change control in SoW. Weekly scope review. No verbal agreements.

LESSON: Saying "yes" to everything fails the customer. Structured change control protects everyone.
```

### Category 5: Technical Judgment (Manager Level)

#### Q11: "When would you recommend BigQuery vs Snowflake vs Redshift for a customer?"
**What They're Looking For:**
- Honest, criteria-based, not religious
- Customer context drives decision
- Acknowledge competitor strengths

**Strong Answer:**
```
DECISION CRITERIA (Customer Context Drives):

BIGQUERY WHEN:
├── Serverless + unified AI (BQML→Vertex) priority
├── Multi-cloud query (Omni) needed
├── No cluster ops capacity
├── Variable/unpredictable workloads (pay-per-query)
├── MAS-TRM/PDPA - native compliance tooling

SNOWFLAKE WHEN:
├── Already all-in AWS/Azure, zero GCP appetite
├── Snowpark + Streamlit ecosystem investment
├── Marketplace/data sharing critical
├── Team has deep Snowflake expertise

REDSHIFT WHEN:
├── Deep AWS integration (Redshift Spectrum, Athena)
├── Existing Redshift investment, migration cost high
├── Predictable steady-state workloads (RA3 reserved)

MY DEFAULT: BigQuery for greenfield + AI mandate. But I'll say "Snowflake makes sense if..." honestly.
```
